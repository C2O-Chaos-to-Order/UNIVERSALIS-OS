## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected subgroup examines fungal mycetoma and two distinct rehabilitation disciplines, and evaluates marine-derived surgical materials against the discipline-specific threshold for Marine Biology.

The assessment distinguishes:

- **Mycology ↔ Physical Therapy:** fungal eumycetoma may progressively damage skin, subcutaneous tissue, muscle, tendons, and bone, causing deformity, loss of mobility, and, in advanced cases, amputation. Physical therapy contributes postoperative rehabilitation, restoration of mobility and physical function, stump care, gait and prosthetic preparation, and prevention of secondary disability. The Mycetoma Research Centre care pathway specifically identifies physiotherapy after surgery, especially with muscle or bone involvement; a 2026 histopathologically confirmed eumycetoma case report documents postoperative referral for stump management and prosthetic fitting. The relationship is restricted to the fungal subset of mycetoma. Mycetoma can also be caused by bacteria (actinomycetoma), which is not a Mycology case. The cited rehabilitation guidance is largely written for mycetoma as a whole and does not demonstrate that a particular physiotherapy protocol is uniquely required for eumycetoma or that a measured rehabilitation effect is established by controlled trials.
- **Mycology ↔ Occupational Therapy:** fungal eumycetoma and other chronic fungal subcutaneous infections can produce deformity, swelling, restricted limb function, difficulty walking, and loss of independence in everyday activities. Occupational Therapy contributes activity and participation assessment, adaptation of daily tasks, assistive devices, adaptive strategies, and support for functional independence. The Mycetoma Nursing Guidelines explicitly call for collaboration with occupational therapists and physical therapists, including rehabilitation plans for functional limitations and support for activities of daily living. The recommendation is limited to the fungal subset when Mycology is the relevant life-science discipline. The guideline also addresses bacterial mycetoma and is not a controlled effectiveness study; it supports an explicit care interface, not a claim that OT interventions have been shown in trials to improve a specified outcome in eumycetoma.
- **Marine Biology ↔ Surgery:** marine organisms are sources of biomaterials and bioactive products investigated for wound coverage, hemostasis, tissue repair, and regenerative applications. Examples include acellular fish-skin grafts used in complex orthopedic wounds and chitosan-based hemostatic dressings with a 510(k) substantial-equivalence determination for temporary management of severely bleeding surgical wounds. These demonstrate a marine-bioresource / surgical-material application pathway. However, the reviewed evidence is predominantly framed through biomaterials science, device engineering, wound care, and surgical practice. Product feedstock is not always traceable to a particular marine organism, and the evidence does not establish a sustained contribution from Marine Biology as a distinct discipline beyond resource provenance and organism-derived material characterization. This pair is not promoted.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not prove that no narrower future interface could be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- **Mycology:** Mycology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-012), Dentistry (LSHS-048), Emergency Medicine (LSHS-049), Nursing (LSHS-050), Preventive Medicine (LSHS-051), Pharmacology (LSHS-110), Epidemiology (LSHS-111), Public Health (LSHS-112), Surgery (LSHS-113), Mental Health (LSHS-158), and Nutrition (LSHS-169) concern other exact pairs. The new PT proposal focuses on physical mobility, movement rehabilitation, post-surgical stump management, and prosthetic preparation. The new OT proposal focuses on ADLs, activity adaptation, assistive devices, and functional independence. The two new pairs are distinct from each other and from Mycology ↔ Surgery or Mycology ↔ Nursing; they do not duplicate treatment of fungal disease or nursing-led care coordination.
- **Marine Biology:** Marine Biology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-060), Public Health (LSHS-061), Preventive Medicine (LSHS-062), Nutrition (LSHS-066), Epidemiology (LSHS-119), Pharmacology (LSHS-121), Emergency Medicine (LSHS-162), Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-191), Nursing (LSHS-192), and Mental Health (LSHS-179) address other exact pairs. Marine Biology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-206) previously examined marine-derived resources in dental biomaterials and was not promoted because the evidence primarily established material sourcing and biomaterials engineering rather than a distinct Marine Biology contribution. LSHS-212 applies the same disciplinary threshold to surgical wound and hemostatic materials. It is a different exact pair and a different clinical application, but the same field-contribution limitation remains. Zoology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-186) is a distinct subject pair and does not substitute for Marine Biology ↔ Surgery.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-210 is reciprocal in fungal-disease rehabilitation: Mycology contributes the understanding and identification of eumycetoma and its tissue effects; Physical Therapy contributes assessment and rehabilitation of movement and physical function after disease-related impairment or surgery.
- LSHS-211 is reciprocal in functional rehabilitation: Mycology contributes the fungal disease context and understanding of tissue damage; Occupational Therapy contributes assessment of daily activities, adaptive strategies, assistive-device use, and practical independence.
- LSHS-212 has no promoted direction or subtype. Marine-derived materials have surgical applications, but their use does not establish the distinct disciplinary role required for a Marine Biology ↔ Surgery candidate.

Important boundaries:

- Mycetoma is not synonymous with fungal disease. Eumycetoma is fungal; actinomycetoma is bacterial. Only confirmed fungal eumycetoma supports the Mycology side of LSHS-210 and LSHS-211.
- The Mycetoma Research Centre's physiotherapy and occupational-therapy guidance applies to mycetoma care broadly. Its rehabilitation recommendations are not evidence that a given protocol has been separately validated for fungal eumycetoma.
- Physical Therapy and Occupational Therapy must not be collapsed into a generic rehabilitation label. PT emphasizes movement and physical function; OT emphasizes ADLs, activity adaptation, assistive strategies, and independence. Actual services can overlap in multidisciplinary care.
- Marine-derived devices or graft materials may be engineered and evaluated in biomaterials science and surgery without a corresponding distinct Marine Biology contribution. An FDA 510(k) substantial-equivalence determination is not a statement that a device is FDA-approved through a premarket approval application, nor does it validate the broad discipline relationship.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. This assessment does not make rehabilitation an antifungal treatment, treat all mycetoma as fungal, or equate the use of marine-derived materials with a verified Marine Biology ↔ Surgery disciplinary relationship.
