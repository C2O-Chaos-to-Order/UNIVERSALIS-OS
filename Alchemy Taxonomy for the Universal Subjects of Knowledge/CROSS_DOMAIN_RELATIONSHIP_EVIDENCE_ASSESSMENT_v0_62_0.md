## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected subgroup examines Life Sciences in relation to Mental Health, Preventive Medicine, and Emergency Medicine. The central test is whether the full Life Sciences subject contributes integrated, multi-level biological evidence or methods that cannot be represented adequately by one previously assessed specialist discipline.

The assessment distinguishes:

- **Life Sciences ↔ Mental Health:** Precision psychiatry and microbiome-informed mental-health research increasingly integrate neural, genetic, molecular, microbial, behavioral, environmental, and clinical information rather than relying on one biomarker or specialist mechanism. A 2026 review of multimodal precision psychiatry combines demographic and psychosocial characteristics with neuroimaging, genetics, and molecular profiling, and discusses mediation, interaction, and machine-learning approaches for heterogeneous pathways and treatment responses. A 2026 scoping review of microbiome-driven precision psychiatry included 28 human studies and found that microbial functions, metabolites, multi-omics signatures, and treatment-response measures may support biological stratification in depression and anxiety; the evidence was predominantly observational, taxonomic findings varied across populations, and microbiome-targeted interventions and AI models remained exploratory. A 2026 clinical-readiness review further distinguishes currently actionable approaches in selected contexts from promising but not-yet-routine neuroimaging, electrophysiological, peripheral-biomarker, digital-phenotyping, and multimodal prediction approaches; multi-omic subtyping remains predominantly experimental for routine individual decisions. A 2026 perspective also links patient-specific brain-function measures, objective behavioral assessment, neuroimaging, and peripheral biomarkers to neural-circuit hypotheses and treatment-response research. Together, these sources support an integrative Life Sciences interface across multiple biological and contextual levels of mental-health research. The candidate is limited to the biological research and precision-stratification interface: it does not reduce Mental Health to psychiatry, imply that mental-health conditions are solely biological, or establish that current multi-omic profiles can diagnose individuals or select routine treatment. [S574–S577]
- **Life Sciences ↔ Preventive Medicine:** Life-course prevention research increasingly combines the exposome with biological susceptibility, intermediate phenotypes, and disease-risk trajectories. A 2026 exposome-wide association study integrated 619 exposure indicators and 305 quantitative phenotypes across ten independent waves of the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey. It reported that aggregate multiexposure profiles can explain clinically relevant phenotypic variance, while emphasizing that most exposure associations remain modest, cross-sectional, and difficult to interpret causally; the authors identify longitudinal follow-up, repeated exposure measurement, functional exposomics, and intervention studies as necessary next steps. A 2026 consensus statement on the life-course exposome emphasizes timing, accumulation and sequencing of exposures, pathway-relevant measurement, context, wearables, administrative records, and remote sensing to identify intervention targets for healthy aging. A 2026 review of wellness-to-disease transitions examines integration of multi-omics, phenomics, digital measurement, and AI for earlier identification of biological changes and prevention-oriented intervention. A 2026 review of computational standards for exposome-wide association studies addresses reproducible analysis of complex exposure mixtures, omics data, and health outcomes. This supports an interface between integrated life-science measurement and preventive medicine's risk assessment, early detection, targeted screening, and prevention planning. It is distinct from the previously screened Life Sciences ↔ Public Health candidate, which concerns population-level molecular surveillance, public-health decisions, intervention systems, and evaluation; this candidate concerns prevention-oriented integration of an individual's or cohort's life-course exposures, biological responses, intermediate phenotypes, and risk trajectory into preventive clinical and health-service decisions. It is also broader than Life Sciences ↔ Nutrition, which was bounded to dietary response and nutritional phenotyping. Current exposome findings are primarily associational, many signals add little incremental predictive value, and causality and clinical utility require further validation. [S578–S581]
- **Life Sciences ↔ Emergency Medicine:** Precision emergency and acute-care research increasingly investigates how integrated biological measurements may help distinguish pathobiological states among patients presenting with similar syndromic or symptom-based labels. A 2025 scoping review of precision acute medical care identified 421 eligible human omics studies among 7,531 screened articles. The review included input from the Society for Academic Emergency Medicine's precision-emergency-medicine consensus process; most included studies were observational, single-nucleotide-polymorphism analysis was common, only a minority included children or older-adult-only cohorts, and one-third were performed outside emergency departments. The authors identified delayed assay turnaround, weak integration with electronic records, and limited randomized evidence as implementation barriers. A 2026 review of sepsis in emergency departments and intensive care describes point-of-care real-time microbiology, bedside immune profiling, echocardiographic hemodynamic phenotyping, omics, transcriptomic signatures, and data-integration approaches for identifying dynamic sepsis subphenotypes and anticipating deterioration. A 2026 precision-critical-care review complements this with a pathway-level model integrating genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics to capture susceptibility, host response, effector function, and real-time physiology; it is adjacent critical-care evidence, not direct proof of routine emergency-department readiness. Together, these sources support a bounded cross-level interface between Life Sciences and emergency medicine for acute biological phenotyping, differential-risk characterization, and research into time-sensitive diagnostic and prognostic pathways. The recommendation does not claim that omics currently replace clinical assessment, that ICU workflows are interchangeable with emergency-department workflows, or that emerging signatures are validated for routine triage. [S582–S584]

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. The recommendations are bounded to the described integrative interfaces, not universal dependencies between the full subject areas. No canonical change is made by this report.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-240 is reciprocal at the mental-health research level: integrated biological evidence informs hypotheses about heterogeneity and response, while clinical phenotypes, lived-function outcomes, and treatment responses guide further biological investigation.
- LSHS-241 is reciprocal at the prevention level: integrated exposures and biological responses inform prevention-oriented risk and early-detection hypotheses, while preventive interventions and longitudinal outcomes guide investigation of exposure-response mechanisms.
- LSHS-242 is reciprocal at the acute-care level: integrated biological measures inform research into acute pathophysiology and risk subgroups, while emergency presentations, time-dependent courses, and outcomes guide investigation of biological responses.

Important boundaries:

- Precision mental-health research does not make mental-health conditions solely biological. Social, behavioral, environmental, developmental, and lived-context factors remain essential to understanding and care.
- Microbiome findings in psychiatric conditions are heterogeneous and predominantly observational. Microbiome-targeted therapies and AI-guided intervention remain exploratory.
- Multi-omic subtyping, peripheral biomarkers, digital phenotyping, and many neuroimaging or electrophysiological prediction approaches remain experimental or not ready for routine individual treatment decisions. External validation, calibration, prospective patient-benefit evidence, and equity evaluation are required.
- Exposome-wide associations are not equivalent to causal effects. Cross-sectional measurement, correlated exposures, measurement error, unmeasured confounding, and population representativeness limit inference.
- Prevention-oriented multi-omics must demonstrate incremental predictive value, decision impact, affordability, and prospective benefit beyond established prevention methods before routine deployment.
- Acute-care omics research remains predominantly observational. Assay turnaround, workflow integration, diverse population representation, and prospective trials remain barriers to emergency-department implementation.
- Critical-care and ICU pathway models can inform acute biological interpretation, but do not automatically transfer to the emergency department's timing, resources, diagnostic uncertainty, or patient flow.
- No candidate implies that a biomarker alone establishes diagnosis, causality, clinical actionability, or a beneficial intervention.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. This assessment does not convert experimental precision-psychiatry models into routine care, exposome associations into causal prevention effects, or acute-care omics signatures into validated emergency-department triage tools.
