## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed pairs selected from the reconciled inventory of 145 remaining pairs.

The topic-focused group examines biological research interfaces with Mental Health at three different levels:

- Genetics ↔ Mental Health: inherited genetic variation, polygenic susceptibility, shared and disorder-specific genetic architecture, and genetic research into mental disorders.
- Molecular Biology ↔ Mental Health: gene expression, transcriptional and epigenetic regulation, RNA biology, molecular signaling, and gene–environment mechanisms studied in mental disorders.
- Cell Biology ↔ Mental Health: cell-type-specific biology, neuronal and glial states, synaptic structure and function, and patient-derived cellular models used to study mental disorders.

The evidentiary scope is deliberately bounded. Much of the direct literature concerns diagnosed psychiatric disorders and specific research cohorts. Mental Health is broader than psychiatric diagnosis and includes psychological, social, cultural, developmental, preventive, therapeutic, recovery, and community dimensions. None of the present assessments reduces the whole subject of Mental Health to biological mechanisms.

Genetics, Molecular Biology, and Cell Biology are also not interchangeable. Genetics investigates inherited variation and genetic architecture; Molecular Biology examines molecular regulation and biochemical mechanisms; Cell Biology examines cellular structures, states, interactions, and functions. These levels can inform one another, but a finding at one level does not automatically establish a finding at another.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory was reconstructed from the v1.13.1 canonical subject lists (18 Life Sciences subjects × 15 Health Sciences subjects = 270 possible pairs) and assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.23.0. The prior ledger contains 125 unique screened pairs. Each selected pair was checked as an unordered pair against all 23 snapshots, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. None appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

The three selected pairs were checked as unordered subject pairs against all assessment tables in v0.1.0 through v0.23.0. The prior ledger contains 125 unique screened pairs with sequential identifiers LSHS-001 through LSHS-125. The exact normalized-pair checks found no match for Genetics ↔ Mental Health, Molecular Biology ↔ Mental Health, or Cell Biology ↔ Mental Health in any of the 23 prior snapshots. Canonical names and identifiers were checked against the v1.13.1 taxonomy.

Previously assessed and related pairings remain distinct:

- Neuroscience ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-005) concerns neural systems and neurobiological research in mental-health conditions. The present Genetics pairing addresses inherited variation and genetic architecture; the Molecular Biology pairing addresses molecular regulatory mechanisms; and the Cell Biology pairing addresses cell types, states, synapses, and cellular models. Neuroscience may connect or integrate these levels, but it is not interchangeable with any of the three canonical subjects.
- Existing Genetics relationships with Medicine, Pharmacology, Preventive Medicine, Nursing, Dentistry, Epidemiology, and Public Health (LSHS-017, 020, 022, 029, 030, 091, and 092) concern other clinical, pharmacological, preventive, professional, or population-health interfaces. LSHS-126 addresses genetic susceptibility and genetic research into mental-health conditions.
- Existing Molecular Biology relationships with Medicine, Surgery, Preventive Medicine, Pharmacology, Epidemiology, Public Health, Nursing, Dentistry, and Nutrition (LSHS-018, 023, 031, 093, 094, 097, 098, and 124) address other clinical, pharmacological, population, professional, or nutritional contexts. LSHS-127 addresses molecular regulation and mechanisms in defined mental-health conditions.
- Existing Cell Biology relationships with Medicine, Surgery, Pharmacology, Nursing, Dentistry, and Nutrition (LSHS-016, 019, 021, 095, 096, and 125) address other clinical, pharmacological, professional, or nutritional contexts. LSHS-128 addresses cell-type states, synaptic biology, and patient-derived cellular models in mental-health research.
- The three new pairs share an overarching research area but have distinct evidence objects: inherited variants and genetic architecture (LSHS-126); molecular regulatory processes and expression (LSHS-127); and cellular types, states, functions, and models (LSHS-128). No evidence is transferred across these pair assessments without a source that supports the specific level.

Direction is reciprocal within each bounded interface:

- LSHS-126: Genetics contributes inherited-variation and genetic-architecture methods; Mental Health supplies disorder, symptom, functional, and experiential phenotypes and research contexts. Clinical and phenotypic observations can refine genetic hypotheses.
- LSHS-127: Molecular Biology characterizes gene expression, epigenetic and transcriptional regulation, and molecular signaling; Mental Health supplies defined conditions, exposures, phenotypes, and translational questions. Clinical and experiential findings can guide molecular research priorities.
- LSHS-128: Cell Biology characterizes cell-type vulnerability, neuronal and glial states, synaptic processes, and cellular models; Mental Health supplies conditions, cohorts, functioning, symptoms, and patient-relevant outcomes. These outcomes can refine cellular-model research questions.

None of these proposals is a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. None implies that biological explanation replaces psychological, social, cultural, environmental, or clinical approaches to mental health.
