## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected topic-focused subgroup examines three distinct interfaces with Dentistry: oral community ecology and preventive dentistry; evolutionary variation in dentition and dental diagnosis; and marine-derived biological resources in dental biomaterials.

The assessment distinguishes:

- **Ecology ↔ Dentistry:** ecological principles applied to the oral cavity, including microbial community structure, biofilm interactions, ecological niches, environmental selection, dysbiosis, and the transition between oral health and disease. This is broader than identifying individual organisms or pathogens. Ecology contributes community- and environment-level models; Dentistry contributes oral-health phenotyping, disease prevention, diagnosis, and intervention contexts. The evidence does not establish that an ecological model alone is a validated clinical diagnostic test or that a microbiome profile can independently determine treatment.
- **Evolutionary Biology ↔ Dentistry:** evolutionary and comparative interpretation of tooth development, dental morphology, adaptation to diet and function, population variation, and inherited or developmentally constrained dental traits, in relation to orthodontic diagnosis, prosthodontic design, endodontic anatomy, periodontal complexity, and personalized treatment planning. The evidence supports a research and interpretive interface, but the degree to which evolutionary findings independently improve clinical outcomes remains incompletely established.
- **Marine Biology ↔ Dentistry:** marine organism-derived resources and biomaterials—including marine algal hydroxyapatite, coral-derived mineral scaffolds, chitosan, and marine adhesive proteins—investigated for periodontal regeneration, oral bone grafting, peri-implant repair, and other dental or craniofacial applications. The evidence demonstrates marine-derived material use in dental research and surgery, but the reviewed contributions are primarily framed through biomaterials engineering, dental surgery, and regenerative dentistry. The sources do not yet establish a sufficiently distinct contribution from Marine Biology as a disciplinary field, beyond marine resource provenance and organism-derived material properties. This pair is not promoted.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not prove that no narrower future interface could be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.
