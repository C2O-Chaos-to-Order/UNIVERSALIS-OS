## 2. Method and important terminology boundaries

Each pair is assessed for a specific interface between the canonical Life Sciences subject and Pharmacology. Evidence was taken from peer-reviewed reviews, professional or standards-based material, and current public-health or clinical guidance. The assessment distinguishes organism-specific biological knowledge from drug action, disposition, exposure, susceptibility interpretation, and therapeutic selection.

Important boundaries:
- **Microbiology is not synonymous with Pharmacology.** Microbiology identifies and characterizes microorganisms, including their growth, physiology, susceptibility, and resistance mechanisms. Pharmacology studies drug action, targets, dose-response, exposure, pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, toxicity, and therapeutic use. Their interface is visible in antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST), organism-informed drug selection, antimicrobial pharmacodynamics, and stewardship. A laboratory susceptibility category is not by itself a guarantee of clinical success.
- **The Microbiology ↔ Pharmacology evidence in this report is primarily clinical and antimicrobial.** It does not claim that all Microbiology is concerned with human infection or that all pharmacology is antimicrobial pharmacology. Clinical microbiology laboratory outputs must be interpreted alongside infection site, host factors, achievable drug exposure, and clinical context.
- **Virology is not synonymous with Pharmacology.** Virology explains viral structure, replication, entry, genome expression, assembly, release, host interactions, and viral variation. Pharmacology studies how therapeutic agents engage viral or host targets, alter viral life-cycle processes, achieve effective exposure, and produce benefit or harm. Antiviral drug discovery is a direct interface, but the existence of a molecular target does not establish that a safe, effective, clinically approved drug exists.
- **Antiviral mechanisms are virus- and drug-specific.** Agents may inhibit viral entry, uncoating, polymerases, proteases, integrases, capsid functions, assembly, or release. A mechanism effective against one viral family cannot automatically be generalized to another.
- **Mycology is not synonymous with Pharmacology.** Mycology studies fungi, including taxonomy, biology, pathogenicity, growth, ecological roles, and host interactions. Pharmacology studies antifungal drug mechanisms, exposure, response, dosing, toxicity, and therapeutic use. The interface is strongest in medical mycology, antifungal susceptibility testing, fungal resistance, pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) optimization, and antifungal stewardship.
- **Fungal susceptibility results are not self-interpreting.** MIC values depend on species identification, testing method, endpoint, breakpoint or epidemiological cutoff availability, infection site, fungal burden, biofilm, host immunity, and achievable drug exposure. A susceptible in-vitro result does not guarantee clinical response.
- **The three Life Sciences subjects remain distinct.** Microbiology is not merely bacterial microbiology; Virology concerns viruses; Mycology concerns fungi. Their therapeutic interfaces are different because their biological targets, replication or growth processes, susceptibility methods, and available drug classes differ.
- A candidate RD-P is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. Evidence of an interface does not establish universal dependency, containment, hierarchy, or guaranteed treatment benefit.
- Evidence grades describe the strength and directness of the bounded subject-pair interface, not the certainty that every antimicrobial or antiviral treatment is effective in every patient or organism.

- Mycology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-048);
- Mycology ↔ Emergency Medicine (LSHS-049).

These pairs do not duplicate the new pharmacology pairings. The distinction is substantive:
- LSHS-108 focuses on microorganism identification and susceptibility evidence as inputs to antimicrobial selection and PK/PD-informed treatment, rather than the broader Microbiology ↔ Medicine or Microbiology ↔ Public Health interfaces.
- LSHS-109 focuses on viral life-cycle mechanisms as targets of antiviral drug discovery and treatment, rather than Virology's previously assessed clinical and public-health interfaces.
- LSHS-110 focuses on fungal susceptibility, antifungal drug mechanisms, and PK/PD optimization, rather than Mycology's previously assessed clinical and dental interfaces.

Direction is recorded as reciprocal at the bounded interface because organism-specific evidence informs drug development and treatment, while pharmacological responses and treatment failures generate questions for microbiological, virological, or mycological investigation. This is not a claim of equal contribution in every clinical context, nor a universal dependency, hierarchy, or enablement edge.
