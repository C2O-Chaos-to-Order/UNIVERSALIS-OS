## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed interfaces in oral and dental health, selected from the reconciled inventory of 106 remaining pairs:

- Neuroscience ↔ Dentistry: trigeminal and other cranial neuralgia, neuropathic orofacial pain, and the differential diagnosis of neural pain that may be mistaken for odontogenic or dental pain.
- Physiology ↔ Dentistry: salivary physiology, oral function, mastication, swallowing, speech, oral homeostasis, and salivary assessment and diagnostics in dental care.
- Botany ↔ Dentistry: examined as a candidate but not promoted. Plant-derived extracts and natural products are being studied and used in oral-health products, but the evidence reviewed does not demonstrate a sufficiently distinct Botany-specific knowledge contribution to Dentistry beyond narrower phytochemistry, microbiology, pharmacology, and product-formulation interfaces.

The two candidate relationships concern different dimensions of oral health. Neuroscience contributes knowledge of neural pathways, cranial nerve function, neuropathic pain mechanisms, and neurological differential diagnosis; Dentistry applies this knowledge when assessing orofacial pain, excluding odontogenic causes, avoiding inappropriate dental procedures, and referring patients for appropriate medical or neurological evaluation. Physiology contributes knowledge of saliva, salivary-gland function, oral fluid homeostasis, and the coordinated physiological functions of mastication, swallowing, and speech; Dentistry uses this knowledge in oral examination, diagnosis, risk assessment, and care planning.

The Botany non-promotion is specific to the full canonical subject-pair claim. Plant-derived ingredients are an active oral-health research area, and some clinical trials report benefits for selected products. However, plant extracts are usually studied as formulated products or as sources of phytochemicals with antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, or other pharmacological effects. The sources do not establish that botanical taxonomy, plant physiology, plant ecology, or another sufficiently distinct Botany-specific body of knowledge is systematically integrated into dental assessment or practice. Botanical source identity and quality may matter to product development, but that alone does not establish a broad Botany ↔ Dentistry relationship. The assessment does not deny the value of plant-derived oral products or preclude future reassessment if direct botanical-science integration is demonstrated.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory contains 18 Life Sciences subjects and 15 Health Sciences subjects, producing 270 possible cross-domain pairs. The preceding assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.36.0 record 164 unique screened pairs, 149 candidate recommendations, and 15 screened but not promoted or deferred, leaving 106 individually unassessed pairs before this batch.

Each selected pair was checked as an unordered subject pair against all 36 prior assessment snapshots, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. The historical assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-164 were reconciled. None of the three selected pairs appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not establish that no narrower future relationship could ever be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

The three selected pairs were checked as unordered subject pairs against all 36 prior assessment snapshots, v0.1.0 through v0.36.0. The reconciled prior ledger contains 164 unique screened pairs with assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-164. Exact normalized-pair checks found no occurrence of Neuroscience ↔ Dentistry, Physiology ↔ Dentistry, or Botany ↔ Dentistry in the prior ledger. Canonical subject names and identifiers were checked against taxonomy v1.13.1.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- Anatomy ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-149) concerns oral, dental, maxillofacial, and craniofacial structures and spatial relationships. Neuroscience ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-165) concerns neural mechanisms of orofacial pain and the dental differential diagnosis of neurological pain; it is not a repeat of the anatomical interface.
- Neuroscience ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-005), Neuroscience ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-034), Neuroscience ↔ Occupational Therapy (LSHS-084), Neuroscience ↔ Medicine (LSHS-135), and Neuroscience ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-032) concern other disciplinary interfaces. None is the exact dental application of trigeminal and cranial neuralgia knowledge assessed in LSHS-165.
- Physiology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-006), Physiology ↔ Emergency Medicine (LSHS-037), Physiology ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-038), Physiology ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-039), Physiology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-102), Physiology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-115), Physiology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-144), Physiology ↔ Public Health (LSHS-145), and Physiology ↔ Epidemiology (LSHS-146) concern other applications. LSHS-166 is specifically the oral physiological interface involving salivary function, oral homeostasis, and oral physical functions.
- Microbiology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-052), Mycology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-048), Immunology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-053), Cell Biology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-096), Biotechnology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-100), Molecular Biology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-098), and Virology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-150) concern microbial, fungal, immune, cellular, technological, molecular, and viral interfaces with dental health. Botany ↔ Dentistry in LSHS-167 is not promoted merely because some oral-health products contain plant extracts; this does not duplicate or revise any of those earlier relationships.
- Plant-derived compounds and botanical-origin products are not by themselves proof of a general Botany ↔ Dentistry interface. The current evidence is more directly about phytochemistry, microbial biofilms, product formulation, and pharmacological effects. LSHS-167 therefore remains distinct from Biotechnology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-100), Microbiology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-052), and other existing dental interfaces.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-165 is reciprocal within the bounded orofacial-pain interface: Neuroscience contributes neural mechanisms and explanations of neuropathic pain; Dentistry uses these concepts to assess symptoms, distinguish odontogenic from non-odontogenic pain, and refer appropriately. Dental observations and procedure histories can inform neurological assessment.
- LSHS-166 is reciprocal within the bounded oral-physiology interface: Physiology explains salivary and oral-function mechanisms; Dentistry applies this knowledge in examination, diagnosis, prevention, and care. Dental findings and salivary measurements can inform physiological research.
- LSHS-167 has no promoted direction or subtype. The evidence does not justify a general subject-pair recommendation.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. The assessment does not equate neuralgia with odontogenic disease, does not treat saliva as the sole determinant of oral health, and does not reclassify plant-derived dental products as proof that Dentistry depends on Botany.
