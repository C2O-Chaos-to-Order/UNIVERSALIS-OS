## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed pairs selected from the reconciled inventory of 139 remaining pairs.

The topic-focused group examines the genetic, molecular, and cellular biology of exercise-based physical therapy:

- Genetics ↔ Physical Therapy: whether inherited genetic variation has a demonstrated, reproducible role in predicting response to physical-therapy interventions or in selecting rehabilitation treatment.
- Molecular Biology ↔ Physical Therapy: molecular responses to therapeutic exercise and loading, including gene expression, microRNA regulation, and pathways associated with skeletal-muscle adaptation.
- Cell Biology ↔ Physical Therapy: cellular mechanotransduction, tissue adaptation, muscle stem-cell responses, and repair mechanisms that may explain responses to structured movement and therapeutic loading.

These three levels are not interchangeable. Genetics concerns inherited variation; Molecular Biology concerns molecular mechanisms and regulation; Cell Biology concerns cellular structure, state, function, and interactions. Evidence for an exercise-associated molecular response does not by itself establish that an inherited variant predicts a patient's physical-therapy outcome. Cellular mechanotransduction also does not establish the clinical effectiveness of a particular rehabilitation protocol.

The assessment distinguishes physical therapy as a clinical profession from athletic training, sport performance, general exercise, and basic exercise physiology. Evidence from athletic cohorts or exercise models may inform research questions, but it must not be silently relabelled as evidence for physical-therapy treatment selection or patient outcomes.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory was reconciled against the v1.13.1 canonical subject lists (18 Life Sciences subjects × 15 Health Sciences subjects = 270 possible pairs) and assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.25.0. The prior ledger contains 131 unique screened pairs. Each selected pair was checked as an unordered subject pair against all 25 snapshots, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. None appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion records insufficient evidence for a sufficiently specific relationship in this assessment; it is not proof that the subjects are unrelated. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

The three selected pairs were checked as unordered subject pairs against all assessment tables in v0.1.0 through v0.25.0. The prior ledger contains 131 unique screened pairs with sequential identifiers LSHS-001 through LSHS-131. Exact normalized-pair checks found no occurrence of Genetics ↔ Physical Therapy, Molecular Biology ↔ Physical Therapy, or Cell Biology ↔ Physical Therapy in any of the 25 prior snapshots. Canonical names and identifiers were checked against the v1.13.1 taxonomy.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- Physiology ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-039) concerns organ-system function and physiological responses in physical-therapy contexts. It is not the same as Molecular Biology ↔ Physical Therapy, which focuses on gene expression and molecular regulation, or Cell Biology ↔ Physical Therapy, which focuses on cellular mechanotransduction and tissue adaptation.
- Neuroscience ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-034) concerns neural systems and rehabilitation-related physical-therapy applications. It does not replace cellular and molecular study of skeletal muscle, connective tissue, or other mechanically loaded tissues.
- Anatomy ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-087) concerns regional structures and anatomical knowledge in physical-therapy assessment or intervention. It is not the same as cellular mechanosensing or molecular adaptation to loading.
- Molecular Biology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-124) concerns molecular responses to nutrients and dietary compounds. The current Molecular Biology pairing concerns exercise- and loading-associated molecular responses in rehabilitation.
- Cell Biology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-125) concerns cellular nutrient sensing and metabolism. The current Cell Biology pairing concerns mechanical stimuli, cellular adaptation, and tissue repair.
- Existing Genetics pairings with Medicine, Pharmacology, Preventive Medicine, Nursing, Dentistry, Epidemiology, Public Health, and Mental Health (LSHS-017, 020, 022, 029, 030, 091, 092, and 126) address other clinical, pharmacological, preventive, professional, population, or mental-health research interfaces. None establishes genotype-guided physical-therapy treatment utility.
- Existing exercise, physical-function, or rehabilitation studies in other pairings are not used as duplicate subject-pair records. The present pairs were checked by exact normalized canonical subject pair, not by keyword overlap.

The three new assessments have distinct evidentiary objects:

- LSHS-132 asks whether inherited variation predicts or modifies response to physical-therapy interventions. Current adjacent evidence did not meet the threshold for promotion.
- LSHS-133 asks how molecular regulation and exercise-responsive molecular pathways relate to therapeutic loading and rehabilitation research.
- LSHS-134 asks how cells sense and respond to mechanical loading, and how cellular adaptation and repair may help explain tissue responses to physical therapy.

Direction is reciprocal only for the two candidates:

- LSHS-133: Molecular Biology characterizes molecular changes and mechanisms associated with exercise and loading; Physical Therapy supplies clinically prescribed intervention types, dosing context, and functional outcomes. Rehabilitation observations can guide molecular research, and molecular findings can refine hypotheses for rehabilitation science.
- LSHS-134: Cell Biology characterizes mechanosensing, cellular signaling, stem-cell behavior, and repair; Physical Therapy applies therapeutic loading and measures movement and function. Clinical loading questions can guide cell research, and cell-level mechanisms can refine rehabilitation hypotheses.

No direction or subtype is accepted for LSHS-132. None of the three assessments is a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim.
