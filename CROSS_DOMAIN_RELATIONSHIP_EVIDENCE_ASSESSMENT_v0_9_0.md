## 2. Method and decision rule

Each pair is assessed for evidence of a specific interface between the two canonical subject scopes. A connection through zoonotic disease, comparative medicine, ecological determinants, marine toxins, or harmful algal blooms is not treated as proof that either whole discipline is contained within, equivalent to, or universally dependent on the other.

The review distinguishes:
- a relationship between distinct canonical subjects from shared identity or equivalence;
- a bounded clinical, research, surveillance, or preventive interface from a whole-subject relationship;
- knowledge contribution from operational dependency or enablement;
- evidence quality from owner acceptance and canonical registration.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. Direction is recorded only where the evidence supports a contribution or information flow. Each relationship is bounded to the scope described in its row.

One Health is used as a multidisciplinary evidence framework for human, animal, and ecosystem health. Its existence does not automatically prove every Zoology/Medicine, Ecology/Medicine, or Marine Biology/Health Sciences pair; the pair-specific evidence and boundaries are stated separately below.

## 4. Duplicate and scope cross-check

The eight pairs were checked against assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.8.0. None is an exact duplicate of a previously screened unordered pair.

One of the original nine possible pairs was deliberately excluded:
- **Ecology ↔ Public Health** is already LSHS-007 from the initial eight-pair pilot. Its prior evidence concerned the WHO One Health interface linking human, animal, plant, and ecosystem health. It is not counted in this report.

Related but distinct pairs that must not be conflated:
- Zoology ↔ Public Health and Zoology ↔ Preventive Medicine address animal-host knowledge in relation to population surveillance and targeted prevention; they are not the same pair as Ecology ↔ Public Health.
- Ecology ↔ Medicine and Ecology ↔ Preventive Medicine concern ecological/environmental determinants at clinical and preventive levels, respectively; neither duplicates Ecology ↔ Public Health.
- Marine Biology ↔ Medicine concerns clinical illness or translational medical use arising from marine organisms, pathogens, toxins, or bioresources; Marine Biology ↔ Public Health concerns population surveillance; Marine Biology ↔ Preventive Medicine concerns exposure reduction and prevention.
- The already assessed Virology, Microbiology, Mycology, and Immunology relationships concern different canonical Life Sciences subjects and are not duplicates of the three selected subjects in this batch.
