## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected subgroup examines the three remaining Life Sciences-root pairings identified in v0.58.0: Nursing, Surgery, and Pharmacology. The central assessment question is whether the full Life Sciences subject contributes a distinct, independently describable body of research or method beyond the specialist relationships already screened.

The assessment distinguishes:

- **Life Sciences ↔ Nursing:** a 2026 scoping review identified 66 publications reporting health-service-delivery outcomes for nursing in genomics, drawn from 11,646 retrieved publications. The included literature covered nursing involvement in oncology, pharmacogenomics, genetic counselling, rare genetic diseases, and symptom science. It reports that multidisciplinary and interprofessional nursing teams can improve access to genomic healthcare and reduce burden on genetic specialists, while concluding that nurses remain underutilized and underprepared in genomic healthcare. This demonstrates a real and developing interface between genomics and nursing service delivery. However, the evidence is predominantly genomics-focused, which is already represented by the specialist Genetics ↔ Nursing assessment (LSHS-029). It does not establish a separate contribution from the full breadth of Life Sciences, beyond genetics/genomics and other narrower biological domains already assessed against Nursing. A broad root-level pairing would therefore overstate the scope of the evidence and duplicate more precise relationships. This pair is not promoted.
- **Life Sciences ↔ Surgery:** 2026 reviews describe organoids, bioengineered tissue constructs, and microphysiological systems for plastic and reconstructive surgery. The platforms combine human-derived multicellular cultures, fluid perfusion, mechanical loading, chemical gradients, tissue modelling, and functional measures for research into wound healing, keloids, fat grafting, and neural or musculoskeletal regeneration. These are important interdisciplinary developments, but the reviewed surgical applications are attributable to narrower and more directly identifiable areas such as tissue engineering, biotechnology, cell and molecular biology, and regenerative biology. The assessment also already contains Biology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-230), which is specifically bounded to comparative and organism-level tissue repair and regenerative biology; Biotechnology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-026), which addresses engineered tissue and cell-based products; and other specialist pairs. The current sources do not establish an additional full Life Sciences contribution that is independent of these narrower interfaces and the broader translational pathways already assessed. This pair is not promoted at the full canonical subject scope assessed.
- **Life Sciences ↔ Pharmacology:** quantitative systems pharmacology (QSP) integrates mechanistic representations of biology with quantitative pharmacology to inform decisions across drug development. A 2026 review describes QSP as an established component of model-informed drug development and identifies applications in mechanism-based dose and regimen selection, combination strategies, biomarker-informed patient stratification, and lifecycle management. A 2026 drug-target landscape review maps 686 biomolecules modulated by 1,702 drugs and describes the transformation of drug discovery through genetics, genomics, proteomics, and diversification of therapeutic modalities. A 2026 review of computational phenotypic drug discovery explains how compound effects can be studied in living systems without prior knowledge of the molecular target. Human organoid reviews describe stem-cell- and tissue-derived models that recapitulate aspects of human tissue architecture and physiology for disease modelling, drug screening, efficacy, and toxicity assessment. Together, these sources establish a bounded, systems-level interface in which integrated life-science knowledge and living-system models inform pharmacological target assessment, mechanism interpretation, exposure–response modelling, and drug-development decisions. This is distinct from the previously assessed specialist pairings that examine one biological discipline or technology at a time. It does not imply that every pharmacological decision requires systems biology, omics, organoids, or new life-science experiments, and it does not make preclinical results equivalent to clinical evidence.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not prove that no narrower future interface could be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- **Life Sciences ↔ Nursing:** Genetics ↔ Nursing (LSHS-029) concerns the specialist genetics/genomics and nursing interface. Microbiology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-013), Virology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-045), Immunology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-054), Cell Biology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-095), Physiology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-144), and Anatomy ↔ Nursing (LSHS-147) address other defined scientific contributions. LSHS-231 is the full Life Sciences umbrella pair. It is not promoted because current service-delivery evidence is predominantly genomics-focused and does not establish an independent full-scope contribution beyond the specialist pairings.
- **Life Sciences ↔ Surgery:** Biotechnology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-026) concerns selected bioengineered tissue and cell-based repair products; Cell Biology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-016) was not promoted as a general pair; Zoology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-186) concerns comparative animal anatomy and xenotransplant-related surgical models; Biology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-230) concerns comparative and organism-level tissue repair, regeneration, and wound-healing models. LSHS-232 examines human-derived microphysiological systems, organoids, and engineered tissue platforms but does not establish a distinct contribution from the full Life Sciences subject beyond these narrower fields and the broader translational pathway.
- **Life Sciences ↔ Pharmacology:** Biotechnology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-025) addresses biologic modalities; Cell Biology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-021) addresses cellular targets and drug-action mechanisms; Immunology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-104), Microbiology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-108), Virology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-109), Mycology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-110), Neuroscience ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-114), Physiology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-115), Anatomy ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-116), Botany ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-120), Marine Biology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-121), Zoology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-122), and Evolutionary Biology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-139) concern individual specialist contributions. LSHS-233 is distinct because it concerns integrated systems-level biological modelling, multi-omics, living-system phenotypes, and cross-level mechanisms used in QSP and drug-development decisions, rather than a single specialist science or therapeutic modality.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-231 has no promoted direction or subtype. Genomics-oriented nursing service delivery is represented by the specific Genetics ↔ Nursing relationship; the full Life Sciences pairing is not independently supported.
- LSHS-232 has no promoted direction or subtype. The current model-system and regenerative-surgery sources are attributable to more specific disciplines and platforms, without a distinct full Life Sciences relationship beyond existing evidence.
- LSHS-233 is reciprocal at the systems-pharmacology research and development level: integrated life-science data and mechanistic models inform drug-target assessment and pharmacological decisions; pharmacological perturbation, exposure–response and drug-response studies generate biological questions and model-validation requirements.

Important boundaries:

- Genomic healthcare is a meaningful nursing service area, but evidence for nursing involvement in genomics does not establish a broad Life Sciences contribution independent of Genetics ↔ Nursing and other specialty pairings. The 2026 scoping review itself notes that nursing remains underutilized and underprepared in genomic healthcare.
- Microphysiological systems, organoids, and engineered regenerative constructs are interdisciplinary research platforms. Their use in surgical research does not make the entire Life Sciences discipline a distinct contributor where the evidence is better assigned to tissue engineering, biotechnology, regenerative biology, and narrower biological methods.
- Quantitative systems pharmacology integrates biology and pharmacology, but it is a bounded approach rather than a universal description of Pharmacology. It must use clearly defined questions, contexts of use, model assumptions, uncertainty assessment, and validation. Omics associations or phenotypic signals do not independently establish drug-target causality or clinical benefit.
- Preclinical organoid, in vitro, computational, and living-system models do not replace clinical pharmacology, safety studies, or controlled clinical evaluation.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. This assessment does not treat genomics-focused nursing service evidence as a full Life Sciences contribution, treat surgical model engineering as a unique umbrella relationship, or convert QSP and preclinical models into proof of clinical efficacy.
