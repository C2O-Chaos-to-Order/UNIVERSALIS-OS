## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed pairs selected from the reconciled inventory of 142 remaining pairs.

The topic-focused group examines Neuroscience interfaces with three population- and prevention-oriented Health Sciences subjects:

- Neuroscience ↔ Epidemiology: population studies of neurological conditions, neuroepidemiological methods, incidence, prevalence, determinants, outcomes, and disease-burden measurement.
- Neuroscience ↔ Public Health: translation of neurological and brain-health knowledge into population-level policy, services, health information systems, research priorities, and intersectoral programmes.
- Neuroscience ↔ Preventive Medicine: evidence-informed risk assessment and interventions intended to reduce cognitive decline and the incidence or impact of selected neurological conditions.

The three interfaces are related but not interchangeable. Epidemiology measures patterns and determinants in defined populations. Public Health organizes population-level policy, services, prevention, surveillance, and health-system responses. Preventive Medicine applies evidence to risk assessment and preventive interventions in clinical and other practice settings. Neuroscience contributes knowledge about the nervous system, brain function, neurological conditions, and brain-health mechanisms, but does not by itself determine population estimates, public policy, or the effectiveness of preventive interventions.

The evidence is condition-specific. Neurological conditions are diverse, and evidence for stroke, epilepsy, dementia, migraine, Parkinson's disease, or other conditions cannot automatically be generalized to all neurological health. Neuroscience is also not interchangeable with Neurology, Epidemiology, Public Health, or Preventive Medicine.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory was reconciled against the v1.13.1 subject lists (18 Life Sciences subjects × 15 Health Sciences subjects = 270 possible pairs) and all 24 assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.24.0. The prior ledger contains 128 unique screened pairs. Each selected pair was checked as an unordered subject pair against the complete ledger, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. None appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. No canonical change is made by this report.

Previously assessed and related pairings remain distinct:

- Neuroscience ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-005 and LSHS-126 are different subject-pair rows only in the latter? The existing LSHS-005 record concerns neural systems and biological processes studied in relation to mental-health conditions. The present pairs concern neurological population studies, public-health responses, and prevention of selected neurological conditions. Mental Health and neurological disorders overlap in some conditions and services but are not treated as equivalent categories.
- Neuroscience ↔ Emergency Medicine (LSHS-033) concerns acute presentations and emergency assessment; it is not the same as population-level neurological epidemiology, public-health strategy, or preventive care.
- Neuroscience ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-034), Occupational Therapy (LSHS-084), Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-032), and Surgery (LSHS-101) address rehabilitation, functional intervention, or operative practice rather than the present population and prevention interfaces.
- Neuroscience ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-114) concerns neural targets, drug action, and neuropharmacology; it does not substitute for neurological disease measurement, health-system policy, or preventive risk reduction.
- Epidemiology pairings already assessed include Microbiology, Virology, Evolutionary Biology, Genetics, Molecular Biology, Mycology, Ecology, Zoology, and Marine Biology (LSHS-003, 004, 089, 091, 093, 107, 111, 117, 118, and 119). These examine specific pathogen, inherited, molecular, fungal, ecological, animal-host, or marine-organism contributions. LSHS-129 instead examines Neuroscience ↔ Epidemiology as a dedicated neurological population-study interface.
- Public Health pairings already assessed include Ecology, Microbiology, Virology, Immunology, Biotechnology, Zoology, Marine Biology, Botany, Evolutionary Biology, Molecular Biology, and Mycology (LSHS-007, 027, 040, 043, 047, 056, 061, 065, 090, 092, 094, and 112). Those records concern their respective subject-specific contributions to population health. LSHS-130 addresses neurological and brain-health knowledge in public-health systems and programmes.
- Preventive Medicine pairings already assessed include Virology, Genetics, Microbiology, Immunology, Mycology, Zoology, Ecology, and Botany (LSHS-015, 022, 028, 042, 046, 051, 057, 059, and 072). Those records concern viral, genetic, microbial, immune, fungal, animal, ecological, or botanical risk and prevention interfaces. LSHS-131 is specifically about brain-health and neurological risk reduction.

The three new pairs have distinct evidence objects:

- LSHS-129 measures the occurrence, distribution, determinants, and burden of neurological conditions in defined populations.
- LSHS-130 translates neurological and brain-health knowledge into population policy, programmes, services, and health information systems.
- LSHS-131 applies brain-health and cognitive-decline evidence to risk assessment and preventive interventions.

Direction is reciprocal within each bounded interface:

- LSHS-129: Neuroscience contributes neurological phenotypes and relevant disease knowledge; Epidemiology measures distributions, determinants, and outcomes in populations. Epidemiological findings can refine neuroscience questions and priority populations.
- LSHS-130: Neuroscience contributes evidence about brain function and neurological conditions; Public Health integrates this with broader population, social, service, and policy evidence. Public-health priorities and outcome data can guide research priorities.
- LSHS-131: Neuroscience contributes brain-health and cognitive-outcome knowledge; Preventive Medicine evaluates and applies evidence-based risk reduction and prevention. Outcomes can refine neuroscience and translational research.

None of these proposals is a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. Neuroscience does not replace epidemiology, public-health science, preventive medicine, clinical neurology, or the wider social and environmental knowledge needed for effective neurological health responses.
