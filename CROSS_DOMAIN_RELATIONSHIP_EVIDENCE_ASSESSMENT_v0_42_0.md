## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected subgroup examines biotechnology interfaces with physical rehabilitation, rehabilitation science, and occupational therapy.

The assessment distinguishes three scopes:

- **Biotechnology ↔ Physical Therapy:** biotechnology-derived orthobiologics, cellular therapies, regenerative products, and tissue-engineering approaches in relation to physical-therapy modalities, graded loading, mechanotherapy, and functional recovery. The proposed interface is limited to research and care contexts where a defined biological technology and a physical-therapy intervention or outcome are explicitly connected.
- **Biotechnology ↔ Rehabilitation Science:** cell and gene therapies, tissue engineering, biomaterials, regenerative medicine, and engineered biological products in relation to rehabilitation science, functional recovery, rehabilitation research design, and integration of biological and physical interventions. This is the broadest and most direct interface in this subgroup.
- **Biotechnology ↔ Occupational Therapy:** considered through assistive technology, adaptive equipment, and technology-enabled participation. These areas are important to occupational therapy, but the sources reviewed do not establish that biotechnology, as distinct from biomedical engineering, electronics, software, materials engineering, or general assistive technology, is a sufficiently direct and stable disciplinary contributor to Occupational Therapy. This pair is not promoted.

The evidence supports a developing regenerative-rehabilitation research area, not a claim that regenerative or cellular products are universally effective, routinely indicated, or interchangeable. Several sources explicitly describe emerging technologies, early translational work, variable evidence quality, and the need for stronger clinical trials and standardization. A candidate RD-P relationship therefore does not endorse a treatment, product, protocol, or clinical indication.

For the two promoted candidates, Biotechnology contributes biological platforms and technologies such as cell and gene therapies, orthobiologics, tissue-engineered constructs, scaffolds, and bioactive materials. Physical Therapy contributes assessment and delivery of physical interventions, including movement, graded loading, exercise, and mechanotherapy. Rehabilitation Science contributes the broader research frameworks, functional outcome measures, and interdisciplinary models through which biological and physical interventions are evaluated and integrated.

The distinction between Physical Therapy and Rehabilitation Science is preserved. Physical Therapy is a specific health-professional and intervention domain; Rehabilitation Science is a broader research and knowledge domain that can include multiple professions, populations, mechanisms, and functional outcomes. The same evidence may inform both, but the two canonical subjects are not interchangeable.

The occupational-therapy non-promotion is a scope boundary, not a statement that occupational therapy does not use technology. The American Occupational Therapy Association describes assistive technology evaluation, recommendation, and training as part of occupational therapy practice. However, assistive technology is not synonymous with biotechnology. A direct relationship would require evidence that biotechnological methods or products—not simply digital devices, electronics, adaptive equipment, or engineering—make a distinct contribution to occupational-therapy research or practice.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory contains 18 Life Sciences subjects and 15 Health Sciences subjects, producing 270 possible cross-domain pairs. The preceding assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.41.0 record 179 unique screened pairs, 160 candidate recommendations, and 19 screened but not promoted or deferred, leaving 91 individually unassessed pairs before this batch.

Each selected pair was checked as an unordered subject pair against all 41 prior assessment snapshots, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. Historical assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-179 were reconciled. None of the three selected pairs appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not establish that no narrower future relationship could ever be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

The three selected pairs were checked as unordered subject pairs against all 41 prior assessment snapshots, v0.1.0 through v0.41.0. The reconciled prior ledger contains 179 unique screened pairs with assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-179. Exact pair checks found no prior occurrence of Biotechnology ↔ Physical Therapy, Biotechnology ↔ Rehabilitation Science, or Biotechnology ↔ Occupational Therapy. Checks covered both name-only historical rows and stable-ID rows. Canonical subject names and identifiers were checked against taxonomy v1.13.1.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- Biotechnology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-024), Biotechnology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-025), Biotechnology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-026), Biotechnology ↔ Public Health (LSHS-027), and Biotechnology ↔ Preventive Medicine (LSHS-028) concern other clinical, pharmacological, surgical, and population-health applications. They are not duplicates of the physical-therapy or rehabilitation-science interfaces.
- Biotechnology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-099), Biotechnology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-100), and Biotechnology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-170) address other health-professional or applied contexts. None is the exact pair assessed here.
- Cell Biology ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-163) concerns cellular mechanisms and rehabilitation-related adaptation. Biotechnology ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-181) is limited to biotechnology-derived platforms and their integration with rehabilitation science; the two interfaces are related but not interchangeable.
- Cell Biology ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-134), Physiology ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-039), and Virology ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-153) concern different biological contributions to physical therapy. They do not duplicate the biotechnology-specific regenerative platform interface in LSHS-180.
- Biotechnology ↔ Occupational Therapy is assessed separately from Biotechnology ↔ Physical Therapy and Biotechnology ↔ Rehabilitation Science. Occupational therapy's use of assistive technology does not establish a biotechnology relationship by itself.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-180 is reciprocal within a bounded translational interface: biotechnology contributes biological products and mechanisms; physical therapy contributes physical interventions and functional assessment. Research questions and functional observations can also inform the development and evaluation of biological interventions.
- LSHS-181 is reciprocal within a broader research interface: biotechnology contributes biological platforms; rehabilitation science contributes functional outcome frameworks, intervention integration, and translational study design.
- LSHS-182 has no promoted direction or subtype. The reviewed occupational-therapy technology evidence does not establish a biotechnology-specific contribution.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. This assessment does not endorse unproven cellular or gene therapies, establish a standard rehabilitation protocol after regenerative intervention, or equate assistive technology with biotechnology.
