# Alchemy Taxonomy

**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** Foundational draft — context register established; domain classifications pending definition and review  
**Coverage:** 22 C2O and UNIVERSALIS contexts

## 1. Purpose

The Alchemy Taxonomy establishes a consistent classification system for the bounded processes, domains, responsibilities, and transformations represented by Alchemy across the C2O and UNIVERSALIS ecosystem.

This foundational release establishes the taxonomy's scope, terminology, context register, classification requirements, and validation rules. It does not presume that an unreviewed domain or specialized classification is already approved.

## 2. Foundational definition

**Alchemy** is the bounded, purposeful process through which a specified subject, domain, system, process, capability, relationship, resource, or state is identified and understood; its relevant constituents and conditions are classified and organized; its relationships and constraints are examined; and defined operations are applied to develop, integrate, maintain, protect, recover, renew, or transform it toward a coherent and usable condition. The resulting state is evaluated against its stated purpose, boundaries, and available evidence.

This is the working root definition for the taxonomy. Its wording and implications remain subject to review before being treated as permanently settled.

## 3. What the taxonomy classifies

The taxonomy must keep four different concepts distinct:

1. **Alchemy domain** — the subject matter or field to which a bounded Alchemical process applies.
2. **Alchemy classification** — a defined category of Alchemical responsibility, process, or specialization within a domain.
3. **Context** — the C2O or UNIVERSALIS setting in which an Alchemy may apply. The 22 contexts in this file establish the coverage universe; they are not, by themselves, 22 Alchemy classifications.
4. **Contextual expression** — the bounded way an Alchemy applies within one or more of those contexts. A contextual expression does not automatically constitute a new Alchemy classification.

A repository, operating system, application, intelligence, assistant, or guardian is not automatically an Alchemy classification merely because the taxonomy applies to it.

## 5. Working taxonomy organization

The following is the working organizational model to be validated as the taxonomy develops:

- **Root Alchemy** — the foundational meaning and common identity of Alchemy.
- **Alchemy domains** — distinct areas of subject matter to which Alchemy applies.
- **Specialized Alchemies** — bounded classifications within an established domain, each with a distinct purpose and responsibility.
- **Contextual expressions** — the application of an established Alchemy within one or more of the 22 contexts.

This organization is a structural model, not permission to create domain names or specialized classifications without establishing their definitions and boundaries.

## 6. Required record for every Alchemy classification

Every proposed classification must be documented using a consistent record. Fields that do not apply must be marked as not applicable with a reason; they must not be silently omitted where they are necessary to establish boundaries.

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Canonical name | One unambiguous name, with controlled spelling and capitalization |
| Identifier | A stable identifier that does not depend only on display order |
| Domain | The established Alchemy domain to which the classification belongs |
| Definition | A precise statement of what the Alchemy is |
| Purpose | The intended outcome or responsibility |
| Subject | The entity, process, resource, relationship, capability, or state on which it operates |
| Scope | What the classification includes |
| Exclusions | What it does not include and where its boundaries lie |
| Operations | The activities or transformations it is responsible for |
| Inputs and conditions | Relevant inputs, resources, prerequisites, and operating conditions |
| Outputs and effects | Intended outputs and observable or assessable changes |
| Relationships | Its demonstrated relationships with other classifications |
| Dependencies | Required conditions, capabilities, or supporting classifications |
| Context applicability | The applicable entries from the 22-context register |
| Authority and boundaries | Applicable permissions, responsibilities, and constraints |
| Evidence | Sources, observations, tests, and limitations supporting its definition |
| Validation criteria | Conditions required to review and accept the classification |
| Status and version | Its lifecycle status, version, and change history |

## 7. Classification integrity rules

1. **Distinct identity:** Each classification must have a clear identity and a definition that distinguishes it from neighboring classifications.
2. **Domain coherence:** A classification must belong to a justified domain and must not be assigned by name alone.
3. **Purpose clarity:** Its purpose and responsibilities must be explicit and bounded.
4. **Non-duplication:** Similar wording is not sufficient reason to create multiple classifications. Genuine differences in subject, responsibility, operation, or outcome must be demonstrated.
5. **No forced symmetry:** C2O and UNIVERSALIS expressions must be aligned where a real correspondence exists, but identical wording or identical implementation must not be assumed.
6. **Context precision:** Each classification must identify only the contexts in which it applies. Applicability to all 22 contexts must never be assumed.
7. **Relationship traceability:** Dependencies, overlaps, intersections, and distinctions must be recorded explicitly and justified.
8. **Boundary preservation:** A classification must not acquire authority, responsibilities, or scope belonging to another context or component.
9. **Evidence discipline:** Proposals, interpretations, examples, simulations, tested behavior, and verified findings must remain distinguishable.
10. **Controlled evolution:** Changes must preserve traceability to prior definitions, decisions, evidence, and versions.

## 8. Relationship modelling

Relationships between Alchemy classifications must be explicit rather than inferred from similar names or shared context. Where applicable, the taxonomy may record:

- specialization or broader-domain relationships;
- composition or constituent relationships;
- intersection or shared-responsibility relationships;
- dependency or prerequisite relationships;
- support or enabling relationships;
- correspondence between C2O and UNIVERSALIS expressions;
- overlap, distinction, incompatibility, or exclusion.

A relationship label records a claim that must be supported by a definition or evidence. Listing a possible relationship type does not assert that a particular relationship exists.

## 9. C2O and UNIVERSALIS edition boundaries

The taxonomy must preserve the established distinction between C2O as the private-source edition and UNIVERSALIS as the public, open-source edition.

Where an Alchemy applies to both editions, the taxonomy may define a shared conceptual identity and then document the applicable edition-specific expressions. This must not collapse their governance, authority, source visibility, or operational boundaries.

Public-facing taxonomy material must not disclose private C2O implementation details, protected information, internal policies, credentials, or private operational evidence. A public classification must remain understandable and usable without requiring access to private C2O internals.

## 10. Review and acceptance requirements

A proposed Alchemy classification is not accepted solely because it has been named or documented. Before acceptance, it must be reviewed for:

- precision and internal consistency of its definition;
- a distinct, justified domain and purpose;
- complete scope, inclusions, and exclusions;
- non-duplication and compatibility with related classifications;
- accurate context applicability;
- explicit relationships and dependencies;
- preserved C2O and UNIVERSALIS boundaries;
- evidence quality, limitations, and traceability;
- consistency with the root definition and the rest of the taxonomy.

Any unresolved ambiguity, unsupported claim, contradiction, or material overlap must be recorded and resolved or explicitly retained as unresolved before acceptance.

## 12. Change control

Every change to this taxonomy must identify the affected definition, classification, context, relationship, or rule. Changes must retain a traceable record of what changed and why. The four repositories must maintain a consistent shared taxonomy baseline, while any future edition-specific additions must respect the C2O and UNIVERSALIS boundaries described above.
