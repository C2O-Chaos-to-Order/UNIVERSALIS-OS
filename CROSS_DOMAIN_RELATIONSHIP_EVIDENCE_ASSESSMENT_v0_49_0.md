## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected subgroup examines airway microbiology in respiratory physical therapy, anatomical body-composition phenotypes in population epidemiology, and animal-model research in human dentistry.

The assessment distinguishes three scopes:

- **Microbiology ↔ Physical Therapy:** respiratory pathogens, sputum microbiology, airway infection, and pathogen surveillance in relation to physiotherapy-led airway clearance and pulmonary rehabilitation for selected chronic airway diseases, particularly bronchiectasis and cystic fibrosis. Microbiological findings can inform infection assessment and antimicrobial treatment in the multidisciplinary care pathway, while respiratory physiotherapy addresses secretion clearance, exercise capacity, and functional outcomes. The evidence does not mean microbiology results determine a specific airway-clearance technique, or that physical therapy treats infection or replaces antimicrobial therapy.
- **Anatomy ↔ Epidemiology:** structural body compartments and anatomical phenotypes—including visceral and subcutaneous adipose tissue, liver and pancreas fat, skeletal muscle, and intramuscular fat—measured in population cohorts and analyzed in relation to age, disease incidence, and cardiometabolic outcomes. The interface is population-level imaging and cohort analysis, not routine individual clinical assessment or a recommendation for universal MRI.
- **Zoology ↔ Dentistry:** zoological and species-specific characteristics of laboratory animals used in peri-implant disease and tooth-regeneration research. Recent systematic reviews demonstrate extensive animal-model use, but the research is framed chiefly through dental science, biomaterials, experimental pathology, and tissue engineering. The available evidence does not establish that the full discipline of Zoology makes a sufficiently distinct and sustained contribution to human Dentistry beyond species selection and animal-model knowledge. This pair is not promoted.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not establish that no narrower future relationship could ever be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.
