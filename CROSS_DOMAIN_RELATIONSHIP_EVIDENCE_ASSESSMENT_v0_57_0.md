## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected subgroup examines (1) the translation of life-science discoveries into clinical medicine, (2) biological knowledge applied to population health through the One Health framework, and (3) the broader umbrella pairing of Life Sciences and Health Sciences.

The assessment distinguishes:

- **Life Sciences ↔ Medicine:** the National Center for Advancing Translational Sciences (NCATS) describes a translational spectrum in which basic research investigates fundamental mechanisms of biology and disease; preclinical research connects basic disease science with human medicine using cell models, animal models, human or animal tissues, and other systems; clinical research relates findings from models to human disease and tests interventions in people; and clinical implementation evaluates adoption into routine care. Translational research therefore creates a defined interface through which life-science knowledge can inform medical diagnosis, therapeutic development, safety evaluation, and clinical practice, while clinical observations and unmet needs can direct new biological research. A 2025 review likewise describes multidisciplinary translation from laboratory discovery through clinical practice and ultimately community- and population-level health benefits. The candidate is bounded to disease-related biological discovery and its evaluation and application in clinical medicine; it does not imply that every life-science finding is medically actionable or that all medical decisions require new laboratory discovery. [S525–S527]
- **Biology ↔ Public Health:** WHO's 2026 One Health fact sheet describes interdependent human, animal, plant, and ecosystem health and the need for cross-sectoral collaboration. WHO reports that more than 60% of emerging infectious diseases reported globally come from animals, wild and domestic. The Quadripartite One Health Joint Plan of Action coordinates action across human, animal, plant, and environmental health; its implementation period was extended in August 2026 through 2029. The One Health High-Level Expert Panel's September 2026 report identifies implementation enablers and barriers across governance, finance, data, community engagement, and workforce capacity. This demonstrates a distinct applied interface in which biological knowledge of organisms, hosts, pathogens, vectors, reservoirs, and plants informs population surveillance, prevention, preparedness, and response. The scope is bounded to living-system and organismal evidence used in integrated population-health work; it does not claim that Biology alone provides the ecological, veterinary, environmental, social, or public-health methods required by One Health. [S528–S532]
- **Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences:** the source base demonstrates important interfaces between basic biological discovery and clinical medicine, and between biological systems and population health. However, the two full subject scopes are extremely broad, and the evidence reviewed does not identify one additional, stable, pair-specific mechanism or method that is distinct from the narrower interfaces already represented in this programme. The umbrella phrase risks restating the combined domain coverage rather than defining a usable relationship with its own boundary, direction, or evidence test. This pair is therefore not promoted at the full canonical subject scope assessed here.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not prove that no narrower future interface could be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.

This subgroup screens two pairs from the first two groups and one umbrella pair from the first group. The remaining inventory after this pass contains 43 pairs.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- **Life Sciences ↔ Medicine:** LSHS-225 is the broad life-science discovery / preclinical research / clinical medicine translation interface. Previously assessed Genetics ↔ Medicine (LSHS-017), Molecular Biology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-018), Cell Biology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-019), Physiology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-001), and Biotechnology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-024) concern their particular specialist disciplines or technologies. They do not eliminate the distinct cross-cutting translation relationship at the broader Life Sciences level.
- **Biology ↔ Public Health:** LSHS-226 addresses biological knowledge across organisms and living systems as an input to integrated One Health surveillance, prevention, and response. Ecology ↔ Public Health (LSHS-007) concerns ecological determinants and ecosystem-health relations; Microbiology ↔ Public Health (LSHS-040) concerns public-health microbiology and laboratory-supported outbreak detection; Zoology ↔ Public Health and other animal-related pairs have their own defined scopes. The current candidate is broader biological-sciences input across animal, plant, host, and pathogen systems, not a replacement for those narrower interfaces.
- **Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences:** LSHS-227 is not promoted. The broad relationship is represented through more specific interfaces and does not create a new independently bounded relationship merely by combining all life-science and health-science domains.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-225 is reciprocal across translational research: life-science discovery and experimental evidence inform medical research and clinical evaluation; clinical questions, observations, and unmet needs guide further life-science research.
- LSHS-226 is reciprocal across One Health and public health: biological findings inform surveillance and prevention; population-health threats and monitoring priorities guide biological research and investigation.
- LSHS-227 has no promoted direction or subtype. The full umbrella pair is not sufficiently specific to define an additional relationship beyond narrower documented interfaces.

Important boundaries:

- Translational research is not a guaranteed or linear route from discovery to treatment. Basic and preclinical findings require appropriate human evaluation, evidence of safety and effectiveness, and clinical implementation assessment.
- One Health is multidisciplinary. Biology provides essential organismal and living-system knowledge, but successful One Health action also requires public-health, veterinary, ecological, environmental, social, governance, data, and community capacities.
- The WHO statistic that more than 60% of emerging infectious diseases reported globally come from animals does not mean that more than 60% of all human diseases are zoonotic, nor that every emerging disease has an animal origin.
- The Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences umbrella pair is not promoted simply because the two broad domains are connected. A distinct candidate requires an independently defined boundary and evidence test.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. This assessment does not infer clinical utility from basic discovery alone, reduce One Health to Biology and Public Health alone, or promote an unbounded umbrella pairing.
