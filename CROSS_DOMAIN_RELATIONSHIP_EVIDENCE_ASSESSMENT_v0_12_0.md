## 2. Method and important terminology boundaries

Each pair is assessed for evidence of a specific interface between the two canonical subject scopes. The assessment is deliberately strict about disciplinary identity:

- **Zoology is not synonymous with animal-assisted therapy.** An animal being present in a therapeutic intervention does not, by itself, establish a Zoology relationship. A candidate requires an identifiable interface involving animal species, movement, behaviour, welfare, or human–animal interaction in a structured therapeutic plan.
- **Botany is not synonymous with Horticulture.** Horticultural therapy and gardening may use plants as materials or occupations, but this does not automatically demonstrate that botanical science contributes to the intervention or rehabilitation process.
- **Ecology is not synonymous with “environment” or a social-ecological model.** Natural settings and environmental context can matter to occupational participation and rehabilitation, but a relationship with the canonical Life Sciences subject Ecology requires a demonstrated contribution from ecological science—such as ecosystem processes, species interactions, or ecological conditions—not merely the existence of an outdoor setting or a generic environmental factor.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A non-promotion records that the available evidence did not establish a sufficiently specific subject-pair relationship in this pass; it is not a finding that the subjects are unrelated.

## 4. Duplicate and scope cross-check

The nine pairs were compared against assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.11.0. None is an exact duplicate of a previously screened unordered pair.

Important adjacent relationships and distinctions:
- Zoology ↔ Occupational Therapy and Zoology ↔ Physical Therapy are distinct from Zoology ↔ Rehabilitation Science. The first two concern professional use of animals within OT and PT plans of care; the third concerns the broader research and evaluation of animal-assisted interventions in rehabilitation.
- Hippotherapy is a tool used within a licensed PT or OT plan of care, not a stand-alone service or a separate profession. The report therefore does not call it “equine therapy” as an independent clinical discipline.
- Botany ↔ OT, PT, or Rehabilitation Science is not accepted merely because gardening and horticultural therapy use plants. Horticulture is an adjacent field and practice; direct botanical-science contribution remains unestablished in these sources.
- Ecology ↔ OT, PT, or Rehabilitation Science is not accepted merely because natural settings and environmental factors affect health and participation. Ecological science must be distinguished from environmental context, ecological dynamics, and social-ecological models.
