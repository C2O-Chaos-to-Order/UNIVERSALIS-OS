## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed Immunology interfaces selected from the reconciled inventory of 130 remaining pairs:

- Immunology ↔ Mental Health: immune dysregulation, inflammatory and neuroimmune mechanisms, immunopsychiatry research, and proposed immune-informed stratification in selected psychiatric disorders.
- Immunology ↔ Physical Therapy: immune-cell responses to exercise and therapeutic loading, immunomodulation associated with repeated exercise, and immune contributions to skeletal-muscle repair and rehabilitation research.
- Immunology ↔ Occupational Therapy: occupational-therapy services for people with immune-mediated inflammatory conditions, particularly rheumatoid arthritis, and whether immunological knowledge constitutes a distinct input into occupational-therapy assessment or intervention.

The three assessments deliberately separate biological association, mechanism, clinical rehabilitation, and professional practice:

- Immune alterations reported in some psychiatric cohorts do not establish a causal immune mechanism in every patient, an immune-defined diagnosis, or routine clinical utility for immunotherapy.
- Exercise can modulate immune-cell trafficking and function, but the magnitude and direction of response depend on intensity, duration, training status, age, health condition, and other context. General exercise-immunology findings are not automatically proof that a particular physical-therapy protocol produces a particular immune or clinical effect.
- Occupational therapy has a documented role in supporting function and participation for people with rheumatoid arthritis and other chronic inflammatory conditions. However, treatment of a person with an immune-mediated disease does not, by itself, establish a distinct subject-pair relationship with Immunology. This report asks whether immunological knowledge is shown to make a specific contribution to OT reasoning or intervention, not merely whether an OT client may have an immune-mediated diagnosis.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory was checked against the v1.13.1 canonical subject lists (18 Life Sciences subjects × 15 Health Sciences subjects = 270 possible pairs) and assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.28.0. The prior ledger contains 140 unique screened pairs with assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-140. Each selected pair was checked as an unordered subject pair against all 28 snapshots, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. None appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion records insufficient evidence for a sufficiently specific subject-pair recommendation in this assessment; it is not a finding that the subjects are unrelated. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

The three selected pairs were checked as unordered subject pairs against all assessment tables in v0.1.0 through v0.28.0. The prior ledger contains 140 unique screened pairs with sequential identifiers LSHS-001 through LSHS-140. Exact normalized-pair checks found no occurrence of Immunology ↔ Mental Health, Immunology ↔ Physical Therapy, or Immunology ↔ Occupational Therapy in any of the 28 prior snapshots. Canonical names and identifiers were checked against the v1.13.1 taxonomy.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- Immunology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-011) concerns clinical medical applications of immunological knowledge. It does not replace the specific immune–psychiatric research interface in LSHS-141.
- Immunology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-104) concerns immunological mechanisms and pharmacological action; Immunology ↔ Emergency Medicine (LSHS-035) concerns acute immune-mediated or allergic presentations; Immunology ↔ Preventive Medicine (LSHS-046) and Immunology ↔ Public Health (LSHS-047) concern prevention and population-health functions. These are not the same as immunopsychiatry research, exercise immunology in PT, or OT for inflammatory arthritis.
- Immunology ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-036) is a distinct previously assessed pair. Its scope concerns rehabilitation research and immune-mediated or immune-related functional questions at the rehabilitation-science level. LSHS-142 is specifically about clinical physical-therapy exercise/loading and the associated immune responses. The new PT pair does not duplicate the broader Rehabilitation Science pairing.
- Immunology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-054) concerns clinical immunology nursing and immune-related patient care. It does not subsume specialist PT exercise/loading or OT occupational-performance interventions for inflammatory arthritis.
- Neuroscience ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-005), Genetics ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-126), Molecular Biology ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-127), and Cell Biology ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-128) concern different neural, inherited, molecular, and cellular research interfaces. LSHS-141 focuses on immune processes and neuroimmune hypotheses in selected psychiatric populations. It does not assert that Immunology replaces Neuroscience or that mental disorders are generally autoimmune.
- Immunology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-106) concerns nutritional and immune-system interactions. It is distinct from the present exercise-induced immune modulation and therapeutic rehabilitation scope.
- The OT evidence for rheumatoid arthritis is not treated as sufficient merely because RA is immune-mediated. The current guidelines support OT for function, self-management, daily activities, and participation; the source material does not demonstrate that Immunology itself supplies a distinctive assessment or treatment method for OT. This boundary is the basis for the LSHS-143 non-promotion.

Direction is reciprocal within the two bounded candidate interfaces:

- LSHS-141: Immunology contributes methods and knowledge for characterizing immune dysregulation, inflammatory mechanisms, and immune-associated subgroups; Mental Health contributes defined psychiatric phenotypes, clinical context, and patient-relevant outcomes. Clinical observations can refine immunological research, and immune findings can guide cautious subgroup research.
- LSHS-142: Immunology characterizes immune responses to exercise, loading, and tissue repair; Physical Therapy defines and applies therapeutic exercise and rehabilitation loading and evaluates function. Rehabilitation observations can guide immunological research, while mechanistic immune findings can inform questions about exercise dose and tissue response.

No direction or subtype is accepted for LSHS-143. None of the three assessments is a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim.
