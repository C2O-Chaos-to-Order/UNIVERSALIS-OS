## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed Virology interfaces selected from the reconciled inventory of 118 remaining pairs:

- Virology ↔ Physical Therapy: physical-function assessment and symptom-responsive rehabilitation for selected post-viral conditions, with the evidence base here focused on post-COVID-19 condition.
- Virology ↔ Occupational Therapy: assessment and support for daily activity, occupational performance, cognitive and fatigue-related limitations, energy management, environmental adaptation, and return-to-work participation for people experiencing long COVID.
- Virology ↔ Rehabilitation Science: research on disability, rehabilitation interventions, outcomes, models of care, and safe service delivery across COVID-19 and long-COVID illness trajectories.

The selected subgroup examines post-infectious rehabilitation after SARS-CoV-2 infection. It does not imply that long COVID is necessarily caused by persistent viral replication, that the underlying mechanisms are fully established, or that all virological infections produce the same rehabilitation needs. WHO's clinical case definition describes post-COVID-19 condition in people with a history of probable or confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection, usually three months after onset, with symptoms lasting at least two months and not explained by an alternative diagnosis; symptoms may fluctuate or relapse. CDC guidance notes that no laboratory test can definitively diagnose or rule out long COVID. Accordingly, these pair assessments concern the documented clinical and research interface between viral infection histories and post-infectious function, not a claim of ongoing active viral infection in every affected person. [S346–S347]

The evidence is direct for the post-COVID-19 setting. Physical Therapy is concerned here with assessment and management of physical function, respiratory symptoms, exercise tolerance, orthostatic intolerance, and individualized physical rehabilitation. Occupational Therapy is concerned with activities of daily living, cognitive and fatigue-related occupational performance, task and environmental adaptation, education, and participation in work and meaningful occupations. Rehabilitation Science is concerned with the generation and evaluation of evidence about disability, interventions, outcomes, service models, implementation, and access. These are related but distinct functions; Rehabilitation Science is not simply another name for either professional practice.

A critical safety boundary is preserved. Post-exertional malaise or post-exertional symptom exacerbation can follow even minor physical or cognitive activity. Current rehabilitation literature and guidance do not support a one-size-fits-all exercise prescription. People with post-exertional symptom exacerbation may require pacing, energy conservation, symptom-contingent activity management, and avoidance of fixed incremental exercise loading; exercise-based training is not universally appropriate. Evidence for some rehabilitation outcomes remains low or very low certainty. [S348–S351]

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory contains 18 Life Sciences subjects and 15 Health Sciences subjects, producing 270 possible cross-domain pairs. The preceding assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.32.0 record 152 unique screened pairs, 138 candidate recommendations, and 14 screened but not promoted or deferred, leaving 118 individually unassessed pairs before this batch.

Each selected pair was checked as an unordered subject pair against all 32 prior assessment snapshots, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. The historical assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-152 were reconciled. None of the three selected pairs appears in the prior ledger.
