## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed Anatomy interfaces selected from the reconciled inventory of 124 remaining pairs:

- Anatomy ↔ Nursing: structural knowledge used to orient systematic patient assessment, examination of body regions and systems, palpation, recognition of anatomical landmarks, and safe nursing procedures.
- Anatomy ↔ Emergency Medicine: anatomical localization used in acute trauma examination, recognition of injury to specific body regions, airway and cervical-spine assessment, and selection or interpretation of diagnostic imaging and urgent interventions.
- Anatomy ↔ Dentistry: specialized knowledge of dental, oral, maxillofacial, craniofacial, and head-and-neck structures used in examination, imaging, diagnosis, treatment planning, and dental procedures.

These are distinct clinical applications of anatomical knowledge. Anatomy studies the structures and spatial organization of the body. Nursing applies structural knowledge within holistic, ongoing patient assessment and care. Emergency Medicine applies anatomical localization to rapid evaluation and stabilization of acutely ill or injured patients. Dentistry applies specialized anatomical knowledge to dentition and the oral and craniofacial structures within its scope.

The evidence is strongest for the acute trauma and dental interfaces. The nursing evidence supports a foundational relationship, but nursing education and clinical practice integrate anatomy with physiology and other biosciences; anatomy alone does not encompass nursing assessment or determine nursing decisions. Anatomy ↔ Nursing is therefore graded moderate–high rather than treated as an exclusive or deterministic relationship.

The recently assessed Physiology ↔ Nursing relationship (LSHS-144) is not duplicated. Physiology ↔ Nursing concerns normal and abnormal function, homeostasis, and interpretation of physiological measures. Anatomy ↔ Nursing concerns structures, spatial relationships, body regions, landmarks, and the structural basis for examination. The two bodies of knowledge complement each other but are not interchangeable.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory was checked against the v1.13.8 canonical subject lists (18 Life Sciences subjects × 15 Health Sciences subjects = 270 possible pairs) and assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.30.0. The prior ledger contains 146 unique screened pairs with assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-146. Each selected pair was checked as an unordered subject pair against all 30 snapshots, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. None appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

The three selected pairs were checked as unordered subject pairs against all assessment tables in v0.1.0 through v0.30.0. The prior ledger contains 146 unique screened pairs with sequential identifiers LSHS-001 through LSHS-146. Exact normalized-pair checks found no occurrence of Anatomy ↔ Nursing, Anatomy ↔ Emergency Medicine, or Anatomy ↔ Dentistry in any of the 30 prior snapshots. Canonical names and identifiers were checked against the v1.13.8 taxonomy.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- Anatomy ↔ Medicine (LSHS-002) concerns the broad medical application of anatomical knowledge. It does not duplicate the Nursing-specific assessment interface in LSHS-147, the acute trauma and urgent structural assessment interface in LSHS-148, or the specialized oral and craniofacial interface in LSHS-149.
- Anatomy ↔ Occupational Therapy (LSHS-086), Anatomy ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-087), Anatomy ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-088), Anatomy ↔ Surgery (LSHS-103), and Anatomy ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-116) concern other professional and clinical applications. None is the exact Nursing, Emergency Medicine, or Dentistry pair assessed here.
- Physiology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-144) concerns function, physiological measurements, and patient response. LSHS-147 concerns structural knowledge, anatomical location, and regional/system examination. The evidence bases overlap in nursing bioscience education but the subject matter and proposed functions are distinct.
- Physiology ↔ Emergency Medicine (LSHS-037), Physiology ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-038), Physiology ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-039), and Physiology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-102) remain distinct from the anatomy-specific trauma and structural localization interface in LSHS-148.
- Cell Biology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-096), Molecular Biology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-098), Mycology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-048), Microbiology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-052), and Immunology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-053) concern cellular, molecular, fungal, microbial, or immune interfaces with dental health. LSHS-149 concerns the anatomical structures and spatial relationships relevant to dental practice.
- Anatomy ↔ Dentistry and Anatomy ↔ Surgery are not interchangeable. LSHS-149 concerns the full dental subject's specialized anatomy needs across examination, imaging, diagnosis, preventive and restorative planning, and procedures; LSHS-103 concerns the broader surgical discipline.
- The trauma sources support LSHS-148 and include emergency team members from several professions. Their use here is limited to the Emergency Medicine subject's acute assessment and intervention interface, not a claim that all trauma care is exclusively performed by emergency physicians or that Anatomy alone supplies all trauma knowledge.

Direction is reciprocal within each bounded interface:

- LSHS-147: Anatomy supplies structural organization and landmarks used in examination; Nursing applies this knowledge during holistic patient assessment, documentation, and care. Nursing observations can prompt more focused anatomical questions.
- LSHS-148: Anatomy supplies structural maps for injury localization, regional examination, and airway/cervical-spine understanding; Emergency Medicine applies this knowledge in acute evaluation, stabilization, imaging decisions, and procedures. Emergency findings can guide focused anatomical investigation.
- LSHS-149: Anatomy supplies detailed dental, oral, and craniofacial structures and spatial relationships; Dentistry applies and refines that knowledge for assessment, imaging, diagnosis, treatment planning, and procedures. Dental practice can identify specialized anatomical structures and terminology requiring further anatomical study.

None of these proposals is a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. Anatomical knowledge complements nursing, emergency, and dental methods rather than replacing their professional knowledge, clinical reasoning, imaging, or patient-centred care.
