This assessment completes the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences screening programme by examining the only remaining pair in the v0.71.0 regenerated inventory:

The assessment uses exact unordered subject-pair matching against assessment-table rows in all 71 preceding snapshots. A candidate RD-P requires more than general relevance: it must identify a contribution from each subject, a stable cross-domain research or practice boundary, and an interface that is not merely a restatement of one already-assessed specialist pair. The root-level candidate must also explain what the integrated interface contributes beyond the narrower specialty pairings without implying that every Health Sciences activity depends on Mycology.

The proposed interface is bounded around fungal knowledge entering health-science research and systems through several connected pathways:

- fungal taxonomy, diversity, biology, ecology, pathogenesis, and host interactions;
- diagnostic identification, laboratory capacity, antifungal susceptibility, and resistance surveillance;
- clinical and population research concerning fungal disease, high-risk groups, and disease burden;
- antifungal discovery, development, treatment evaluation, and stewardship;
- healthcare-associated outbreak investigation, environmental exposure assessment, and One Health coordination; and
- fungal-related nutrition, disability, and functional outcomes where supported by the relevant specialist evidence.

Mycology contributes the organism-level knowledge, classification, ecological and biological interpretation, diagnostic methods, and susceptibility or resistance evidence. Health Sciences contributes clinical and population-health questions, patient and disease contexts, diagnostic and care pathways, prevention priorities, laboratory and surveillance systems, treatment and outcome evaluation, health policy, and service implementation.

Research relevance is not clinical validation. A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. It does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, verification, or owner acceptance. No canonical change is made by this report.

### LSHS-270 — Mycology ↔ Health Sciences

The evidence establishes a sustained and multidisciplinary interface between Mycology and Health Sciences. The World Health Organization's 2026 Blueprint for strengthening responses to fungal disease and antifungal resistance organizes action across public-health and health-system readiness, therapies and diagnostics, laboratory systems and surveillance, outbreak preparedness, and social and environmental drivers. This provides an explicit global health-system framework rather than a single clinical-specialty application. The WHO Fungal Priority Pathogens List and its earlier diagnostic and treatment landscape work identify unmet research, diagnostic, therapeutic, surveillance, and access needs. Those sources were already registered in the programme and are reused under their original source IDs. [Previously registered S246–S247 and related WHO diagnostic sources]

New evidence strengthens the cross-cutting case. CDC's July 2026 national surveillance report for *Candida auris* describes 13,507 clinical cases and 27,853 screening cases reported for specimens collected during 2022–2024, with case counts increasing over that period. The report links species identification and screening with infection prevention, laboratory capacity, susceptibility testing, and public-health response. The CDC page records an erratum correcting the New York State Department of Health surveillance-team member names; the erratum does not revise the reported case counts or findings used here. This remains one pathogen-specific surveillance example, not an estimate for all fungal disease. [S663]

A 2026 review of invasive fungal diagnostics describes movement beyond culture-only approaches toward molecular methods, biomarkers, and point-of-care testing, while identifying cost, access, turnaround time, and standardization constraints. A 2026 review of the antifungal pipeline documents approved therapies and investigational compounds at different development stages. A further review published in November 2026 discusses emerging antifungal and host-directed treatment approaches. These sources connect Mycology's identification, pathogenesis, susceptibility, and resistance knowledge with Health Sciences' diagnostic, pharmacological, clinical-trial, regulatory, and treatment-evaluation systems. They do not establish that investigational products are approved or effective, or that one treatment strategy applies across fungal species and indications. [S664–S666]

The root-level interface extends beyond clinical infection management. Recent reviews connect fungal ecology, environmental change, host-pathogen interactions, plant and animal health, food systems, and antifungal resistance within One Health and planetary-health research. CDC's 2026 guidance for healthcare-associated mold outbreaks links organism identification and laboratory evidence with environmental investigation, facility mitigation, infection prevention, and health-department coordination. WHO's 2025 reports on fungal tests and treatments identify gaps in diagnostic access and therapeutic research, especially in settings without well-equipped laboratories. These connections support a shared cross-domain research and response boundary spanning clinical, laboratory, public-health, environmental, and health-system work. Environmental fungal detection alone does not prove the source of a patient's infection, and ecological or in-vitro findings do not by themselves establish human disease causation. [S667–S671]

Those records address narrower clinical, diagnostic, professional, epidemiological, therapeutic, nutritional, or functional settings. LSHS-270 concerns the common fungal-science contribution across several Health Sciences settings, including the connected chain of organism identification, biological interpretation, diagnostics, resistance evidence, treatment and prevention evaluation, surveillance, outbreak response, and health-system coordination. It is not merely a new name for any one of those specialty pairings.

The earlier **Mycology ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-158)** assessment remains **Do not promote**. Its report found that neuropsychiatric manifestations in severe fungal central-nervous-system infection did not establish a general disciplinary relationship with Mental Health. LSHS-270 does not reverse that decision or imply that fungal infection generally constitutes a mental-health condition. Likewise, prior specialty-specific candidate dispositions remain unchanged and are not counted again.

**Candidate RD-P:** fungal taxonomy, systematics, diversity, morphology, physiology, ecology, and evolution; fungal-host interactions, pathogenesis, and disease biology; fungal identification, microscopy, culture, molecular detection, biomarkers, and diagnostic interpretation; antifungal susceptibility, drug resistance, and fungal response to environmental and therapeutic pressures; fungi relevant to human, animal, plant, food, and built environments / cross-disciplinary Health Sciences research and systems involving fungal disease investigation, diagnostic laboratory capacity, clinical and population surveillance, antifungal and host-directed therapeutic research, treatment and stewardship evaluation, infection prevention, healthcare-associated outbreak investigation, environmental and occupational exposure assessment, One Health coordination, and health-system readiness. Mycology contributes species- and organism-level knowledge, biological and ecological mechanisms, identification and characterization methods, and interpretation of susceptibility and resistance; Health Sciences contributes patient and population contexts, diagnostic and care pathways, clinical and public-health questions, therapeutic and preventive evaluation, laboratory and surveillance infrastructure, policy, and health outcomes. The relationship is reciprocal: health-science questions and surveillance needs direct mycological investigation, while validated mycological evidence informs selected diagnostic, clinical, preventive, population, and health-system research and action. This does not imply that all fungi are pathogens, all fungal exposure causes disease, all positive detections prove invasive infection, environmental detection alone identifies a clinical source, or emerging diagnostics and therapeutics are universally validated or accessible.**
