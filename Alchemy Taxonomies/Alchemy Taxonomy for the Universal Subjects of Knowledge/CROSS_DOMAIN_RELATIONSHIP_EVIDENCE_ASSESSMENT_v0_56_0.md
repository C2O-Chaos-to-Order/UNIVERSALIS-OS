## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. This topic-focused subgroup assesses the three specialist-to-specialist pairs identified in v0.55.0 as the remaining Occupational Therapy combinations: Microbiology ↔ Occupational Therapy, Molecular Biology ↔ Occupational Therapy, and Cell Biology ↔ Occupational Therapy.

The assessment distinguishes:

- **Microbiology ↔ Occupational Therapy:** occupational-therapy professional standards explicitly require infection-prevention and control practices to reduce microorganism transmission. They address hand hygiene, personal protective equipment, point-of-care risk assessment, and cleaning, disinfection, and sterilization of practice environments, devices, and equipment. A prospective study in nursing-home rehabilitation gyms sampled equipment and personnel hands, explicitly including physical or occupational therapy personnel, and used microbiological culture to identify multidrug-resistant organisms (MDROs). A 2026 study examined adhesion of antibiotic-resistant bacteria to patient-applied thermoplastic medical devices and included Occupational Therapy and Biomedical Sciences affiliations. Together, these sources establish a practical microbiology interface for understanding microbial contamination, persistence, transmission, and resistance in occupational-therapy environments and patient-contact equipment. The relationship is bounded to infection prevention, equipment and device safety, and microbiological risk assessment; it does not imply that occupational therapists independently perform microbiological diagnostics or prescribe antimicrobial treatment.
- **Molecular Biology ↔ Occupational Therapy:** occupational-therapy research has measured molecular biomarkers of inflammation, oxidative stress, and neuroplasticity alongside occupation-related or occupational-therapy interventions. A randomized trial involving 30 people with chronic stroke compared virtual-reality motor-control training with conventional occupational therapy and assessed serum IL-6, ICAM-1, HO-1, 8-OHdG, and BDNF alongside functional outcomes. A systematic review of rehabilitation trials identified 119 blood and cerebrospinal-fluid biomarkers across 50 randomized trials, providing wider rehabilitation-research context but not OT-specific evidence. An occupational-therapy journal article also discusses genomics and regenerative rehabilitation as emerging areas for OT research and implementation. This supports an emerging research interface for molecular measurement and interpretation in OT-related functional-recovery research. It does not establish routine molecular testing in OT, clinical validity of individual biomarkers, or treatment decisions based solely on molecular measurements. The candidate scope is molecular processes and biomarker research, not inherited variant interpretation as such; that is distinct from the previously assessed Genetics ↔ Occupational Therapy pair.
- **Cell Biology ↔ Occupational Therapy:** OT literature discusses sensory-driven neuroplasticity, and occupation-based intervention studies have reported neurophysiological changes. However, the available OT-specific evidence reviewed here does not establish a sufficiently distinct cellular-biology contribution. The biomarker trial cited for Molecular Biology measures circulating molecular markers; it does not demonstrate a specific cell-level pathway caused by OT. A small occupation-based intervention report measured corticomotor reorganisation using transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), which is a neurophysiological measure rather than direct cellular analysis. A review of sensory-driven neuroplasticity in OT also cautions that direct transfer from basic-science evidence to sensory-integration treatment assumptions is not straightforward. These sources establish adjacent molecular and neuroscience questions, but not a stable Cell Biology ↔ Occupational Therapy disciplinary relationship at the scope assessed. This pair is not promoted.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not prove that no narrower future interface could be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-222 is reciprocal in applied infection prevention: Microbiology supplies knowledge of pathogens and contamination; OT service environments, equipment, and workflows provide the applied context and identify infection-prevention questions.
- LSHS-223 is reciprocal in translational research: Molecular Biology supplies molecular measurement and response interpretation; Occupational Therapy supplies intervention, function, participation, and recovery contexts.
- LSHS-224 has no promoted direction or subtype. Biomarker measurement and neurophysiological change do not, without direct cell-level evidence, establish a distinct Cell Biology contribution.

Important boundaries:

- Infection-prevention responsibilities in OT are applied professional responsibilities. They do not imply that occupational therapists are microbiology laboratory specialists or prescribers.
- Rehabilitation-gym contamination evidence sampled both physical- and occupational-therapy personnel, but not every equipment result can be attributed only to OT. The small transfer substudy does not establish rates of patient infection.
- Patient-applied thermoplastic-device bacterial adhesion research supports device microbiology but does not establish that every device is an OT orthosis or that one decontamination approach is universally effective.
- Molecular biomarker research in OT is emerging. Biomarkers measured in a small study are not automatically validated clinical endpoints or individual treatment-selection tools.
- Cell Biology requires a distinct cell-level contribution; a blood biomarker, neurophysiological measurement, or general reference to neuroplasticity is not alone sufficient.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. This assessment does not infer patient infection from contamination, clinical benefit from biomarker change, or cell-level mechanisms from systems-neuroscience observations.
