## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected subgroup evaluates the proposed general Biology interfaces with Medicine, Nursing, and Surgery. The central test is whether the broad Biology subject contributes a distinct, identifiable scientific scope beyond previously screened specialist disciplines and general curriculum integration.

The assessment distinguishes:

- **Biology ↔ Medicine:** Medical education and clinical research clearly rely on biological knowledge, but the evidence reviewed does not establish an additional, independently bounded relationship between the full subject of Biology and Medicine. The National Center for Advancing Translational Sciences' framework already documents the broader Life Sciences ↔ Medicine translation pathway (LSHS-225), from basic biological and disease-mechanism research through preclinical studies, clinical trials, implementation, and public-health research. Medical curricula then organize distinct scientific contributions—such as molecular biology, cell biology, physiology, pathology, pharmacology, and genetics—into integrated scientific foundations for clinical practice. The current Yale Scientific Foundations curriculum illustrates this multi-disciplinary organization. The exact narrower pairings have been screened separately in this programme. A general statement that medicine uses biological knowledge, or that biology is taught in medical education, would duplicate those more specific and the prior broader Life Sciences ↔ Medicine interface rather than define a separate Biology-specific relationship. This pair is not promoted at the full canonical scope assessed.
- **Biology ↔ Nursing:** Nursing education and practice literature supports the importance of bioscience knowledge, but the empirical evidence for its use as a distinct general Biology contribution to qualified nurses' clinical decision-making remains limited. A 2025 systematic scoping review of qualified nurses' bioscience knowledge included 15 papers and found mixed views about its usefulness in patient care, with a dearth of convincing evidence that bioscience knowledge is being used to direct clinical decision-making. A separate 2025 systematic review of undergraduate bioscience education included 98 studies from 28 countries and found improvements in short-term learning and student satisfaction, while recommending further research on long-term impact and the theory-to-practice connection. These studies support bioscience education, but curriculum and foundational knowledge alone do not establish a distinct operational relationship between the full Biology discipline and Nursing. The specific applied interfaces with Microbiology, Immunology, Cell Biology, Physiology, and Anatomy have already been assessed in this programme. This broad pair is not promoted.
- **Biology ↔ Surgery:** Comparative and translational wound-healing research provides a more specific interface. A 2026 systematic review synthesized 129 animal-model studies of skin wound healing and proposes a staged translational model: genetically tractable mice for mechanism discovery, rats for longitudinal screening, and rabbits or porcine models for validation of scar phenotype and epithelial closure before clinical trials. A 2026 review of adult mammalian regeneration examines tissue-specific regenerative capacity across species, including deer antlers, bat skin, and African spiny mice, and discusses implications for future therapies. A November 2026 review describes inflammatory repair mechanisms conserved from Drosophila to mammals, linking injury responses, immune-cell transitions, macrophage–fibroblast interactions, stem-cell activation, regeneration, and fibrosis. Surgical and reconstructive literature identifies the unresolved clinical challenges to which this biology is relevant, including burns, chronic wounds, infections, scarring, tissue loss, and repair. Together, these sources support a bounded Biology ↔ Surgery research interface focused on comparative and organism-level tissue repair, regenerative capacity, wound-healing models, and translational validation for surgical and reconstructive practice. The evidence is primarily review and preclinical-model evidence; it does not establish that a particular biological strategy improves surgical outcomes in patients. This scope is distinct from comparative animal anatomy and xenotransplant research (Zoology ↔ Surgery, LSHS-186), and from a general cellular-mechanism pairing (Cell Biology ↔ Surgery, LSHS-016): the candidate concerns integrated tissue and organism-level repair biology and the selection and translation of model systems, not only anatomical similarity or the involvement of individual cells.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not prove that no narrower future interface could be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

All 57 preceding assessment snapshots, v0.1.0 through v0.57.0, were reconciled against this subgroup. The preceding ledger contains 227 unique assessment IDs through LSHS-227. Early pilot restatements and later report snapshots were not counted as new assessments when IDs repeated. The v0.57.0 report explicitly lists Biology ↔ Medicine, Biology ↔ Nursing, and Biology ↔ Surgery among the still-unassessed pairs. None of the three selected pairs appears as an exact previously assessed pair. Canonical subject names and identifiers were checked against taxonomy v1.13.8.

The v0.57.0 reconciled remaining inventory contained 43 pairs: 13 Life Sciences ↔ specific Health Sciences pairings, 14 Biology ↔ Health Sciences pairings, and 16 specialist Life Sciences ↔ the Health Sciences root. This batch screens three of the 14 Biology pairings; 40 pairs remain.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- **Life Sciences and Biology with Medicine:** Life Sciences ↔ Medicine (LSHS-225) addresses the broad discovery-to-clinical translation spectrum. Genetics ↔ Medicine (LSHS-017), Molecular Biology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-018), Cell Biology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-019), Physiology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-001), Immunology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-011), Microbiology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-009), Virology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-010), Mycology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-012), and Biotechnology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-024) assess narrower scientific or technology-specific interfaces. LSHS-228 does not establish a new independent Biology-specific relationship beyond these. It is not promoted.
- **Biology and specialist sciences with Nursing:** Microbiology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-013), Virology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-045), Immunology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-054), Cell Biology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-095), Physiology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-144), and Anatomy ↔ Nursing (LSHS-147) concern specific scientific contributions to nursing education and practice. LSHS-229 evaluates the general Biology umbrella and finds that bioscience education alone does not establish a distinct practice relationship. It is not promoted.
- **Biology with Surgery:** Cell Biology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-016) was not promoted because cellular processes are involved in many forms of tissue injury and repair, but the earlier evidence did not establish a sufficiently stable full-scope relationship. Zoology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-186) was promoted as a candidate based on comparative animal anatomy, organ-system similarities and differences, and translational surgical or xenotransplant models. LSHS-230 is distinct in its focus on integrated tissue- and organism-level repair biology, comparative regenerative capacity, wound-healing model validity, and translation into surgical/reconstructive wound research. It does not duplicate animal anatomy or xenotransplantation, and it does not merely restate that cells participate in wound healing.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-228 has no promoted direction or subtype. The broad Biology-to-Medicine relationship is represented through the broader Life Sciences ↔ Medicine translation proposal and narrower specialist pairings.
- LSHS-229 has no promoted direction or subtype. The general bioscience-to-nursing claim is too broad and insufficiently evidenced as a distinct relationship beyond specific specialist contributions and educational foundations.
- LSHS-230 is reciprocal within translational wound-healing research: comparative and organism-level biological findings inform model selection and research into tissue repair, regeneration, scarring and fibrosis; surgical/reconstructive challenges and clinical outcomes guide priorities for biological investigation and preclinical model evaluation.

Important boundaries:

- The Biology ↔ Medicine non-promotion is not a claim that biology is irrelevant to medicine. It reflects the absence of an additional Biology-specific interface distinct from the broad Life Sciences translation pathway and the numerous specialist pairings already assessed.
- Nursing education research supports the value of bioscience learning, but short-term knowledge gains and curriculum content are not equivalent to demonstrated clinical decision-making effects or a distinct full-scope Biology ↔ Nursing relationship.
- Animal wound models and comparative regeneration research are useful for hypothesis generation and preclinical evaluation, but no model perfectly reproduces human repair. Species-specific healing mechanisms, contraction versus re-epithelialization, age, sex, comorbidities, infection, and other variables affect translation.
- Biology ↔ Surgery is restricted to tissue repair, regenerative biology, wound-healing models, and surgical/reconstructive translation. It does not imply that all surgery is biological research, or that preclinical findings establish clinical effectiveness.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. This assessment does not treat medical curriculum integration as a new RD-P, equate nursing bioscience education with proven clinical utility, or convert preclinical tissue-regeneration evidence into established surgical treatment recommendations.
