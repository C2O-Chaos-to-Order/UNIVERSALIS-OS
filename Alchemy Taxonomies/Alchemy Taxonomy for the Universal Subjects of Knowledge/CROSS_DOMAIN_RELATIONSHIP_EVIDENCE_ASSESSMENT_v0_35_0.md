## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed Genetics interfaces selected from the reconciled inventory of 112 remaining pairs:

- Genetics ↔ Emergency Medicine: identification and acute management of selected inherited metabolic and other genetic disorders presenting with time-sensitive, potentially life-threatening decompensation.
- Genetics ↔ Occupational Therapy: genetically confirmed neuromuscular disease, particularly Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD), as a defined clinical context for occupational-performance, activities-of-daily-living, and participation assessment.
- Genetics ↔ Rehabilitation Science: research on rehabilitation approaches, functional outcomes, and changing disability trajectories in spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) and related genetic neuromuscular disorders, including the era of disease-modifying and gene-based treatments.

The three interfaces span acute diagnosis and management, profession-specific functional care, and rehabilitation research. They are not interchangeable. The acute-care evidence concerns inherited metabolic emergencies and rapid genomic diagnosis in selected critically ill children. The Occupational Therapy evidence concerns a defined genetic diagnosis and its functional consequences, with occupational therapists participating in multidisciplinary DMD care. The Rehabilitation Science evidence concerns the synthesis and evaluation of rehabilitation approaches and outcomes for children with SMA across changes in disease-modifying treatment.

The scope is deliberately bounded. It does not suggest that all emergency presentations require genomic testing, that a genetic diagnosis alone determines emergency treatment, that every person with a genetic disorder requires Occupational Therapy, or that the rehabilitation evidence for DMD and SMA generalizes to all genetic conditions. Rapid sequencing is not a substitute for urgent clinical assessment or biochemical investigations where those investigations are needed for immediate treatment. Variants of uncertain significance do not by themselves establish a diagnosis or justify high-risk treatment.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory contains 18 Life Sciences subjects and 15 Health Sciences subjects, producing 270 possible cross-domain pairs. The preceding assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.34.0 record 158 unique screened pairs, 143 candidate recommendations, and 15 screened but not promoted or deferred, leaving 112 individually unassessed pairs before this batch.

Each selected pair was checked as an unordered subject pair against all 34 prior assessment snapshots, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. The historical assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-158 were reconciled. None of the three selected pairs appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

The three selected pairs were checked as unordered subject pairs against all 34 prior assessment snapshots, v0.1.0 through v0.34.0. The reconciled prior ledger contains 158 unique screened pairs with assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-158. Exact normalized-pair checks found no occurrence of Genetics ↔ Emergency Medicine, Genetics ↔ Occupational Therapy, or Genetics ↔ Rehabilitation Science in the prior ledger. Canonical subject names and identifiers were checked against taxonomy v1.13.8.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- Genetics ↔ Medicine (LSHS-017) concerns a broad clinical-genetics and medical-diagnosis interface. LSHS-159 narrows the application to acute inherited metabolic emergencies and time-sensitive evaluation and stabilization.
- Genetics ↔ Nursing (LSHS-029), Genetics ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-020), Genetics ↔ Preventive Medicine (LSHS-022), Genetics ↔ Epidemiology (LSHS-091), Genetics ↔ Public Health (LSHS-092), Genetics ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-123), Genetics ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-126), and Genetics ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-132) concern other Health Sciences applications. None is an exact duplicate of the acute emergency, occupational-performance, or rehabilitation-research pair assessed here.
- Genetics ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-132) concerns physical-therapy applications of genetic information. LSHS-160 addresses the distinct Occupational Therapy focus on daily activities, occupational performance, assistive and environmental adaptation, and participation. It does not assume that OT and PT are interchangeable.
- Neuroscience ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-032), Immunology ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-036), Physiology ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-038), Anatomy ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-088), Zoology ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-077), Botany ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-080), Ecology ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-083), and Virology ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-155) concern other foundational, biological, or disease-context research interfaces. LSHS-161 specifically concerns inherited neuromuscular disease, genetic variation, gene-based treatments, and rehabilitation evidence in SMA.
- Genetics ↔ Emergency Medicine does not duplicate Genetics ↔ Medicine or Genetics ↔ Nursing. The current evidence concerns acute recognition and management of selected inherited metabolic crises, not the general application of clinical genetics or nursing practice.
- The DMD and SMA examples are distinct disorders and their evidence is not generalized to all inherited disorders. The DMD source supports an OT role in a disease-specific multidisciplinary care programme; the SMA source supports a rehabilitation-research relationship and also describes the limitations of current intervention evidence.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-159 is reciprocal within the bounded acute inherited-metabolic emergency interface: Genetics contributes etiological and molecular knowledge; Emergency Medicine recognizes, stabilizes, investigates, and manages the acute presentation. Emergency findings and phenotype can prompt genetic evaluation.
- LSHS-160 is reciprocal within the bounded DMD/neuromuscular-care interface: Genetics establishes the inherited diagnosis and relevant disease context; Occupational Therapy evaluates activities, performance, participation, and individual support needs. Functional findings can inform ongoing multidisciplinary care.
- LSHS-161 is reciprocal within the bounded research interface: Genetics contributes inherited-disease and phenotype knowledge; Rehabilitation Science evaluates disability, interventions, outcomes, and service approaches across disease and treatment trajectories. Rehabilitation research can identify questions about phenotype and functional outcomes for genetics-informed investigation.

None of these proposals is a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. The evidence does not imply that genetic information by itself determines emergency treatment, occupational performance, or individual rehabilitation response.
