## 1. Scope and safeguards

This assessment continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences screening programme. The selected subgroup is taken from the reconciled v0.65.0 remainder:

- Biology ↔ Preventive Medicine
- Biology ↔ Emergency Medicine
- Biology ↔ Rehabilitation Science

The assessment tests whether Biology, as a specific Life Sciences subject, contributes a distinct and independently testable method, research question, decision boundary, or output to each Health Sciences subject beyond (a) the already assessed broader Life Sciences relationship and (b) the existing specialist scientific pairings.

The canonical taxonomy identifies Biology as **USA-LS-002**, a subject under Life Sciences. It identifies Preventive Medicine as **USA-HS-013**, Emergency Medicine as **USA-HS-014**, and Rehabilitation Science as **USA-HS-015**, all under Health Sciences. These names and identifiers were checked against taxonomy v1.13.8.

A biological contribution can be substantial without establishing a new Biology-level RD-P. Biology is a broad subject, while several of the relevant research methods and clinical interfaces are already represented by named specialist subjects such as Genetics, Physiology, Immunology, Microbiology, Neuroscience, Anatomy, and Cell Biology. The parent-level Life Sciences relationships also already capture integrated biological evidence across multiple levels. The assessment therefore distinguishes evidence of scientific relevance from evidence of a new, independently bounded relationship.

The following safeguards apply:

- Relatedness does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, or containment.
- A broad curriculum foundation or use of biological knowledge does not by itself establish a new RD-P relationship.
- A prior Life Sciences-level candidate is a relevant broader record, not automatic proof that every child-subject pairing must be promoted.
- Research biomarkers, multi-omics, and biological models are not treated as routine clinical tools without validation and evidence of decision impact.
- A non-promotion applies to the pair's current broad scope and current evidence; it does not prohibit a narrower, future proposal if a distinct Biology-specific contribution is demonstrated.
- This report is supplemental assessment evidence only. It does not amend the taxonomy, add an owner-accepted relationship, or change any repository authority boundary.

## 4. Existing relationship and scope cross-check

The following previously assessed relationships were used to test whether the new Biology-level proposals would add a distinct boundary.

### Biology ↔ Preventive Medicine

- **Life Sciences ↔ Preventive Medicine (LSHS-241):** the broader candidate already covers integrated genomic, epigenetic, molecular, metabolic, microbial, immune, physiological, and multi-exposure research across the life course and its use in preventive-medicine risk assessment and prevention planning.
- **Biology ↔ Public Health (LSHS-226):** a distinct Biology-level candidate already addresses biological knowledge across organisms and living systems as an input to One Health surveillance, prevention, preparedness, and response. It is not the same as Preventive Medicine, but it reinforces the need to avoid treating all biological contributions to prevention as one undifferentiated edge.
- **Life Sciences ↔ Public Health (LSHS-234):** the broader candidate addresses molecular and multi-level biological evidence integrated into population surveillance, public-health decisions, interventions, and evaluation.
- Specialist Preventive Medicine pairs include Microbiology (LSHS-042), Immunology (LSHS-046), Zoology (LSHS-057), Ecology (LSHS-059), Marine Biology (LSHS-062), Botany (LSHS-072), Neuroscience (LSHS-131), Physiology (LSHS-185), Anatomy (LSHS-187), Cell Biology (LSHS-188), and Evolutionary Biology (LSHS-193). These records preserve specific scientific contributions rather than requiring a general Biology-level relationship.

### Biology ↔ Emergency Medicine

- **Life Sciences ↔ Emergency Medicine (LSHS-242):** the broader candidate already describes cross-level acute biological phenotyping and research into time-sensitive diagnostic and prognostic pathways, with explicit limitations around observational evidence, turnaround, representation, and workflow implementation.
- Specialist Emergency Medicine pairs include Neuroscience (LSHS-033), Immunology (LSHS-035), Physiology (LSHS-037), Microbiology (LSHS-041), Virology (LSHS-044), Mycology (LSHS-049), Zoology (LSHS-068), Botany (LSHS-070), Ecology (LSHS-073), Anatomy (LSHS-148), Genetics (LSHS-159), Marine Biology (LSHS-162), Molecular Biology (LSHS-164), Biotechnology (LSHS-207), and Evolutionary Biology (LSHS-217).
- The 2026 emergency sepsis review is treated as evidence for an emerging integrated biological research interface, not proof that transcriptomic or multi-omic approaches are validated for general emergency-department triage. The 2025 precision acute-care scoping review and the 2026 review were already discussed in LSHS-242; the prior source identifiers S582–S584 are retained rather than re-numbered.

### Biology ↔ Rehabilitation Science

- **Life Sciences ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-243):** the broader candidate already covers biological mechanisms, adaptations, translational models, intervention research, functional capacity, activities and participation, multimodal rehabilitation, and clinical/community translation.
- Specialist Rehabilitation Science records include Neuroscience (LSHS-032), Immunology (LSHS-036), Physiology (LSHS-038), and Zoology (LSHS-077), alongside Life Sciences ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-237) and Life Sciences ↔ Occupational Therapy (LSHS-238). The latter was not promoted in its earlier assessment because the available evidence did not establish a distinct full-Life-Sciences interface beyond the specialist relationships. The same scope logic applies to the proposed general Biology pair.
- The prior NIH and Cochrane sources registered in v0.63.0 as S585–S588 are retained for the LSHS-243 comparison. They are not assigned new source identifiers here.

No pair in this subgroup establishes a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. The three non-promotions preserve distinctions among the broad Life Sciences interfaces, specialist scientific pairings, and Biology as a canonical subject under Life Sciences.
