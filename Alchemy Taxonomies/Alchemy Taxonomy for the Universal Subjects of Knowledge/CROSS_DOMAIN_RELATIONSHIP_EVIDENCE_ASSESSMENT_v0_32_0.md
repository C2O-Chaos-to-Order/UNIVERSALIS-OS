## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed Virology interfaces selected from the reconciled inventory of 121 remaining pairs:

- Virology ↔ Dentistry: oral and perioral viral disease, recognition and diagnostic investigation of selected viral lesions, and virology-informed infection prevention in dental care.
- Virology ↔ Surgery: HIV-specific perioperative assessment and planning, including interpretation of viral load and immune status, antiretroviral treatment continuity, selected drug-interaction considerations, and infection-control obligations.
- Virology ↔ Nutrition: HIV-specific nutrition screening, assessment, diagnosis, and individualized nutrition support in relation to disease stage, symptoms, altered metabolism, body composition, comorbidities, and antiretroviral treatment.

The selected group examines Virology's interfaces with oral healthcare, perioperative care, and clinical nutrition. It does not imply that every dental, surgical, or nutrition activity is virological. The evidence is direct for defined clinical contexts: oral viral infection and dental infection prevention; HIV-specific surgical planning; and HIV-specific nutrition care. The Virology ↔ Nutrition relationship is graded moderate–high because the evidence is strong for HIV-related nutrition care but narrower than the full scope of either canonical subject.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory contains 18 Life Sciences subjects and 15 Health Sciences subjects, producing 270 possible cross-domain pairs. The preceding assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.31.0 record 149 unique screened pairs, 135 candidate recommendations, and 14 screened but not promoted or deferred, leaving 121 individually unassessed pairs before this batch.

Each selected pair was checked as an unordered subject pair against all 31 prior assessment snapshots, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. The historical assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-149 were reconciled. None of the three selected pairs appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

The three selected pairs were checked as unordered subject pairs against all 31 prior assessment snapshots, v0.1.0 through v0.31.0. The reconciled prior ledger contains 149 unique screened pairs with assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-149. Exact normalized-pair checks found no occurrence of Virology ↔ Dentistry, Virology ↔ Surgery, or Virology ↔ Nutrition in the prior ledger. Canonical subject names and identifiers were checked against taxonomy v1.13.8.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- Virology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-010) concerns a broad clinical virology and medical diagnosis/management interface. LSHS-151 is specifically the perioperative application of virological status and HIV treatment guidance within Surgery.
- Virology ↔ Preventive Medicine (LSHS-015), Virology ↔ Public Health (LSHS-043), Virology ↔ Emergency Medicine (LSHS-044), Virology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-045), Virology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-109), and Virology ↔ Epidemiology (LSHS-004) concern other Health Sciences applications. None is an exact duplicate of the dental, surgical, or nutrition interfaces assessed here.
- Microbiology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-052), Mycology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-048), and Immunology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-053) concern microbial, fungal, and immune interfaces with dentistry. LSHS-150 is specifically concerned with viral pathogens, oral viral manifestations, selected viral lesion testing, and virology-informed dental infection prevention. General dental infection-control practices are not being reclassified as exclusively virological.
- Microbiology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-014), Cell Biology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-016), Immunology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-105), Mycology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-113), Physiology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-102), and Anatomy ↔ Surgery (LSHS-103) address other biological or clinical-surgical interfaces. LSHS-151 is specifically the HIV virology and perioperative planning interface.
- Physiology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-006), Botany ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-063), Marine Biology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-066), Ecology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-067), Genetics ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-123), Molecular Biology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-124), Cell Biology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-125), and Immunology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-106) concern other nutritional relationships. LSHS-152 is specifically HIV-related nutrition assessment and support; it does not duplicate the physiological, food-resource, ecological, genetic, cellular, or immune interfaces.
- The prior non-promotion of Cell Biology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-016) remains unchanged. It is not reassessed or overridden by the HIV-specific Virology ↔ Surgery interface.

Direction is reciprocal within each bounded interface:

- LSHS-150: Virology supplies pathogen-specific understanding of oral viral infection, manifestations, and diagnostic principles; Dentistry applies this knowledge in oral assessment, selected testing, and infection prevention. Dental observations and diagnostic needs can prompt more focused virological investigation.
- LSHS-151: Virology supplies knowledge and interpretation of HIV infection, viral load, suppression, and antiviral treatment context; Surgery applies this information in appropriate elective perioperative assessment, treatment coordination, and procedure-specific planning. Perioperative findings may prompt further virological consultation. Urgent surgery is not delayed solely to obtain HIV viral-load or CD4 results under the cited guideline.
- LSHS-152: Virology supplies knowledge of HIV infection and treatment context; Nutrition applies this context in screening, nutritional assessment, diagnosis, and individualized nutrition support. Nutrition findings can inform coordinated care and identify needs for further clinical assessment.

None of these proposals is a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. The evidence is specific to the stated clinical applications and does not imply professional equivalence or whole-field containment.
