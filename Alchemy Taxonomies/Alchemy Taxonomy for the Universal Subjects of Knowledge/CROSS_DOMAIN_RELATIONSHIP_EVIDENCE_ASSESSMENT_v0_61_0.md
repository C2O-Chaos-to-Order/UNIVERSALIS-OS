## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected subgroup examines Life Sciences in relation to Physical Therapy, Occupational Therapy, and Dentistry. The central test is whether the full Life Sciences subject contributes an integrated, multi-level body of biological evidence or method that cannot be represented adequately by one previously assessed specialist discipline.

The assessment distinguishes:

- **Life Sciences ↔ Physical Therapy:** precision rehabilitation and exercise-based rehabilitation research increasingly connect molecular, genomic, epigenetic, transcriptomic, proteomic, metabolomic, and metagenomic measurements with neural adaptation, immune and metabolic response, tissue remodeling, extracellular-matrix turnover, mitochondrial and microvascular adaptation, and functional recovery. A 2026 systematic review of omics applications in spinal-cord-injury rehabilitation describes the use of genomics, epigenomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, and metagenomics to monitor rehabilitation interventions and biological changes associated with neurorecovery, immune modulation, metabolic profiles, and functional outcomes. A separate 2026 review of exercise-based rehabilitation describes mechanotransduction, gene regulation, epigenetic mechanisms, mitochondrial and metabolic adaptation, tissue remodeling, microvascular responses, and candidate circulating biomarkers. A 2026 physiotherapy scoping review examines EEG- and neuroimaging-derived brain entropy and complexity measures as possible indicators of neuroplastic adaptation during rehabilitation. Together, these sources support an emerging systems-level interface between integrated life-science response biology and physical-therapy intervention, monitoring, and functional recovery. The evidence remains early: the spinal-cord-injury review reports small samples and heterogeneity; the neuroplasticity scoping review includes eight studies and concludes that the measures are not ready for routine clinical decisions; and biomarker kinetics, tissue specificity, analytical variability, and longitudinal validation remain unresolved. This candidate does not imply that molecular measures currently select the best therapy for an individual or replace validated functional outcomes. [S563–S566]
- **Life Sciences ↔ Occupational Therapy:** occupational therapy research and perspectives recognize that biological processes can influence sensory regulation, readiness, and participation. A 2026 systematic review of sensory processing and occupational performance included 20 heterogeneous studies and reported associations between sensory-processing differences and autonomy, motor planning, social participation, emotional self-regulation, and well-being. A 2026 occupational-therapy perspective describes microbiota–gut–brain interactions across microbial, immune, and central nervous system processes as possible contextual physiological modulators of occupational readiness and participation in children with autism spectrum disorder and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder. However, the authors explicitly identify it as a conceptual Perspective rather than a systematic or scoping review; causal mechanisms remain unconfirmed, sample and methodological limitations affect the underlying microbiota literature, and translational relevance for rehabilitation remains limited. A 2025 review of genomics and bioethics in occupational therapy is primarily conceptual and ethical, describing implications for professional reasoning rather than demonstrating a mature integrated biological intervention or outcome pathway. The available evidence therefore supports relevance of selected biological processes to occupational performance, but it does not yet demonstrate an additional full-Life-Sciences interface distinct from the many specialist relationships already assessed. [S567–S569]
- **Life Sciences ↔ Dentistry:** precision dentistry research increasingly integrates genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic information with advanced imaging and computational analysis to interpret oral health, disease mechanisms, diagnosis, and individualized care. A 2025 systematic review examined 50 studies on multi-omics, advanced imaging, and AI in precision dentistry, while identifying requirements for standardization, ethical implementation, and multicenter validation. A 2025 review of omics in dentistry describes the use of genomic variation, transcriptomic signatures, proteomic and metabolomic biomarkers, and integrated multi-omics to characterize caries, periodontitis, oral cancer, and oral pathogenesis. A 2026 review of the oral microbiome emphasizes that microbial composition alone cannot account for host response, functional variation, or strain-level pathogenic differences, and argues for hypothesis-driven combined multi-omics. A 2026 review of regenerative dentistry connects epithelial–mesenchymal interactions, stem and progenitor-cell behavior, extracellular-matrix signaling, cell-free therapies, biomaterials, regenerative endodontics, periodontal and bone regeneration, enamel repair, organoids, and whole-tooth bioengineering. Together, these sources support an integrated interface across oral microbial ecology, host biology, molecular pathology, tissue development and repair, multi-omics, and imaging. The evidence is not uniformly mature: many organoid, whole-tooth, and cell-free strategies remain preclinical or early translational; favorable tissue formation does not necessarily establish full functional regeneration; and standardized protocols and long-term human evidence remain necessary. The candidate concerns integration across multiple biological levels and clinical evidence types, not a claim that every technology is ready for routine dentistry. [S570–S573]

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. The recommendations are bounded to the described integrative interfaces, not universal dependencies between the full subject areas. No canonical change is made by this report.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-237 is reciprocal at the rehabilitation-response level: integrated biological measures inform hypotheses and monitoring of response to physical-therapy interventions; intervention exposure and functional outcomes guide further research into biological adaptation.
- LSHS-238 is not promoted. The literature provides possible links between physiological regulation and occupational readiness, but has not yet demonstrated a distinct full-domain interface with sufficient empirical or translational support.
- LSHS-239 is reciprocal at the oral-health research level: integrated biological findings inform oral-disease diagnosis and regenerative hypotheses; dental phenotypes, biological specimens, imaging, and treatment outcomes direct further life-science investigation.

Important boundaries:

- Omics findings in spinal-cord-injury rehabilitation are based on a heterogeneous and relatively small evidence base. Candidate biomarkers are not automatically validated measures of rehabilitation response or treatment-effect modifiers.
- Acute exercise-induced molecular responses do not necessarily demonstrate sustained tissue adaptation. Biomarker kinetics, tissue specificity, assay variability, and longitudinal validation must be considered.
- Brain entropy and complexity measures in neurorehabilitation remain investigational and are not ready for routine treatment selection.
- Occupational-therapy biological perspectives must preserve the distinction between physiological modulation and causal explanation of occupational performance. Participation, environment, lived experience, and social context cannot be reduced to biological signals.
- Oral microbiome taxonomic composition alone cannot establish function or pathogenic potential; host response, functional variation, and strain-level differences matter.
- Advanced regenerative-dentistry approaches differ in translational maturity. Organoids, whole-tooth bioengineering, and many cell-free strategies remain primarily experimental or early translational; favorable healing does not necessarily equal complete functional regeneration.
- Multi-omics and AI-assisted dental workflows require data standardization, independent validation, ethical implementation, and clinical evidence before routine deployment.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. This assessment does not convert emerging rehabilitation biomarkers into treatment standards, conceptual microbiota–gut–brain frameworks into causal occupational-therapy mechanisms, or experimental dental regeneration into demonstrated complete functional restoration.
