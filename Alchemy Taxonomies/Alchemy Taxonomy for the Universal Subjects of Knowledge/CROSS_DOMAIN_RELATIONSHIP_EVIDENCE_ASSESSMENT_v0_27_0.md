## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed Neuroscience interfaces selected from the reconciled inventory of 136 remaining pairs:

- Neuroscience ↔ Medicine: translational neuroscience, clinical neurological diagnosis, biomarker and imaging evaluation, treatment monitoring, and application of nervous-system research to medical care.
- Neuroscience ↔ Nursing: specialist neuroscience nursing assessment, care, monitoring, symptom management, education, prevention, coordination, and support for people with neurological conditions.
- Neuroscience ↔ Nutrition: nutritional cognitive neuroscience, nutritional influences on brain development and function, and relationships between dietary patterns, cognition, and brain-health outcomes.

The three pairs address distinct contributions. Medicine integrates clinical diagnosis, treatment decisions, and whole-person care; Nursing applies a distinct professional process to human responses, ongoing care, education, monitoring, and coordination; Nutrition studies dietary intake, nutritional status, and interventions. Neuroscience contributes evidence about the nervous system and brain function, but does not replace clinical reasoning, nursing practice, nutritional assessment, or the social and environmental dimensions of health.

The report separates mechanistic plausibility, observational association, intervention findings, and demonstrated clinical utility. In particular, a diet–cognition association does not prove that a specific dietary intervention prevents neurological disease, and a neuroscience-derived imaging or biomarker result does not become a routine clinical decision tool without adequate external validation and prospective utility evidence.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory was checked against the v1.13.1 canonical subject lists (18 Life Sciences subjects × 15 Health Sciences subjects = 270 possible pairs) and assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.26.0. The prior ledger contains 134 unique screened pairs with assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-134. Each selected pair was checked as an unordered subject pair against all 26 snapshots, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. None appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. No canonical change is made by this report.

None is the Neuroscience ↔ Medicine pair assessed here.

The three new pairs have distinct evidence objects:

- LSHS-135 concerns translation of neuroscience into medical diagnosis, clinical assessment, treatment development, monitoring, and care.
- LSHS-136 concerns the specialized nursing scope for people with actual or potential nervous-system conditions, including assessment, monitoring, education, coordination, and care.
- LSHS-137 concerns dietary exposures and nutritional status in relation to brain development, neural function, cognition, and brain-health outcomes.

Direction is reciprocal within each bounded interface:

- LSHS-135: Neuroscience contributes mechanisms, methods, and candidate diagnostic or therapeutic insights; Medicine integrates these with clinical assessment and patient-care decisions. Medical outcomes and unmet needs can guide neuroscience research.
- LSHS-136: Neuroscience contributes nervous-system and condition-specific knowledge; Nursing applies that knowledge through professional assessment, care, monitoring, education, advocacy, and coordination. Nursing observations and outcomes can inform further neuroscience and clinical research.
- LSHS-137: Neuroscience contributes knowledge and methods for examining the brain and cognition; Nutrition characterizes dietary exposures, nutritional status, and interventions. Nutritional findings can motivate neural research, and neuroscience can refine nutrition hypotheses and outcomes.

None of these proposals is a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. Clinical translation, nursing practice, and nutritional intervention remain subject to their own evidence, professional standards, and validation requirements.
