## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. This topic-focused subgroup examines Evolutionary Biology in relation to infectious-disease nursing, occupational therapy and human occupation, and surgery.

The assessment distinguishes:

- **Evolutionary Biology ↔ Nursing:** a peer-reviewed review explicitly proposes an evolutionary-nursing model for infectious disease, based on interactions and co-evolutionary dynamics between human hosts and microorganisms. Evolutionary Biology contributes explanations of pathogen adaptation, virulence, persistence, host–pathogen interaction, and selection under interventions. Nursing contributes patient-facing infection prevention, care processes, antimicrobial-stewardship participation, education, exposure reduction, and longitudinal observation. Current World Health Organization material confirms that antimicrobial resistance (AMR) and infection prevention and control (IPC) are active components of nursing and midwifery education. The evidence supports a bounded research and education interface, but the evolutionary-nursing model remains conceptual and the 2005 review itself notes that evolutionary approaches had made limited impact in nursing. The WHO 2026 curriculum assessment tool addresses AMR and IPC; it does not claim that evolutionary biology is already a standard nursing competency in every jurisdiction.
- **Evolutionary Biology ↔ Occupational Therapy:** occupational-science and occupational-therapy literature has explicitly used primatology, phylogenetic history, human behavioural ecology, and evolutionary accounts of play and occupation to examine the origins, adaptive functions, and development of human occupations. This supports a theoretical and research interface for understanding occupation, adaptation, human development, and therapeutic use of meaningful activity. The evidence is largely conceptual and includes older foundational literature; it does not validate a particular clinical intervention or establish that all occupational therapy should be framed evolutionarily.
- **Evolutionary Biology ↔ Surgery:** publications apply evolutionary concepts such as selection, niche, convergence, divergence, punctuated equilibrium, and extinction to surgical innovation, professional specialisation, organisational competition, and scope of practice. In the located examples, these are analogical or organisational models. They do not establish that biological evolutionary mechanisms or methods materially inform surgical diagnosis, operative technique, wound-healing biology, or patient outcomes as a distinct subject-pair interface. The pair is not promoted at the broad disciplinary scope assessed here.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not prove that no narrower future interface could be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-219 is principally a knowledge-to-practice and research interface: evolutionary analysis explains host–pathogen dynamics and pathogen adaptation; nursing research and practice identify prevention, stewardship, education, and patient-care questions where that knowledge may be applied.
- LSHS-220 is reciprocal at the theory and research level: evolutionary and comparative research informs questions about the origins, adaptation, and functions of occupation; occupational-therapy questions provide applied contexts for evaluating those explanations.
- LSHS-221 has no promoted direction or subtype. The available material uses evolutionary models analogically for organisational or innovation analysis and does not establish a biological-science contribution to surgical care.

Important boundaries:

- Evolutionary Biology does not substitute for Microbiology, infectious-disease diagnosis, or antimicrobial prescribing. The nursing proposal is limited to evolutionary mechanisms and their implications for infectious-disease nursing, prevention, education, and stewardship.
- Evolutionary approaches in nursing remain developing; the 2026 WHO AMR/IPC curriculum tool is evidence of current nursing education priorities, not proof that evolutionary biology is already routinely taught or implemented.
- Evolutionary accounts of human occupation and play are theoretical lenses, not universal explanations that supersede cultural, social, environmental, or individual context.
- Evolutionary analogy in surgical innovation or professional organisation is not automatically evidence of a biological relationship with Surgery.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. This assessment does not turn conceptual models into clinical efficacy evidence or treat metaphorical organisational evolution as a biological mechanism.
