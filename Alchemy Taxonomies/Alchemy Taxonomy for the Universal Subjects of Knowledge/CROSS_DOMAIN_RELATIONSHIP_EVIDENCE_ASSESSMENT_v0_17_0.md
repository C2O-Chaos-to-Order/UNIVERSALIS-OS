## 2. Method and important terminology boundaries

Each pair is assessed for a specific interface between the two canonical subject scopes. Evidence of shared vocabulary or an immune-related application is not, by itself, sufficient; the assessment identifies the distinct contribution of Immunology and the corresponding Health Sciences subject.

Important boundaries:
- **Immunology is not synonymous with Pharmacology.** Immunology studies immune components, mechanisms, cell types, pathways, and responses. Pharmacology studies the actions, targets, dose-response, disposition, safety, and therapeutic use of drugs. Immunopharmacology connects these scopes by studying how pharmacological agents alter immune function and how immunological knowledge informs the design, evaluation, and use of immune-targeted drugs.
- **Immunology is not synonymous with Surgery.** Surgical tissue injury, transplantation, and perioperative care can produce important immune responses. Immunological knowledge informs selected procedures and surgical care, especially graft rejection, immune suppression, inflammation, infection susceptibility, and tissue repair. This does not mean all surgical practice is immunological or that surgeons alone manage perioperative immune function.
- **Immunology is not synonymous with Nutrition.** Nutritional status and selected nutrients can influence immune function; immune mechanisms also inform the rationale and evaluation of nutrition interventions. Immunonutrition is a bounded area within clinical nutrition and perioperative care. Evidence in specific patient groups does not support generalized claims that a food, supplement, or dietary pattern broadly “boosts” immunity.
- **Immunology is not synonymous with Epidemiology.** Immunological assays and markers can contribute to population-based epidemiological studies. Seroepidemiology and serodynamics use antibody and other immune-response information to estimate exposure, transmission history, population immunity under defined assumptions, and vaccine impact. Antibody detection does not automatically prove protective immunity in an individual.
- **Population-level and individual-level interpretation must remain separate.** A serological marker may be useful for estimating exposure patterns in a sampled population while being insufficient to determine whether one person is protected from infection.
- **Surgical immune response is multidisciplinary.** Surgery, anesthesiology, transplant medicine, critical care, pathology, infectious-disease practice, and clinical pharmacology may all contribute. The presence of a multidisciplinary care pathway does not erase the separate canonical subject identities.
- A candidate RD-P is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. Evidence of an interface does not establish universal dependency, containment, hierarchy, or guaranteed treatment benefit.
- Evidence grades describe the strength and directness of the subject-pair interface, not the certainty that every drug, nutritional intervention, surgical practice, or epidemiological measure produces a clinical or population benefit.

These pairings do not duplicate the current group:
- LSHS-104 is the Immunology ↔ Pharmacology relationship, distinct from Immunology ↔ Emergency Medicine and the other previously assessed immunology pairs.
- LSHS-105 concerns the immunological mechanisms of transplantation and perioperative surgical care, not emergency care or rehabilitation science.
- LSHS-106 concerns nutritional inputs and immune function, not the previously assessed immunology/public-health or immunology/nursing practice interfaces.
- LSHS-107 concerns population-level immune measurements and epidemiological inference, not Immunology ↔ Public Health as a broader health-programme relationship.

Direction is recorded as reciprocal at the bounded interface where both subject scopes contribute, but this is not a claim of equal contribution in every application:
- LSHS-104: immunology informs drug-target and pathway selection; pharmacological action and response provide experimental and therapeutic feedback.
- LSHS-105: immunology informs transplant and perioperative understanding; surgical contexts provide clinical questions and outcomes for immune research.
- LSHS-106: nutrition affects defined immune functions and immune status informs nutrition research and intervention design.
- LSHS-107: immunological measurements inform population studies, while epidemiological designs establish the context and validity of those measurements.

None of these recommendations is a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim.
