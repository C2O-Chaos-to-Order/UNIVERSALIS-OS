## 1. Scope and safeguards

This assessment continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences programme using the next three pairs in the v0.66.0 reconciled remaining inventory:

- Genetics ↔ Health Sciences
- Evolutionary Biology ↔ Health Sciences
- Ecology ↔ Health Sciences

The assessment uses exact unordered subject-pair matching. A candidate RD-P requires more than general relevance: it must identify a contribution from each subject, a stable cross-domain research or practice boundary, and an interface that is not merely a restatement of one already-assessed specialist pair. A root-level candidate must also explain why its integrated scope adds value beyond the collection of specialist pairings without claiming that every Health Sciences activity depends on the Life Sciences subject.

The three interfaces are supported by different kinds of evidence and have different maturity profiles:

- **Genetics ↔ Health Sciences:** genetic and genomic research is translated into health research, diagnosis, risk assessment, prevention and treatment through clinical and population-health programmes. The World Health Assembly's 2026 precision-medicine resolution recognizes genomic information as one part of a wider clinical, molecular and health-data approach and calls for the infrastructure, workforce, governance and equitable access needed for health-system integration. This is a cross-cutting health-system interface, not a claim that precision medicine is synonymous with Genetics or that every health-science decision requires genetic testing.
- **Evolutionary Biology ↔ Health Sciences:** evolutionary medicine supplies a complementary explanatory and hypothesis-generating framework connecting evolutionary history, natural selection, trade-offs, constraints, mismatch and host–pathogen coevolution with health research, education, prevention and selected clinical questions. A dedicated Oxford University Press journal explicitly aims to connect evolutionary biology with the health sciences, and current research spans infection, drug resistance, cancer, mental health and preventive questions. The framework's clinical and educational implementation remains uneven; evolutionary explanations must be testable and must not become unsupported adaptationist stories.
- **Ecology ↔ Health Sciences:** One Health and related ecosystem-health frameworks connect ecological systems, biodiversity, species interactions, land use and environmental change with human, animal and plant health, surveillance, prevention and response. The boundary here is ecological analysis within integrated health-science approaches, not generic environmental relevance. One Health has an established international framework, but the broader literature on ecological determinants of health still lacks consistent definitions; the candidate is therefore limited to identifiable ecosystem-health methods and collaborations, not a universal ecology-to-health dependency.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. It does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, verification, or owner acceptance. No canonical change is made by this report.
