## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected subgroup examines three interfaces: hereditary genetics and surgical decision-making; botanical identification and population-level plant-exposure epidemiology; and physiological assessment in preventive medicine.

The assessment distinguishes three scopes:

- **Genetics ↔ Surgery:** inherited pathogenic variants and hereditary cancer-risk assessment in relation to selected risk-reducing and oncologic surgical decisions. The evidence concerns defined hereditary cancer contexts and individualized decisions; it does not imply that every genetic variant, every carrier, or every cancer indication calls for surgery.
- **Botany ↔ Epidemiology:** botanical taxonomy and plant-identification knowledge in relation to the epidemiological characterization of human plant exposures and intoxications, including their distribution across populations, exposure routes, taxa, and reported outcomes. Poison-centre records are a selected surveillance and case-reporting source, not a complete census of exposures or a population-incidence denominator.
- **Physiology ↔ Preventive Medicine:** physiological function and measurements in relation to selected cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic risk assessment, early detection, and preventive exercise counselling. The evidence supports bounded assessment pathways; it does not establish that every physiological measure is a validated screening test or that exercise testing is required for all asymptomatic people.

The three interfaces are distinct from existing assessments involving Genetics, Botany, and Physiology with other Health Sciences subjects. Pair-specific evidence is required; shared vocabulary, adjacent practice, or a common clinical context alone is insufficient.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. No canonical change is made by this report.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-183 is a reciprocal, multidisciplinary interface: genetic evidence can inform individualized surgical risk-reduction and oncologic planning, while surgical context defines relevant procedural choices and outcomes for hereditary-risk care.

- LSHS-185 is primarily a physiology-to-prevention application, with preventive assessment and outcomes generating research questions about physiological mechanisms, measurements, and risk modification.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. This assessment does not recommend a specific operation, infer population incidence from poison-centre reports, or require routine physiological or exercise testing for everyone.
