## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected subgroup examines disability measurement and rehabilitation research in fungal disease, biotechnology-enabled biosensing for mental-health biomarker monitoring, and water-based physical therapy evaluated against the disciplinary scope of Marine Biology.

The assessment distinguishes:

- **Mycology ↔ Rehabilitation Science:** fungal neglected tropical diseases (NTDs), especially eumycetoma, can cause tissue destruction, deformity, pain, disability, reduced mobility, and restrictions in activities and participation. Rehabilitation Science contributes frameworks for describing functioning and disability, standardized outcome measurement, quality-of-life and participation assessment, and research into rehabilitation needs and outcomes. Mycology contributes the fungal disease classification, causative organisms, disease progression, and sequelae that define the population and disease context. The evidence supports a research and measurement interface, but standardized disability measures for fungal NTDs remain limited and intervention effectiveness is not established by the observational sources reviewed. This is distinct from the previously assessed Mycology ↔ Physical Therapy and Mycology ↔ Occupational Therapy pairs: the present scope is rehabilitation science as a research discipline, not a claim about a particular therapy profession.
- **Biotechnology ↔ Mental Health:** biosensors and nanobiosensors using molecular or biochemical recognition, electrochemical or optical transduction, and biological sample matrices are being developed to detect or monitor biomarkers associated with stress, anxiety, depression, and other psychiatric conditions. The relevant targets include cortisol, dopamine, serotonin, cytokines, proteins, exosomal cargo, and circulating microRNAs. Biotechnology contributes biological recognition elements, assay design, biofunctionalization, sample-to-signal platforms, and analytical validation; Mental Health contributes clinical phenotypes, psychiatric research questions, longitudinal monitoring contexts, and interpretation alongside established assessment. The evidence supports an emerging research and monitoring interface, not stand-alone psychiatric diagnosis or routine clinical use. The recommendation does not classify every electronic sensor, EEG device, or general-purpose digital wearable as Biotechnology.
- **Marine Biology ↔ Physical Therapy:** aquatic rehabilitation uses water’s buoyancy, resistance, and hydrostatic properties to support exercise and functional recovery. Recent meta-analyses report benefits for selected musculoskeletal and knee conditions. However, the interventions are aquatic exercise and rehabilitation, generally delivered in controlled water or pool settings. The reviewed sources do not demonstrate a contribution from marine organisms, ecosystems, oceanographic processes, or Marine Biology methods. The therapeutic medium being water does not itself establish a Marine Biology ↔ Physical Therapy disciplinary relationship. This pair is not promoted.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not prove that no narrower future interface could be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.

LSHS-213 is different: it concerns rehabilitation science as a research discipline, including disability classification, ICF-based functional and participation measurement, quality-of-life outcomes, and rehabilitation-needs research. The evidence sources may overlap in disease context, but the subject-pair function and disciplinary contribution are not interchangeable.
- **Biotechnology:** Biotechnology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-024), Pharmacology (LSHS-025), Surgery (LSHS-026), Public Health (LSHS-027), Preventive Medicine (LSHS-028), Nursing (LSHS-099), Dentistry (LSHS-100), Nutrition (LSHS-170), Physical Therapy (LSHS-180), Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-181), Occupational Therapy (LSHS-182), Epidemiology (LSHS-196), and Emergency Medicine (LSHS-207) address other exact pairs. LSHS-214 concerns the more specific use of biological recognition and biochemical biosensing platforms for mental-health biomarker research and adjunctive monitoring. It is not a general biotechnology / healthcare relationship, nor a claim that conventional mental-health assessment is being replaced.
- **Marine Biology:** Marine Biology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-060), Public Health (LSHS-061), Preventive Medicine (LSHS-062), Nutrition (LSHS-066), Epidemiology (LSHS-119), Pharmacology (LSHS-121), Emergency Medicine (LSHS-162), Mental Health (LSHS-179), Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-191; not promoted), Nursing (LSHS-192), Dentistry (LSHS-206; not promoted), and Surgery (LSHS-212; not promoted) concern other exact pairs. LSHS-191 and LSHS-212 examined marine-derived biomaterials and their regenerative, wound-care, or surgical applications, and were not promoted because material provenance and engineering alone did not establish a distinct Marine Biology contribution. LSHS-215 examines a separate intervention area—water-based physical therapy—and is not a duplicate of the biomaterial assessments. It is not promoted because aquatic exercise does not require Marine Biology knowledge or methods.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-213 is reciprocal within rehabilitation research: Mycology defines fungal disease populations, sequelae, and disease-specific factors; Rehabilitation Science supplies models and outcome methods for analyzing disability, function, participation, quality of life, and rehabilitation needs.
- LSHS-214 is reciprocal within translational biosensing research: Biotechnology supplies biological recognition and assay platforms; Mental Health supplies the psychiatric context, clinically meaningful outcomes, and interpretation requirements for biomarker monitoring.
- LSHS-215 has no promoted direction or subtype. Aquatic exercise is a Physical Therapy modality, but the reviewed evidence does not establish Marine Biology as a contributing discipline.

Important boundaries:

- Mycetoma can be fungal (eumycetoma) or bacterial (actinomycetoma). Fungal disease evidence must be distinguished from mixed mycetoma cohorts; disability estimates from studies that do not stratify etiology must not be attributed exclusively to Mycology.
- Rehabilitation Science is a distinct research discipline and must not be collapsed into Physical Therapy or Occupational Therapy. The three examine related but different questions: research models and outcomes, movement and physical function, and daily activities and independence.
- Mental-health biomarkers are adjunctive research or monitoring measures. Peripheral cortisol, serotonin, dopamine, inflammatory markers, and other analytes require context-sensitive interpretation and are not stand-alone psychiatric diagnostic tests.
- Aquatic rehabilitation does not become Marine Biology merely because water is used. Evidence of a distinct marine biological contribution requires organism, ecosystem, physiological, ecological, or oceanographic knowledge or methods that materially shape the intervention.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. This assessment does not attribute mixed-etiology disability data exclusively to fungal disease, convert biosensor prototypes into validated psychiatric diagnostics, or equate pool-based rehabilitation with Marine Biology.
