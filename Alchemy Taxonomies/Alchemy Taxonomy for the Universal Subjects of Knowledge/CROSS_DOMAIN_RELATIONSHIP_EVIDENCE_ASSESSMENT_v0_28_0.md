## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed Evolutionary Biology interfaces selected from the reconciled inventory of 133 remaining pairs:

- Evolutionary Biology ↔ Medicine: evolutionary medicine as a complementary explanatory framework for disease vulnerability, host–pathogen dynamics, biological trade-offs, evolutionary constraints, and environmental mismatch in medical research and clinical reasoning.
- Evolutionary Biology ↔ Pharmacology: evolutionary processes that shape drug response and resistance, including mutation accessibility, selection under drug pressure, resistance–fitness trade-offs, and the use of evolutionary forecasting in drug discovery and candidate prioritization.
- Evolutionary Biology ↔ Mental Health: evolutionary psychiatry as a research framework for investigating evolutionary origins of psychological capacities, symptoms, and vulnerabilities relevant to selected mental disorders.

The three interfaces are distinct. Medicine integrates basic and applied biomedical knowledge with diagnosis, treatment, and patient care. Pharmacology studies drug action, therapeutic effects, safety, and development. Mental Health encompasses much more than psychiatric disorders and includes psychological, social, cultural, developmental, preventive, therapeutic, recovery, and community dimensions. Evolutionary Biology can contribute explanatory and predictive frameworks to specific questions in each domain, but does not replace their methods, evidence standards, or professional practices.

The report distinguishes:
- evolutionary explanations of why bodies and populations may be vulnerable to disease from proximate explanations of how disease mechanisms operate;
- evidence of drug-resistance evolution and predictive models from proof that a specific drug candidate will remain effective in clinical use;
- testable evolutionary hypotheses about mental-health phenomena from speculative adaptationist narratives about the origins of particular disorders.

No disorder is assumed to be an adaptation. Natural-selection explanations must be explicit, falsifiable where possible, and compatible with comparative, genetic, developmental, historical, and contemporary evidence. The report does not treat evolutionary psychiatry as a new treatment modality or as a substitute for established mental-health care.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory was reconciled against the v1.13.8 canonical subject lists (18 Life Sciences subjects × 15 Health Sciences subjects = 270 possible pairs) and assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.27.0. The prior ledger contains 137 unique screened pairs with assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-137. Each selected pair was checked as an unordered subject pair against all 27 snapshots, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. None appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

The three selected pairs were checked as unordered subject pairs against all assessment tables in v0.1.0 through v0.27.0. The prior ledger contains 137 unique screened pairs with sequential identifiers LSHS-001 through LSHS-137. Exact normalized-pair checks found no occurrence of Evolutionary Biology ↔ Medicine, Evolutionary Biology ↔ Pharmacology, or Evolutionary Biology ↔ Mental Health in any of the 27 prior snapshots. Canonical names and identifiers were checked against the v1.13.8 taxonomy.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- Evolutionary Biology ↔ Epidemiology (LSHS-089) concerns population-level disease occurrence, determinants, and epidemiological analysis. Evolutionary Biology ↔ Public Health (LSHS-090) concerns evolutionary contributions to population-health policy and programmes. LSHS-138 concerns the wider medical interface and evolutionary explanations of disease vulnerability, mechanisms, and clinical reasoning; LSHS-139 concerns drug-response and resistance evolution; LSHS-140 concerns evolutionary hypotheses within mental-health research.
- Genetics ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-020), Molecular Biology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-008), Microbiology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-108), Virology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-109), and Mycology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-110) address inherited, molecular, or organism-specific contributions to pharmacological mechanisms and drug action. LSHS-139 instead focuses on evolutionary dynamics and selection under drug pressure as a cross-cutting framework for resistance prediction and drug-development decisions. The microbial and viral examples are evidence for the bounded application, not a claim that Evolutionary Biology replaces those organismal sciences.
- Neuroscience ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-005), Genetics ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-126), Molecular Biology ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-127), and Cell Biology ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-128) address neural, inherited, molecular, and cellular research interfaces with mental-health conditions. LSHS-140 concerns evolutionary hypotheses about species-wide vulnerabilities, emotional and behavioral capacities, trade-offs, and environmental mismatch. It is not a substitute for or repetition of the mechanistic and neurobiological interfaces.
- The evolutionary-psychiatry interface is not treated as equivalent to all Mental Health. Its strongest current expression is in research into selected psychiatric phenomena and mental disorders; broader psychological, social, cultural, developmental, preventive, therapeutic, recovery, and community dimensions remain essential to the full Health Sciences subject.
- The current Evolutionary Biology ↔ Medicine and Evolutionary Biology ↔ Pharmacology records are also not blanket claims that every disease or drug response has a useful evolutionary explanation. The interface is limited to questions where evolutionary hypotheses, selection dynamics, or resistance forecasts can be specified and evaluated.

Direction is reciprocal within each bounded interface:

- LSHS-138: Evolutionary Biology supplies hypotheses about the origins of vulnerability and evolutionary constraints; Medicine supplies clinical phenotypes, mechanistic evidence, patient context, and outcomes. Medical observations can refine evolutionary questions.
- LSHS-139: Evolutionary Biology supplies methods for examining selection, mutation accessibility, adaptation, and resistance trade-offs; Pharmacology supplies drug mechanisms, exposure, efficacy, safety, and development decisions. Drug-response and resistance data can refine evolutionary models.
- LSHS-140: Evolutionary Biology supplies principles for formulating testable hypotheses about evolutionary vulnerability and psychological capacities; Mental Health supplies defined phenomena, clinical and experiential context, and evidence that can support, constrain, or reject those hypotheses. Mental-health findings can refine evolutionary research questions.

None of these proposals is a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. Evolutionary Biology is not a substitute for proximate biomedical mechanisms, pharmacological evaluation, or the broader body of mental-health knowledge and care.
