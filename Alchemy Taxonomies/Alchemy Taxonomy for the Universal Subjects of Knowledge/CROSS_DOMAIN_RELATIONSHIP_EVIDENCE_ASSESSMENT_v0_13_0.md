## 2. Method and important terminology boundaries

Each pair was screened for a specific interface between the two canonical subject scopes. Evidence of a shared curriculum topic alone was not treated as sufficient. Professional education standards were used as corroborating evidence and paired with clinical practice guidance, clinical protocols, systematic reviews, or direct rehabilitation research where available.

Important boundaries:
- **Neuroscience is not synonymous with Occupational Therapy.** Neuroscience can inform understanding of neural impairment, motor learning, neuroplasticity, and recovery. Occupational Therapy retains its distinctive focus on occupation, participation, adaptation, and meaningful everyday performance.
- **Physiology is not synonymous with Occupational Therapy.** The candidate concerns measurable physiological responses to occupation-based and therapeutic activity, including cardiopulmonary demand, activity tolerance, fatigue, and breathlessness. It does not imply that all OT work is exercise physiology.
- **Anatomy is not synonymous with Occupational Therapy or Physical Therapy.** The candidate interfaces are limited to structural knowledge used in defined assessment and intervention tasks, such as hand tendon injury, joint movement, muscle function, palpation, and anatomical interpretation. Anatomy does not define either clinical profession.
- **Anatomy is not synonymous with Rehabilitation Science.** The candidate concerns anatomical structure and tissue morphology as objects of rehabilitation assessment and research. It does not imply that every rehabilitation question is anatomical or that emerging imaging measures are validated clinical decision rules.
- A candidate RD-P is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. Evidence of an interface does not establish universal dependency, containment, hierarchy, or clinical efficacy.
- Evidence grades describe the strength and directness of the subject-pair interface, not the certainty that any single treatment produces a clinical benefit.

The subject-pair distinctions are retained:
- Neuroscience ↔ Occupational Therapy is distinct from the previously screened Neuroscience ↔ Physical Therapy and Neuroscience ↔ Rehabilitation Science pairs because it concerns occupation-based neurological assessment and participation.
- Physiology ↔ Occupational Therapy is distinct from the existing Physiology ↔ Physical Therapy and Physiology ↔ Rehabilitation Science pairs because its focus is physiological load and response during occupation-based activity, including OT activity-tolerance practice.
- Anatomy ↔ Occupational Therapy is a hand/upper-limb occupation and hand-therapy interface; it is not the previously screened Anatomy ↔ Medicine relationship.
- Anatomy ↔ Physical Therapy concerns examination, movement analysis, and structural clinical reasoning in PT; it is not the previously screened Anatomy ↔ Medicine relationship.
- Anatomy ↔ Rehabilitation Science concerns structural measurement and tissue adaptation in rehabilitation research. It is separate from both clinical PT practice and the previously screened Physiology ↔ Rehabilitation Science pair.

The four existing assessments are not counted again, and no new pair ID has been assigned to them.
