## 2. Method and important terminology boundaries

Each pair is assessed for a specific interface between the two canonical subject scopes. A shared clinical setting, shared product, or foundational curriculum topic is not by itself sufficient to establish a distinct subject-pair relationship.

Important boundaries:
- **Cell Biology is not synonymous with Nursing.** Cellular mechanisms of inflammation, proliferation, migration, angiogenesis, and tissue remodelling are relevant to wound healing. Nursing wound-care practice assesses tissue condition and manages internal and external factors affecting healing. The relationship is limited to the application of cellular repair knowledge within wound-care assessment and management; nurses do not thereby become cellular biologists, and not all nursing is wound care.
- **Cell Biology is not synonymous with Dentistry.** Oral tissue regeneration involves cell proliferation, differentiation, immunomodulation, and interactions between cells and tissue scaffolds. Dental pulp and periodontal regeneration are specific interfaces. Some methods have human clinical evidence, while others remain experimental; new tissue formation does not automatically establish complete functional regeneration.
- **Molecular Biology is not synonymous with Nursing.** Molecular tumour profiling and next-generation sequencing can generate findings used in precision-oncology workflows. Nursing roles may include coordination, interpretation support, patient education, navigation, and management of targeted-treatment effects. The professional role and competency framework remain under development; a nurse's use of a molecular report does not imply that nurses perform the underlying molecular laboratory analysis.
- **Molecular Biology is not synonymous with Dentistry.** Oral-fluid nucleic acids and molecular biomarkers are studied as potential diagnostic adjuncts. The 2026 living evidence review identified only one diagnostic-accuracy study and no diagnostic-impact studies for salivary adjuncts in oral potentially malignant disorders and oral squamous cell carcinoma; the evidence was insufficient for clinical decision-making. This limitation prevents promotion in this pass; it does not establish that the research areas are unrelated.
- **Biotechnology is not synonymous with Nursing.** Nurses may assess wounds and participate in multidisciplinary care involving biologic or bioengineered products. A product's presence in a nursing care pathway does not by itself demonstrate a distinct Biotechnology ↔ Nursing disciplinary relationship. Evidence of a specific nursing role in the selection, application, monitoring, development, or evaluation of biotechnology is needed.
- **Biotechnology is not synonymous with Dentistry.** Dental tissue engineering, bioactive materials, cell-based methods, and regenerative protocols form a specific applied interface. Their clinical maturity varies substantially; some methods are supported by established or emerging clinical protocols, while exogenous cell transplantation and whole-tooth bioengineering remain experimental.
- **Cell Biology and Biotechnology are related but distinct.** Cell Biology explains cellular structures and processes; Biotechnology applies biological systems, materials, and engineered methods to develop or deliver technologies. A single regenerative-dentistry example may involve both, but does not make the two subject identities interchangeable.
- A candidate RD-P is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. Evidence of an interface does not establish universal dependency, containment, hierarchy, or clinical efficacy.
- Evidence grades describe the strength and directness of the subject-pair interface, not the certainty that every treatment, test, technology, or clinical application produces a patient benefit.
