## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected subgroup examines marine-organism biology in nursing assessment and first response, evolutionary explanations of chronic-disease susceptibility in preventive medicine, and anatomical body-composition phenotyping in population health research.

The assessment distinguishes three scopes:

- **Marine Biology ↔ Nursing:** marine and aquatic organism characteristics, venoms, stinging mechanisms, and exposure context in nursing assessment, first aid, clinical escalation, and prevention education for marine envenomations. The evidence includes a current adult clinical protocol explicitly intended for registered and enrolled nurses, a nursing-practice article, and population-level poison-centre surveillance. The scope is marine-organism-specific; it does not imply that all aquatic animal exposures are marine, that nursing staff independently identify every organism, or that these protocols supersede local scope-of-practice rules and toxicology advice.
- **Evolutionary Biology ↔ Preventive Medicine:** evolutionary trade-offs, life-history theory, developmental programming, and evolutionary mismatch as research frameworks for understanding chronic-disease susceptibility and the timing of preventive action. The evidence is strongest as an explanatory and hypothesis-generating framework for life-course and cardiometabolic prevention. It does not establish that a single evolutionary account explains every chronic disease, validate a new clinical risk score, or replace evidence-based preventive guidance.
- **Anatomy ↔ Public Health:** anatomical compartments and structural phenotypes—including visceral and subcutaneous adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, and intramuscular adipose tissue—measured at population scale to characterize distributions, disparities, and associations with cardiometabolic outcomes. The interface is population-level research and surveillance; it is distinct from Anatomy ↔ Preventive Medicine (LSHS-187), which focused on individual clinical measurement and preventive risk assessment. Large imaging cohorts do not by themselves establish that whole-body MRI should be used for routine population screening.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. No canonical change is made by this report.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-192 is reciprocal within a defined clinical-education and research interface: marine organism biology informs hazard recognition and exposure context, while nursing practice and surveillance data identify care, education, and research needs.
- LSHS-193 is primarily a conceptual and research interface: evolutionary mechanisms and life-history frameworks inform hypotheses about disease susceptibility and prevention timing; preventive research evaluates whether those hypotheses lead to effective, equitable interventions.
- LSHS-194 is reciprocal within population research: anatomy provides structural phenotypes and measurement definitions, while public-health cohorts, determinants, and outcomes inform population-level anatomical analysis.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. This assessment does not replace local nursing protocols, elevate evolutionary hypotheses into clinical standards, or recommend universal whole-body imaging.
