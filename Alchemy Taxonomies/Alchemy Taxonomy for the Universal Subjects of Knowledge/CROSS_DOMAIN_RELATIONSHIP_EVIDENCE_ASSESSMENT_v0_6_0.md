## 2. Method and decision rule

Each pair is assessed on evidence for a specific, defensible interface between the two canonical subject scopes. Evidence for a clinical application or specialist practice is not treated as proof of equivalence, containment, or universal dependency.

The assessment preserves these distinctions:
- shared identity versus a relationship between distinct subjects;
- a bounded clinical or research interface versus a whole-subject relationship;
- knowledge contribution versus dependency or enablement;
- evidence quality versus owner acceptance and canonical registration.

A candidate RD-P is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered relationship. Direction is only recorded where the sources support a contribution or exchange; otherwise the relationship remains non-directional. All eight pairs below are kept bounded to the stated interface.
