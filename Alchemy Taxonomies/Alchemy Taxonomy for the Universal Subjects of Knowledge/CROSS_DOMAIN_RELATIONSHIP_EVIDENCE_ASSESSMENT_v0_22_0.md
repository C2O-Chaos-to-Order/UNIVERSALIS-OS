## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed pairs selected from the reconciled inventory of 151 remaining pairs.

The topic-focused group examines natural-product and animal-bioactive interfaces with Pharmacology:

- Botany ↔ Pharmacology: plant-derived medicinal raw materials, pharmacognosy, botanical identity and quality, and pharmacological characterization.
- Marine Biology ↔ Pharmacology: non-venom marine-invertebrate natural products, bioactivity, mechanisms, and drug discovery.
- Zoology ↔ Pharmacology: animal venom systems and animal-derived bioactive molecules as sources of pharmacological leads.

The three pairs are intentionally bounded. The evidence does not establish that all plants, marine organisms, animals, or natural products have therapeutic value; that every pharmacological discovery depends on these subjects; or that one subject contains another.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. Each selected pair was checked as an unordered pair against all assessment tables in v0.1.0 through v0.21.0. Historical name-only entries and later stable-ID entries were both considered. None of the selected pairs appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

The three selected pairs were checked as unordered subject pairs against the full assessment ledger in v0.1.0 through v0.21.0. The prior ledger contains 119 unique screened pairs with sequential identifiers LSHS-001 through LSHS-119. Canonical subject names and identifiers were checked against the v1.13.8 baseline. None of the selected pairs is an exact duplicate.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- Botany ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-063), Botany ↔ Medicine (LSHS-064), and Botany ↔ Public Health (LSHS-065) concern nutritional, clinical, and public-health applications of plant knowledge. Botany ↔ Emergency Medicine (LSHS-070), Nursing (LSHS-071), and Preventive Medicine (LSHS-072) concern plant-toxic exposures, patient care, or exposure prevention. LSHS-120 concerns plant-source identity, pharmacognosy, and pharmacological characterization of plant-derived products, not plant exposure or nutrition alone.
- Marine Biology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-060), Public Health (LSHS-061), Preventive Medicine (LSHS-062), and Nutrition (LSHS-066) concern clinical, population-health, preventive, or nutritional interfaces. LSHS-121 concerns non-venom marine-invertebrate natural-product discovery and pharmacological mechanisms, not marine-origin illness, public-health surveillance, or nutritional use.
- Zoology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-055), Public Health (LSHS-056), Preventive Medicine (LSHS-057), Emergency Medicine (LSHS-068), Nursing (LSHS-069), and rehabilitation disciplines (LSHS-075–077) address clinical, zoonotic, preventive, acute-care, nursing, or animal-assisted rehabilitation questions. Zoology ↔ Epidemiology (LSHS-118) addresses animal-population disease surveillance and measurement. LSHS-122 is specifically about animal venoms and selected animal-derived bioactive molecules as pharmacological leads.
- Existing Pharmacology pairings remain distinct: Molecular Biology (LSHS-008), Cell Biology (LSHS-021), Immunology (LSHS-104), Microbiology (LSHS-108), Virology (LSHS-109), Mycology (LSHS-110), Neuroscience (LSHS-114), Physiology (LSHS-115), and Anatomy (LSHS-116). Those records address molecular, cellular, immune, microbial, neural, physiological, or anatomical contributions. The present records address plant-source pharmacognosy, non-venom marine-invertebrate natural products, and animal-venom systems.
- Marine Biology and Zoology can overlap in the taxonomic classification of marine animals. The scope distinction here is deliberate: LSHS-121 is restricted to non-venom marine-invertebrate natural products; LSHS-122 is restricted to animal venoms and venom-derived molecules irrespective of habitat. Venom-specific compounds are not double-counted as evidence for LSHS-121.

The assessments do not establish that the source subjects are interchangeable, that all source organisms contain useful drug leads, or that a promising bioactivity assay demonstrates clinical benefit.

Direction is reciprocal within each bounded interface:

- LSHS-120: Botany supplies plant identity, source-material, and variation knowledge; Pharmacology tests biological activity, mechanism, exposure, dose, safety, and therapeutic relevance. Pharmacological characterization can guide botanical-material selection and standardization.
- LSHS-121: Marine Biology supplies non-venom marine-invertebrate source and organismal context; Pharmacology evaluates isolated compounds, their biological activities, mechanisms, and therapeutic potential. Pharmacological results can guide subsequent marine biodiscovery.
- LSHS-122: Zoology supplies animal and venom-system context; Pharmacology characterizes constituent molecules, target interactions, dose-response, safety, and potential therapeutic use. Pharmacological results can inform further comparative and organismal research.

None of these proposals is a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim.
