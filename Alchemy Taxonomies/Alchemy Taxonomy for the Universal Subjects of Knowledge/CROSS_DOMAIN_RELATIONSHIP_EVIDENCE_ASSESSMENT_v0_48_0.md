## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected subgroup examines evolutionary locomotor biology in physical-therapy gait rehabilitation, cellular morphology in emergency assessment of acute hematologic disorders, and plant-derived biomaterials in surgical tissue reconstruction.

The assessment distinguishes three scopes:

- **Evolutionary Biology ↔ Physical Therapy:** evolutionary conservation of interlimb locomotor control, comparative vertebrate locomotion, and the evolutionary context of human gait as a research framework for neurological physical therapy after stroke, spinal cord injury, or traumatic brain injury. The central review explicitly discusses translation of evolutionary and comparative locomotor findings to clinical neurorehabilitation. This is a bounded research interface; it does not establish that an evolutionary explanation is required for every gait intervention or that evolution-informed protocols have independently proven superior outcomes.
- **Cell Biology ↔ Emergency Medicine:** cellular morphology and hematopoietic-cell abnormalities identified through peripheral blood smear and related cytologic assessment in the emergency evaluation of acute leukemia and selected hematologic emergencies. The diagnostic pathway is multidisciplinary and involves clinical laboratory medicine, pathology/hematopathology, and emergency clinicians. The relationship is limited to urgent recognition, interpretation, and escalation of cellular findings; it does not make all Cell Biology part of emergency practice or suggest that emergency clinicians independently provide definitive hematopathologic diagnosis.
- **Botany ↔ Surgery:** plant-derived materials and plant-inspired structural architectures used in tissue-engineering and regenerative-medicine research relevant to potential reconstruction. The evidence is primarily biomaterials and bioengineering research; the 2026 review reports no human clinical trials of the plant-based scaffolds it assesses. The sources do not establish that Botany, as a full scientific discipline, makes a sufficiently direct and distinct contribution to surgical practice beyond biomaterials science, chemical/materials engineering, tissue engineering, and regenerative medicine. This pair is not promoted.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not establish that no narrower future relationship could ever be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.
