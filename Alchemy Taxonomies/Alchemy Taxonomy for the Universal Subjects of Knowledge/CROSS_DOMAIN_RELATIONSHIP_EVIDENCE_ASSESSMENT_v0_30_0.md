## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed Physiology interfaces selected from the reconciled inventory of 127 remaining pairs:

- Physiology ↔ Nursing: physiological knowledge used in nursing assessment, measurement, interpretation, monitoring, and evaluation of patient responses.
- Physiology ↔ Public Health: physiological mechanisms relevant to population health risks and public-health responses, illustrated by heat- and cold-related cardiovascular vulnerability, risk communication, and protective interventions.
- Physiology ↔ Epidemiology: physiological mechanisms that help explain epidemiological associations between environmental exposures and health outcomes, illustrated by heat strain and heat-related illness.

The three interfaces are related but distinct. Nursing applies professional assessment and care processes to an individual’s physiological, psychological, social, and other needs. Public Health organizes population-level surveillance, prevention, services, communication, and policy. Epidemiology studies the distribution and determinants of health-related states or events in populations. Physiology characterizes normal and disordered functions of organisms and the mechanisms that maintain homeostasis. Physiological measurement, mechanistic explanation, population-level risk estimation, and professional care are not interchangeable.

The evidence is especially direct for core physiological measurements in nursing assessment and for temperature-related cardiovascular and heat-health research. The report does not imply that every nursing activity is physiological, that physiology alone determines population policy, or that physiological mechanisms by themselves establish population-level causation. In particular, an epidemiological association between temperature and illness must be interpreted with exposure measurement, population characteristics, protective behaviours, co-exposures, and study design in view.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory was checked against the v1.13.8 canonical subject lists (18 Life Sciences subjects × 15 Health Sciences subjects = 270 possible pairs) and assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.29.0. The prior ledger contains 143 unique screened pairs with assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-143. Each selected pair was checked as an unordered subject pair against all 29 snapshots, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. None appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

The three selected pairs were checked as unordered subject pairs against all assessment tables in v0.1.0 through v0.29.0. The prior ledger contains 143 unique screened pairs with sequential identifiers LSHS-001 through LSHS-143. Exact normalized-pair checks found no occurrence of Physiology ↔ Nursing, Physiology ↔ Public Health, or Physiology ↔ Epidemiology in any of the 29 prior snapshots. Canonical names and identifiers were checked against the v1.13.8 taxonomy.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- Physiology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-001) concerns medical and clinical applications of physiological knowledge. It does not duplicate the nursing-specific assessment and monitoring application in LSHS-144.
- Physiology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-006) concerns nutrient-related physiological function and response; Physiology ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-039) concerns physiological responses in physical-therapy contexts; Physiology ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-038), Occupational Therapy (LSHS-085), Emergency Medicine (LSHS-037), Surgery (LSHS-102), and Pharmacology (LSHS-115) concern their own clinical, rehabilitation, emergency, operative, or drug-related interfaces. None is the exact Physiology ↔ Nursing, Public Health, or Epidemiology pair.
- Neuroscience ↔ Epidemiology (LSHS-129) concerns population-level study of neurological conditions. LSHS-146 concerns physiological responses to exposure and physiological mechanisms that inform epidemiological inference; the neurological subject is not involved.
- Neuroscience ↔ Public Health (LSHS-130) concerns translation of neurological and brain-health knowledge into population programmes. LSHS-145 concerns physiological responses to environmental exposures and cardiovascular vulnerability in public-health planning.
- Evolutionary Biology ↔ Epidemiology (LSHS-089) and Evolutionary Biology ↔ Public Health (LSHS-090) concern evolutionary processes and population-health questions. The present pairs concern organismal function and physiological response, not evolutionary explanation.
- The CDC and AHA heat-health examples support both LSHS-145 and LSHS-146 but are used to establish different cross-domain functions. LSHS-145 concerns how physiological vulnerability informs population surveillance, preparedness, risk communication, and protective policy. LSHS-146 concerns how physiological mechanisms explain or constrain epidemiological exposure–outcome relationships and risk estimates. Shared subject matter does not make the two Health Sciences subjects interchangeable.
- Nursing assessment includes physiological, psychological, social, spiritual, and environmental domains. LSHS-144 concerns the specific physiological knowledge used in assessment and monitoring, not an attempt to reduce nursing practice to vital signs or bodily functions.

Direction is reciprocal within each bounded interface:

- LSHS-144: Physiology supplies knowledge of normal and abnormal function and the mechanisms represented by clinical measures; Nursing applies that knowledge through assessment, monitoring, interpretation, communication, and individualized care. Nursing observations can refine physiological questions and priorities.
- LSHS-145: Physiology supplies mechanisms and evidence about environmental stress and physiological vulnerability; Public Health uses that evidence with population, social, environmental, and service information to design protective measures. Population outcomes can refine physiological and exposure research.
- LSHS-146: Physiology supplies mechanisms and response variation that help interpret health outcomes; Epidemiology quantifies exposure, distribution, determinants, and risk across populations. Epidemiological findings can guide physiological research questions, and physiology can improve the interpretation of epidemiological associations.

None of these proposals is a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. Physiological knowledge complements nursing, public-health, and epidemiological methods rather than replacing them.
