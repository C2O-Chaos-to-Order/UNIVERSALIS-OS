## 2. Method and important terminology boundaries

Each pair is assessed for a specific interface between the two canonical subject scopes. Professional training requirements, clinical monitoring standards, and operative research are considered together, but a shared educational topic alone is not treated as proof of a substantive relationship.

Important boundaries:
- **Neuroscience is not synonymous with Surgery.** Neuroscience informs knowledge of neural structure, function, sensory pathways, motor pathways, and functional integrity. A bounded interface occurs in selected neurosurgical and spinal procedures where intraoperative neurophysiological monitoring (IONM) can provide information about neural function during surgical manipulation. This does not mean all surgery is neurological, that all neurosurgical procedures use IONM, or that neurophysiological signals are infallible.
- **Physiology is not synonymous with Surgery or Anesthesiology.** Physiological knowledge informs assessment and management of surgical patients, including circulatory function, perfusion, oxygen delivery, shock, fluid and electrolyte balance, and the response to injury. Surgeons, anesthesiologists, intensivists, nurses, and other perioperative professionals have distinct but coordinated responsibilities. The relationship is not an assertion that every perioperative physiological task belongs to the Surgery discipline.
- **Anatomy is not synonymous with Surgery.** Anatomy provides structural knowledge of organs, tissues, vessels, nerves, and anatomical variation. Surgery applies that knowledge in operative planning, identification of structures, dissection, and procedural decisions. The interface is particularly evident where anatomical variation changes the operative approach or increases technical complexity.
- **Neuroscience and neurophysiological monitoring are not interchangeable.** IONM is a specialized applied method drawing on neural pathway and electrophysiological knowledge. Monitoring practice also depends on surgical context, anesthesia, technical quality, expert interpretation, and team response.
- **Physiological monitoring is not a substitute for clinical judgment.** Hemodynamic measurements are affected by calibration, device assumptions, patient-specific factors, and the limitations of available surrogates. A measurement does not automatically dictate one correct surgical or anesthetic action.
- **Applied surgical anatomy is narrower than all of Anatomy.** Evidence about operative anatomy and anatomical variants supports a bounded relationship, not a claim that the whole Anatomy discipline exists to serve Surgery.
- A candidate RD-P is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. Evidence of an interface does not establish universal dependency, containment, hierarchy, or a guaranteed improvement in patient outcomes.
- Evidence grades describe the strength and directness of the subject-pair interface, not the certainty that every monitoring method or surgical application improves outcomes.

## 4. Duplicate, direction, and scope cross-check

The three pairs were compared as unordered subject pairs against all assessment rows in v0.1.0 through v0.15.0. All prior assessment identifiers LSHS-001 through LSHS-100 were present in the reviewed report history. The earliest pair cells without canonical identifiers were checked by exact subject names; the later reports were checked using their stable canonical IDs. None of the selected pairs appears in the prior ledger.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:
- Neuroscience ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-032);
- Neuroscience ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-034);
- Physiology ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-038);
- Physiology ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-039);
- Anatomy ↔ Medicine (LSHS-002).

These do not duplicate the new Surgery pairings. The distinction is substantive:
- LSHS-101 concerns neurosurgical and spinal procedural protection of neural function, rather than neurological rehabilitation or physical therapy.
- LSHS-102 concerns physiological knowledge applied to surgical patient assessment and perioperative management, rather than general physiology in Medicine or exercise physiology in Rehabilitation Science and Physical Therapy.
- LSHS-103 concerns operative planning, structural identification, dissection, and anatomical variation, rather than the use of anatomy in general medical education and clinical assessment alone.

Direction is not presumed to be symmetrical:
- For LSHS-101, the primary applied direction is neuroscience-informed surgical planning and functional monitoring; surgical observations and outcomes provide applied research questions but do not create a general dependency.
- For LSHS-102, the primary applied direction is physiology-informed surgical care; the operative context exposes physiological questions and requires coordinated multidisciplinary interpretation.
- For LSHS-103, the primary applied direction is anatomy-informed operative planning and execution; surgical cases provide applied anatomical problems and feedback.

None of these proposals is a hierarchy edge, dependency, enablement relationship, or claim that all operations require the same level of input from the corresponding Life Sciences discipline.
