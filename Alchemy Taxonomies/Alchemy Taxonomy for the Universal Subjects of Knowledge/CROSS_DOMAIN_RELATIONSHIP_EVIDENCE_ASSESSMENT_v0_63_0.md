## 1. Scope and safeguards

This assessment continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences programme using the three pairs identified in the v0.62.0 reconciled remaining inventory:

- Life Sciences ↔ Rehabilitation Science
- Biology ↔ Health Sciences
- Biology ↔ Pharmacology

The assessment uses an exact unordered-pair boundary. Evidence that a general scientific field is relevant to a health discipline does not, by itself, establish a new RD-P relationship. Promotion requires an identifiable contribution from each subject, a stable operational boundary, and outputs or questions that are not adequately represented by already-assessed specialist relationships.

The assessment distinguishes:

- **Life Sciences ↔ Rehabilitation Science:** rehabilitation research is not limited to one clinical profession or intervention. The NIH research framework describes rehabilitation research as the study of mechanisms, interventions, and methods that improve, restore, or replace function, including body-system function, activities, participation, and quality of life. Its translational-science objectives explicitly call for cell-, tissue-, and model-organism research to identify physiological mechanisms and targets; investigation of biological and physiological adaptations; biomarkers; and bench-to-bedside translation. Cochrane Rehabilitation's global research definition describes rehabilitation as a multimodal, person-centred, collaborative process directed at body structures, functions, activities, participation, and contextual factors. The resulting interface is broader than Life Sciences ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-237): the latter is bounded to integrated biological response during physical-therapy interventions, whereas this pair concerns the wider rehabilitation-science research cycle across mechanisms, translational models, multiple rehabilitation modalities, functional outcomes, participation, and research-to-practice translation. These sources establish the existence and scope of the interface; they do not prove that any particular biomarker, mechanism, or biological intervention is clinically validated or effective.
- **Biology ↔ Health Sciences:** the Health Sciences root is an umbrella over multiple distinct subject areas, not one operational method or homogeneous practice. Biology is relevant to many of those subjects, but a general statement that health disciplines use biological knowledge does not identify one additional, independently testable relationship across the full Health Sciences scope. The programme already contains narrower specialist pairs and broader, bounded relationships, including Life Sciences ↔ Medicine (LSHS-225), Biology ↔ Public Health (LSHS-226), and Life Sciences ↔ Public Health (LSHS-234), as well as many discipline-specific Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences pairs. A new Biology ↔ Health Sciences edge at the full root scope would restate those interfaces unless a distinct method, decision boundary, or output can be identified across the root domains. No such independent boundary is established in this pass.
- **Biology ↔ Pharmacology:** there is a direct and fundamental connection: pharmacology studies drug action on biological systems and organisms. However, the fact that pharmacology investigates biological systems does not by itself establish an additional RD-P relationship with the entire Biology subject. The concrete mechanisms and methods are already more precisely represented by specialist pairings such as Genetics, Molecular Biology, Cell Biology, Physiology, Immunology, Microbiology, Virology, Mycology, Neuroscience, Anatomy, Botany, Marine Biology, Zoology, Evolutionary Biology, and Biotechnology with Pharmacology. The integrative systems-level relationship is also already assessed separately as Life Sciences ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-233), including quantitative systems pharmacology, integrated biological mechanisms, multi-omics, living-system phenotypes, and drug-development decisions. The current evidence therefore establishes broad subject overlap and foundational relevance, but not an additional independently bounded Biology-specific interface beyond those existing records.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. It does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion applies to the broad scope examined in this pass; it does not rule out a narrower future pair-specific proposal if new evidence establishes a distinct contribution. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

All 62 preceding assessment snapshots, v0.1.0 through v0.62.0, were re-established for this pass and reconciled using exact unordered subject-pair matching. The v0.62.0 report is the latest reconciled ledger: it records 242 unique screened pairs, assessment IDs through LSHS-242, and the three selected pairs in its 28-pair unassessed remainder. The three exact pairs were cross-checked against that historical assessment sequence and were not found as previously assessed pairs. New IDs LSHS-243 through LSHS-245 are therefore assigned in sequence; no prior assessment ID is reused.

Related but non-identical historical records remain distinct:

- **Life Sciences ↔ Rehabilitation Science:** Life Sciences ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-237) addresses integrated biological response during PT rehabilitation. LSHS-243 addresses the broader rehabilitation-science research discipline: mechanisms, intervention development, multimodal rehabilitation research, functioning, activity, participation, context, and translational evaluation. The pair is not a relabelling of Physical Therapy.
- **Biology ↔ Health Sciences:** Life Sciences ↔ Medicine (LSHS-225), Biology ↔ Public Health (LSHS-226), and Life Sciences ↔ Public Health (LSHS-234) are distinct bounded relationships. LSHS-244 is not promoted because a full-root pairing would aggregate those and numerous specialist interfaces without a separately identifiable operational boundary.
- **Biology ↔ Pharmacology:** Molecular Biology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-008) is a different exact pair and was not promoted in its earlier assessment. Life Sciences ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-233) is a broader but specifically bounded systems-level relationship. LSHS-245 is not promoted because the general Biology contribution is intrinsic to pharmacology's definition and the more specific contributions are already represented by the specialist pairings.

No assessment in this subgroup creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. The one candidate recommendation is supplemental evidence only and does not amend the canonical taxonomy.
