## 2. Method and decision rule

Each pair is assessed for evidence of a specific interface between the two canonical subject scopes. Evidence that plants, marine organisms, or ecological systems influence health is not treated as sufficient by itself; the relationship must be bounded to an identifiable research, clinical, nutritional, or public-health interface.

The review distinguishes:
- a relationship between distinct canonical subjects from shared identity or equivalence;
- a bounded application from a whole-subject relationship;
- knowledge contribution from dependency or enablement;
- evidence quality from owner acceptance and canonical registration.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. Direction is recorded only where the sources support a meaningful contribution or information flow.

Nearby relationships that must remain distinct:
- Botany ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-063) concerns botanical species, edible plant diversity, and plant nutrient composition. Ecology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-067) concerns ecosystem biodiversity, ecosystem services, and food-system resilience.
- Botany ↔ Medicine (LSHS-064) concerns medicinal-plant identification and regulated botanical drug development. It is not a claim that every herbal preparation is effective or that Botany is equivalent to pharmacology or Medicine.
- Botany ↔ Public Health (LSHS-065) is limited to taxonomic identification in plant-exposure surveillance, toxic-plant risk assessment, education, and prevention. It is not a general statement that botany determines public health.
- Marine Biology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-066) is not a repeat of Marine Biology ↔ Medicine or Marine Biology ↔ Public Health; the subject pair and the nutritional-food-resource scope are distinct.
