# Alchemy Assessment Methodology

**Status:** Sections 1–3 — working draft; Section 3 added for review  
**Governing source:** Alchemy Taxonomy for the Universal Subjects of Knowledge, version 1.13.8  
**Scope:** Base-level assessment and development of the Universal Subjects of Knowledge taxonomy

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

The purpose of the Alchemy Assessment Methodology is to establish a consistent, evidence-based method for investigating, evaluating, validating, and developing the Alchemy Taxonomy of the Universal Subjects of Knowledge.

The methodology applies Alchemy as the governing investigative and developmental method. It examines each Universal Subject and its corresponding Subject Alchemy in terms of its identity, definition, composition, organization, boundaries, functions, relationships, and appropriate applications.

The objective is not merely to determine whether the existing taxonomy is correct. It is also to identify and address substantiated omissions, inconsistencies, ambiguities, insufficient distinctions, incomplete relationships, and opportunities for legitimate expansion.

### 1.2 Governing Alchemical Principle

Every assessment must use the working root definition of Alchemy stated in Section 2 of the governing Alchemy Taxonomy for the Universal Subjects of Knowledge, version 1.13.8. The taxonomy identifies this wording and its implications as subject to review; assessments must preserve that status and must not represent the wording as permanently settled.

**Canonical working root definition (verbatim):**

> Alchemy is the bounded, purposeful process through which a specified subject, domain, system, process, capability, relationship, resource, or state is identified and understood; its relevant constituents and conditions are classified and organized; its relationships and constraints are examined; and defined operations are applied to develop, integrate, maintain, protect, recover, renew, or transform it toward a coherent and usable condition. The resulting state is evaluated against its stated purpose, boundaries, and available evidence.

Assessments must apply this working definition as the current governing baseline while recording any evidence-supported ambiguity, limitation, or proposed refinement separately. No assessment may silently replace or permanently settle the canonical wording.

Assessment must therefore investigate the actual subject rather than simply assign it a label, compare its name with other subjects, or assume that its existing description is complete.

### 1.3 Base-Level Scope

All assessment and developmental work under this methodology is restricted to the Base-level development of the Universal Subjects of Knowledge taxonomy.

Within this boundary, assessments may investigate and develop:

1. Universal Subject identities and definitions.
2. Subject-Alchemy identities and their individual characteristics.
3. Subject composition, constituents, and internal organization.
4. Canonical subject hierarchies and parent-child relationships.
5. Subject boundaries, distinctions, and areas of overlap.
6. Functions, purposes, responsibilities, and applicable operations.
7. Relationships between distinct Universal Subjects and their Alchemies.
8. Dependencies, enabling relationships, and relevant interactions.
9. Contextual mappings and context-specific expressions.
10. Evidence, sources, definitions, and supporting research.
11. Missing or insufficiently developed taxonomy content.
12. Evidence-supported additions, refinements, and corrections.
13. Independent recording of mapping, expression authoring, review, evidence assessment, owner acceptance, implementation, testing, verification, and publication authorization, with canonical source provenance.

Expansion must strengthen the taxonomy's coverage, accuracy, clarity, coherence, and practical usefulness while preserving the identity and integrity of its established subjects.

### 1.4 Expansion Requirements

An assessment must not assume that the existing taxonomy is complete merely because a subject, hierarchy, relationship, or contextual mapping has already been recorded.

Where an assessment identifies a potential improvement, it must investigate whether that improvement is justified.

Potential expansions may include:

- Missing or insufficiently represented subject constituents.
- More precise or complete subject definitions.
- Necessary distinctions between related subjects.
- Previously unrecorded but substantiated relationships.
- Missing hierarchy relationships supported by the authoritative subject source.
- Additional explanatory or contextual detail.
- Corrections to inconsistencies or unsupported classifications.

No proposed addition becomes canonical solely because it appears plausible or improves apparent completeness. Its rationale, supporting evidence, relationship to existing taxonomy content, and limitations must be recorded and reviewed.

### 1.5 Preservation of Canonical Authority

The owner-supplied Universal Subjects of Knowledge hierarchy remains authoritative for canonical subject names and supplied parent-child placement.

Assessments may identify potential corrections or improvements, but must distinguish proposed changes from established taxonomy content.

No assessment may silently:

- Rename or redefine a canonical subject.
- Merge distinct subject identities.
- Duplicate an existing canonical Subject Alchemy.
- Change an established hierarchy relationship.
- Convert a proposed relationship into an accepted relationship.
- Treat an unrecorded relationship as proof that no relationship exists.
- Present an unsupported interpretation as an established finding.

Any proposed canonical change must retain its original state, rationale, supporting evidence, and review disposition. Where contextual records are maintained separately from the taxonomy, each record must retain the exact canonical source version and immutable source-file identifier (Git blob SHA), so its provenance remains traceable when the record is exported or separated from its companion documentation.

### 1.6 Evidence and Finding Distinctions

Every assessment must distinguish among:

- **Established:** Information already established in the authoritative taxonomy or its accepted source material.
- **Evidence-supported:** A finding supported by identifiable and traceable evidence.
- **Proposed:** A potential addition, correction, interpretation, or relationship awaiting the required review and acceptance.
- **Hypothetical:** A possibility being considered for investigation but not established by evidence.
- **Unresolved:** A question or finding for which the available information is insufficient to reach a justified conclusion.
- **Rejected:** A proposal or interpretation examined and found unsupported, inconsistent, or otherwise unsuitable, with its rationale recorded.

These distinctions must not be collapsed into a single completion or approval status.

### 1.7 Scope Exclusions and Level Boundary

This methodology is limited to Base-level assessment and development. It does not introduce or develop Meta-level or higher-level methodological frameworks, stages, or systems at this time.

This level boundary concerns the level of methodological development; it does not remove, rename, or exclude any canonical Universal Subject supplied in the taxonomy. In particular, a canonical subject whose name contains the word “Meta,” including Meta-Knowledge Sciences, remains within the taxonomy's subject inventory and is assessed as a Universal Subject at the scope governed by this methodology. Its canonical inclusion does not authorize expansion of this methodology into a Meta-level framework.

All additions and findings must remain within the Base-level subject knowledge and taxonomy development described in this section.

### 1.8 Governing Objective

The governing objective of every assessment is:

**To use Alchemy to investigate, understand, validate, refine, and legitimately expand the Universal Subjects of Knowledge taxonomy at the Base level, producing a more accurate, complete, coherent, distinguishable, evidence-supported, and usable body of organized knowledge without exceeding its authorized scope.**


## 2. Alchemical Assessment Principles

Every assessment conducted under this methodology must apply the working root definition of Alchemy stated in Section 2 of the governing taxonomy, version 1.13.8, while preserving the taxonomy's explicit statement that the wording and implications remain subject to review. These principles translate that working definition into consistent requirements for investigating, evaluating, refining, and expanding the Universal Subjects of Knowledge. Any challenge or proposed refinement must be recorded as a distinct finding; this methodology does not independently settle or amend the canonical definition.

The principles govern the quality and conduct of assessment. They do not establish new Alchemical stages, independent classification systems, or higher-level methodological frameworks.

### 2.1 Subject Identity and Fidelity

Every assessment must begin with the actual Universal Subject of Knowledge being examined.

The assessment must:

1. Preserve the exact canonical subject name and stable identifier.
2. Identify its corresponding Subject Alchemy.
3. Preserve its supplied definition, distinguishing that definition from any subsequent interpretation or proposed expansion.
4. Identify its canonical parent, children, and other established hierarchy memberships.
5. Maintain its distinct identity in relation to other Universal Subjects and Subject Alchemies.

A subject must not be redefined merely to make it fit an existing classification, relationship, or proposed organizational structure.

### 2.2 Bounded Purpose and Scope

Every assessment must establish what it is investigating and why.

The assessment must identify:

- The specific subject or Subject Alchemy under examination.
- The question, deficiency, ambiguity, or opportunity being investigated.
- The boundaries of the subject and the assessment.
- The intended result of the investigation.
- The conditions and limitations that affect the assessment.

An assessment must remain sufficiently bounded to make its findings understandable, traceable, and independently reviewable.

A broad subject may require several distinct investigations. Their findings must remain identifiable rather than being combined into an unsupported general conclusion.

### 2.3 Composition and Constituents

Assessment must investigate the actual constituents of a subject rather than treating its canonical name as a complete description.

Depending on the nature of the subject, relevant constituents may include:

1. Knowledge and information.
2. Concepts, definitions, and principles.
3. Objects, entities, and resources.
4. Methods, techniques, and processes.
5. Structures, classifications, and organizational relationships.
6. Functions, purposes, and responsibilities.
7. Conditions, constraints, and dependencies.
8. Inputs, outputs, and resulting effects.

These are investigative considerations, not a mandatory assertion that every subject contains every listed type of constituent.

Each constituent must be included because it is relevant to the actual subject and supported by an appropriate rationale.

### 2.4 Classification and Organization

Assessment must examine whether the subject's constituents are classified and organized accurately and coherently.

This includes examining:

- Whether concepts are defined sufficiently to distinguish them.
- Whether classifications reflect meaningful differences.
- Whether subordinate subjects belong under their recorded parent.
- Whether related but distinct subjects have been improperly combined.
- Whether a concept has been duplicated under different identities.
- Whether important constituents or distinctions are missing.
- Whether the existing organization creates ambiguity or inconsistency.

Any proposed classification or organizational change must retain its rationale, evidence, affected identities, and review status.

An apparent gap must be investigated before it is treated as a genuine omission.

### 2.5 Hierarchical Integrity and Distinction

The owner-supplied Universal Subjects hierarchy remains authoritative for canonical names and supplied parent-child placement.

Assessment must preserve the distinction between:

- A parent subject and its subordinate subjects.
- A subject and its corresponding Subject Alchemy.
- A shared canonical identity and a duplicate identity.
- A hierarchical relationship and a separate relationship between distinct subjects.
- A subject's established identity and a proposed interpretation of that identity.

A parent does not absorb or replace its children. A shared identity does not become multiple canonical identities merely because it appears in more than one hierarchy.

Where a hierarchy appears incomplete, inconsistent, or unsuitable, the assessment must document the issue and identify the evidence or owner direction required to resolve it. It must not silently alter canonical placement.

### 2.6 Relationships and Interdependencies

Assessment must investigate relevant relationships between Universal Subjects and their corresponding Subject Alchemies.

It must distinguish established relationships from relationships that are proposed, inferred, hypothetical, or unresolved.

Relationship classifications must follow the governing taxonomy's existing relationship codebook. These include:

- SH — Shared canonical identity.
- HI — Established hierarchy.
- RD-P — Proposed related but distinct.
- DP — Dependency.
- EN — Enables or supports.
- NS — No direct relationship stated in the current source.
- UA — Unassessed.

These classifications must not be treated as interchangeable.

In particular:

1. A shared identity is not a relationship between duplicate subject identities.
2. A hierarchy relationship does not establish that two subjects are equivalent.
3. A proposed related-but-distinct relationship remains proposed until the required review and acceptance.
4. A dependency or enabling relationship requires a supportable explanation of what depends on, or is enabled by, what.
5. NS is not proof that a relationship does not exist.
6. UA must remain distinct from a completed assessment that found no direct relationship in the available source.

Every proposed relationship must identify the subjects involved, the relationship rationale, supporting sources, relevant distinctions, limitations, and acceptance state.

### 2.7 Contextual Specificity

Every Subject Alchemy is mapped across the 22 canonical contexts established in the governing taxonomy.

Assessment must preserve both:

- The subject's canonical identity, which remains consistent across contexts.
- The context-specific expression, which must reflect the actual responsibilities, operations, relationships, permissions, and boundaries of that context.

A mapping establishes the required subject–context intersection. It does not establish that the intersection has a completed contextual definition or that an implementation exists.

Contextual expressions must be individually reasoned and must not be produced by mechanically repeating generic descriptions across different contexts.

The same subject may have different contextual responsibilities without becoming a different canonical subject Alchemy.

### 2.8 Operations, Conditions, and Results

Assessment must examine the operations appropriate to the subject and the conditions under which they are applied.

The investigation should establish, where relevant:

1. What the subject receives or uses as inputs.
2. What operations or processes are performed.
3. What conditions, constraints, or dependencies affect those operations.
4. What outputs, changes, or effects result.
5. Whether the resulting condition is coherent and usable for its stated purpose.
6. What evidence supports that evaluation.

Operations must be derived from the actual subject and its purpose. They must not be assigned merely because they appear in a generic description of Alchemy.

A proposed operation must remain distinguishable from an operation already established or implemented.

### 2.9 Evidence, Uncertainty, and Proportional Conclusions

Every substantive finding must be supported by identifiable evidence or explicitly marked as awaiting evidence.

Assessment must distinguish:

- What the canonical taxonomy already establishes.
- What supplied source material establishes.
- What external or additional evidence supports.
- What the assessment has independently examined.
- What remains proposed, hypothetical, or unresolved.
- What has been rejected and why.

Conclusions must be proportionate to the evidence available.

An absence of evidence must not be presented as evidence of absence. A plausible interpretation must not be represented as a verified fact. A source's statement must not automatically be treated as independent confirmation of that statement.

Where sources conflict, the assessment must preserve the disagreement, identify the affected claims, and document the basis for any proposed resolution.

### 2.10 Legitimate Expansion and Improvement

The methodology explicitly permits the Base-level expansion and improvement of the Universal Subjects of Knowledge taxonomy.

Assessments may identify and develop justified:

- Definitions and explanatory detail.
- Subject constituents and internal distinctions.
- Classifications and organizational improvements.
- Relationships and dependencies.
- Missing or insufficiently represented knowledge.
- Clarifications of scope and boundaries.
- Contextual expressions and operational descriptions.
- Corrections to demonstrable inconsistencies.

Every proposed addition or correction must identify:

1. The existing taxonomy content affected.
2. The issue or opportunity being addressed.
3. The proposed change or addition.
4. Its rationale and supporting evidence.
5. Its relationship to existing canonical subjects and classifications.
6. Any uncertainties, limitations, or competing interpretations.
7. Its proposed status and required review or owner acceptance.

Expansion must strengthen the taxonomy without introducing unsupported subjects, duplicating established identities, or silently changing canonical authority.

### 2.11 Traceability and Change Integrity

Every assessment must produce a traceable account of what was examined and what was concluded.

Records must preserve, as applicable:

- The governing taxonomy version and exact source identity, including the source-file Git blob SHA where a register or other detached record is maintained.
- The assessed subject and stable identifier.
- The assessment question and scope.
- The sources and evidence examined.
- The methods and reasoning applied.
- Established findings and proposed changes.
- Unresolved questions and limitations.
- Review and acceptance outcomes.
- The resulting changes and their version history.

Changes to shared taxonomy content must remain synchronized across the four authorized repositories.

Repository-specific implementation details must remain within their appropriate OS or App responsibility and edition boundaries. C2O private-source information must not be disclosed through UNIVERSALIS public-facing material.

### 2.12 Separation of Assessment and Completion States

An assessment must not treat investigation, documentation, review, acceptance, implementation, testing, verification, and publication authorization as a single completion state.

Each state must be recorded independently and supported by its own evidence or approval. Where the Contextual Expression Register is used, testing and verification must also be recorded in their dedicated `testing_status` and `verification_status` fields. Their initial values, `Not yet tested` and `Not yet verified`, indicate that no outcome has been recorded; they do not imply that either activity has occurred. A testing outcome does not automatically establish verification, and neither state substitutes for implementation, review, evidence assessment, owner acceptance, or publication authorization.

For example:

- A subject may be mapped without having a completed contextual expression.
- A contextual expression may be authored without having been reviewed.
- A finding may be evidence-supported without having received owner acceptance.
- A proposal may be accepted without being implemented.
- An implementation may exist without having passed verification.
- A completed technical review does not itself authorize publication.

No assessment may claim a higher state of completion than its recorded evidence supports.

### 2.13 Governing Principle

**Every assessment must investigate the actual subject, preserve its canonical identity, understand its relevant constituents and conditions, examine its classifications and relationships, evaluate its appropriate operations and results, and use traceable evidence to justify any conclusion or proposed expansion.**

The purpose of these principles is to ensure that the Universal Subjects of Knowledge taxonomy develops through accurate, distinguishable, coherent, evidence-supported, and controlled Base-level assessment.


## 3. Assessment Structure and Required Records

Every assessment must be organized as a traceable set of records that identifies the subject under examination, defines the investigation, preserves the evidence and reasoning used, records each finding and its limitations, and distinguishes the separate states of review, acceptance, implementation, testing, verification, and publication authorization.

The records must be sufficiently complete for another reviewer to understand what was examined, reproduce or challenge the reasoning where practicable, identify the exact canonical source used, and determine which conclusions are established, proposed, hypothetical, unresolved, or rejected.

An assessment may use one document or several linked records. The format is flexible; the required information and separation of states are not. A register entry is an index or tracking record and does not replace the substantive evidence, review, implementation, test, verification, or authorization record to which it refers.

### 3.1 Assessment identity and control record

Every assessment must have a stable assessment identity and a control record containing, as applicable:

1. A unique assessment identifier that is not reused for another assessment.
2. An assessment title and concise statement of the matter being examined.
3. The assessment version or revision identifier.
4. The assessment's current work state and the date on which that state was recorded.
5. The responsible assessor or contributing authors.
6. The intended reviewer and the person or authority responsible for any required owner decision, where known.
7. The assessment's creation date and material revision dates.
8. The repositories, files, branches, or other controlled locations in which the assessment and its supporting records are maintained.
9. Links or identifiers for related assessments, registers, evidence records, decisions, and change records.

The control record must not imply that an assessment is complete, accepted, implemented, verified, or authorized merely because an assessment file exists or has been updated.

### 3.2 Canonical subject identity and source-provenance record

Every assessment must identify the exact Universal Subject and Subject Alchemy under examination. The identity record must include:

1. The canonical Universal Subject name.
2. The canonical subject-Alchemy name and stable subject-Alchemy identifier.
3. The applicable top-level hierarchy and the subject's recorded parent-child placement.
4. The canonical definition or source description, where supplied.
5. Any shared hierarchy membership associated with the same canonical identity.
6. The exact canonical taxonomy version used.
7. The canonical taxonomy source-file path and immutable Git blob SHA.
8. The repository and source reference from which the canonical file was obtained, with a commit or other retrieval reference where available.
9. Any known source limitation, discrepancy, or unresolved provenance issue.

The canonical source version and blob SHA must identify the actual source file examined. A branch name, repository name, release label, or companion Markdown document alone is not a substitute for the immutable source-file identifier. Detached records must retain sufficient provenance to identify their canonical source without relying on undocumented conversational context.

For the current governing baseline, records derived from taxonomy version 1.13.8 must identify canonical source blob SHA `3e7c570943b4e65307f01618ac81b031051ad1a9`, unless a later authorized canonical version is explicitly adopted. The source identity must not be silently changed when an assessment is copied, exported, or synchronized.

### 3.3 Purpose, question, scope, and boundary record

Every assessment must state:

1. The specific question, issue, deficiency, ambiguity, relationship, or opportunity being investigated.
2. Why the investigation is necessary.
3. The subject matter and identities included.
4. The relevant hierarchy, context, relationship, or other boundaries.
5. What is expressly excluded from the investigation.
6. The intended assessment output or decision to be informed.
7. The assumptions, dependencies, and constraints that may affect the work.
8. The conditions under which the assessment would need to be revisited.

The scope must be sufficiently precise to prevent findings from being generalized beyond the subject, evidence, or conditions actually examined. Where a broad issue requires multiple investigations, each investigation must retain its own identifiable question and findings.

All work under this methodology remains within the Base-level boundary established in Sections 1 and 2. A canonical subject name containing the word “Meta” does not authorize a higher-level methodological framework.

### 3.4 Method, procedure, and assessment-criteria record

Every assessment must record how the investigation was conducted. As applicable, this record must identify:

1. The assessment method and the sequence of investigative steps.
2. The sources, datasets, taxonomy records, or other inputs selected for examination.
3. The inclusion and exclusion criteria applied.
4. The definitions, classification rules, relationship codebooks, and comparison criteria used.
5. Any calculations, transformations, searches, sampling, or analytical procedures performed.
6. Any tools, software, versions, configurations, or environments that materially affect the result.
7. Any assumptions, deviations from the intended procedure, or procedural limitations.
8. The criteria used to determine whether a finding is established, evidence-supported, proposed, hypothetical, unresolved, or rejected.

A method description must be specific enough to distinguish work actually performed from work merely planned. A proposed procedure, hypothetical example, or description of a possible test must not be represented as an executed assessment activity.

### 3.5 Subject-to-context mapping record

Where an assessment concerns contextual mapping, it must identify the canonical subject-Alchemy identity and the exact context identity being mapped.

For each applicable subject–context intersection, the record must preserve:

1. The canonical subject-Alchemy identifier.
2. The canonical context identifier and context name.
3. The stable contextual-expression record identifier.
4. The mapping state recorded in the Contextual Expression Register.
5. The exact canonical source version and source-file blob SHA.
6. The source matrix or other authoritative record supporting the mapping.
7. Any discrepancy between the source mapping and the assessment's observed record.

The Contextual Expression Register is the controlled index for the 264 unique subject-Alchemy identities and 22 canonical contexts, producing 5,808 unique subject–context records. Its stable record identifiers and mapping state must be preserved when records are referenced or exported.

A recorded mapping establishes that a subject–context intersection is present in the source matrix and tracked by the register. It does not establish that a contextual expression has been authored, reviewed, evidenced, accepted, implemented, tested, verified, or authorized for publication. No such state may be inferred from the mapping record alone.

### 3.6 Contextual-expression record

Where a contextual expression is within the assessment's scope, it must be recorded separately from the mapping that identifies its required subject–context intersection.

Each authored contextual expression must identify and address, as relevant to the actual subject and selected context:

1. The exact canonical subject-Alchemy identity and context identity.
2. A context-specific definition.
3. The context-specific purpose and responsibilities.
4. Scope, boundaries, and explicit exclusions.
5. Appropriate operations and processes.
6. Relevant relationships and dependencies.
7. Authority, permissions, and constraints applicable to that context.
8. Relevant inputs, outputs, and effects.
9. Supporting evidence and validation criteria.
10. Sources, limitations, uncertainties, and unresolved points.

The expression must be individually reasoned from the actual subject and context. It must not be generated by mechanically repeating a generic description across contexts. Shared canonical subject identity does not establish identical responsibilities, operations, authority, permissions, implementation, or source visibility.

The register's contextual-content fields must remain blank until the corresponding content has actually been authored. A blank field must not be filled with an invented placeholder merely to create an appearance of completion. Where an expression has not been authored, the record must retain the register's applicable not-yet-authored state.

### 3.7 Source and evidence records

Every substantive finding must link to identifiable sources or evidence records. Each evidence record must include, as applicable:

1. A stable evidence identifier.
2. The source title, author or issuing organization, and source type.
3. The source publication date, version, or other relevant date information.
4. A stable URL, repository path, document identifier, or other retrievable locator.
5. The date the source was accessed or obtained.
6. The exact page, section, line, record, dataset field, or other location supporting the claim.
7. The claim or assessment question for which the evidence is relevant.
8. The evidence actually extracted or observed, with sufficient context to avoid misrepresentation.
9. The method used to interpret, compare, or evaluate the evidence.
10. Source limitations, uncertainty, conflicts of interest, dependencies, or known reliability concerns.
11. Any conflicting or corroborating sources and whether they are genuinely independent.
12. The relationship between the evidence record and the finding it supports or challenges.

A citation or source listing without an identified supporting claim is not, by itself, a complete evidence assessment. A source's assertion must not automatically be described as independently corroborated. Evidence must support only the claims that it can reasonably establish.

### 3.8 Finding, analysis, and conclusion records

Every substantive finding must have a stable identifier and a traceable connection to the assessment question, relevant subject identity, method, sources, and evidence.

A finding record must include, as applicable:

1. The finding identifier and concise statement.
2. The affected canonical subject, Subject Alchemy, context, relationship, or taxonomy element.
3. The classification of the finding as established, evidence-supported, proposed, hypothetical, unresolved, or rejected, as defined in Section 1.6.
4. The supporting evidence identifiers and relevant source locations.
5. The reasoning that connects the evidence to the finding.
6. Any alternative interpretations or contradictory evidence considered.
7. The limitations, uncertainty, and conditions affecting the conclusion.
8. The practical or taxonomic implications.
9. Any proposed addition, correction, or further investigation.
10. The review and decision records associated with the finding, when available.

A finding's classification describes the evidential or decision state of that finding. It does not substitute for the separate review, owner-acceptance, implementation, testing, verification, or publication-authorization records.

### 3.9 Relationship and dependency records

Where an assessment examines relationships between distinct Universal Subjects or Subject Alchemies, each relationship must be recorded as an individually traceable relationship record.

The record must identify:

1. The stable canonical identifiers of both or all subjects involved.
2. The relationship classification used under the governing taxonomy's relationship codebook.
3. Directionality, where the relationship is directional.
4. The specific rationale describing what the relationship means.
5. The sources and evidence supporting or challenging the relationship.
6. The distinctions that preserve the identities of the subjects involved.
7. Any dependencies, enabling conditions, constraints, or effects relevant to the relationship.
8. The relationship's proposed, established, unresolved, or other applicable assessment state.
9. The required review and owner-acceptance state.

The existing relationship classifications must retain their defined meanings: SH (shared canonical identity), HI (established hierarchy), RD-P (proposed related but distinct), DP (dependency), EN (enables or supports), NS (no direct relationship stated in the current source), and UA (unassessed).

These codes must not be repurposed as general completion statuses. In particular, NS is not proof that no relationship exists; UA is not a completed negative assessment; and RD-P remains a proposal pending the required review and acceptance. A contextual mapping is not itself a relationship between two distinct subjects.

### 3.10 Review and owner-acceptance records

Technical or substantive review and owner acceptance must be recorded separately.

A review record must identify, as applicable:

1. The assessment, findings, expressions, or proposed changes reviewed.
2. The reviewer and review date.
3. The scope and criteria of the review.
4. The sources, evidence, and reasoning examined.
5. Issues, corrections, objections, and unresolved matters raised.
6. The review outcome and its rationale.
7. Any required follow-up and the record to which it applies.

An owner-acceptance record must independently identify:

1. The specific proposal, finding, relationship, or change submitted for owner decision.
2. The owner or authorized decision-maker.
3. The decision and date.
4. The rationale, conditions, or limitations attached to the decision.
5. Any remaining matters that have not been accepted.

A review does not itself establish owner acceptance. Owner acceptance does not by itself establish implementation, testing, verification, or publication authorization. Pending, absent, or unrecorded decisions must not be represented as approval.

### 3.11 Implementation, testing, and verification records

Implementation, testing, and verification are separate activities and must have separate records.

An implementation record must identify, as applicable, the approved requirement or accepted change, the implemented artifact, its version, the responsible implementation context, the implementation location, and the evidence showing what was actually implemented. A proposal or accepted design is not proof of implementation. The Contextual Expression Register's `implementation_status` value `NI` means implementation is not asserted by that register.

A testing record must identify:

1. The requirement, expression, artifact, or behavior tested.
2. The test identifier, purpose, and defined criteria.
3. The test procedure, inputs, and expected results.
4. The actual execution environment, tools, and versions where material.
5. The date and responsible tester.
6. The observed results and retained evidence.
7. Failures, deviations, limitations, and blocking conditions.
8. The resulting testing status.

The Contextual Expression Register's testing-status values must use its documented codebook exactly: `Not yet tested`, `Testing in progress`, `Tested — passed`, `Tested — failed`, or `Testing blocked`. A passed test must link to its supporting test record and does not automatically establish verification.

A verification record must independently identify:

1. The requirement, acceptance criterion, or evidence claim being verified.
2. The verification identifier, purpose, and defined criteria.
3. The verification method and the evidence examined.
4. The verifier and date.
5. The result, supporting record, and any limitations.
6. Failures, deviations, unresolved matters, or blocking conditions.
7. The resulting verification status.

The Contextual Expression Register's verification-status values must use its documented codebook exactly: `Not yet verified`, `Verification in progress`, `Verified`, `Verification failed`, or `Verification blocked`. Verification must be supported by its own traceable record. It must not be inferred from a passed test, a completed review, an implementation record, or the existence of an assessment document.

### 3.12 Publication-authorization and release records

Publication authorization must be recorded independently from technical review, owner acceptance, implementation, testing, and verification.

Where publication or distribution is proposed, the authorization record must identify, as applicable:

1. The exact content and version proposed for publication.
2. The intended repository, edition, audience, and distribution location.
3. The authorized decision-maker and applicable authority.
4. The decision, date, conditions, and limitations.
5. The applicable rights, licensing, confidentiality, and third-party restrictions.
6. The evidence that the approved content is the content actually prepared for release.

The Contextual Expression Register's `publication_authorization` value `P` means authorization remains pending. Presence in a repository, inclusion in a pull request, a successful technical check, or a completed review does not constitute publication authorization.

C2O private-source information must remain within its authorized private boundaries. UNIVERSALIS public-facing material must not expose private C2O implementation details, credentials, confidential operational evidence, or private policies. A release record must identify the applicable edition boundary and must not assume that authorization for one edition authorizes publication in another.

### 3.13 Change history, record linkage, and retention

Every material change to an assessment or its supporting records must be traceable.

The change record must identify, as applicable:

1. The affected assessment, finding, expression, evidence, relationship, or decision record.
2. The previous and revised versions or states.
3. What changed and why.
4. The person or process responsible for the change.
5. The date and repository commit or other immutable change reference.
6. The evidence, review, or authorization supporting the change.
7. Any related records that require reassessment because of the change.

Linked records must retain stable identifiers so that changes in one record do not silently break the traceability of another. Superseded records must remain distinguishable from current records; history must not be overwritten in a way that conceals prior findings, failures, objections, or decisions.

When shared taxonomy content is changed, the applicable synchronization and change-control requirements in Section 2.11 must be followed. Repository-specific implementation records must remain within their appropriate OS or App responsibility and edition boundaries.

### 3.14 Missing information, non-applicability, and record completeness

The absence of information must be represented accurately.

1. A blank content field means the required content has not been supplied in that field; it must not be interpreted as a negative finding.
2. A not-yet-assessed state must remain distinct from a completed assessment that reached a supported conclusion.
3. A blocked activity must identify the blocking condition and must not be reported as completed.
4. A failed test or verification must retain its result and supporting evidence; failure must not be erased by replacing it with a later successful result.
5. A field or record may be treated as not applicable only when its non-applicability is justified by the assessment's actual scope and recorded with a rationale in the appropriate assessment record. This must not be used to bypass a required canonical register field or its controlled codebook.
6. Missing sources, unavailable owner decisions, absent approvals, and unresolved questions must remain visible as such.
7. No status may be advanced merely to make an assessment appear complete or consistent with another record.

The required record set is conditional only where the activity or subject matter is genuinely outside the assessment's scope. Where a record is required, it must either contain the relevant information or explicitly identify the outstanding information and its status. A summary, hypothetical example, planned activity, or unsupported assertion does not satisfy a required record.

### 3.15 Minimum assessment record set

At minimum, every assessment must contain or explicitly link to:

1. Assessment identity and control information.
2. Canonical subject identity and exact source provenance.
3. Purpose, question, scope, and exclusions.
4. Method and assessment criteria.
5. Relevant mapping and context identifiers, where contextual mapping is in scope.
6. Contextual-expression records, where expression authorship is in scope.
7. Source and evidence records supporting substantive findings.
8. Individually identifiable findings, reasoning, and limitations.
9. Relationship records, where relationships are examined.
10. Review records and separate owner-acceptance records, where required.
11. Separate implementation, testing, and verification records, where those activities are undertaken or claimed.
12. Publication-authorization records, where publication or distribution is proposed.
13. Change history and links to related records.

The minimum set does not require an assessment to claim that every possible activity has occurred. It requires the assessment to identify the applicable activities, preserve the evidence for any activity claimed as completed, and leave unperformed, pending, blocked, or unresolved activities accurately represented.

**Governing requirement:** An assessment is traceable only when its subject identity, canonical source, investigative method, evidence, findings, limitations, and applicable decisions can be followed through linked records. Mapping is not expression; expression is not review; review is not owner acceptance; acceptance is not implementation; implementation is not testing; testing is not verification; and none of these states independently grants publication authorization.
