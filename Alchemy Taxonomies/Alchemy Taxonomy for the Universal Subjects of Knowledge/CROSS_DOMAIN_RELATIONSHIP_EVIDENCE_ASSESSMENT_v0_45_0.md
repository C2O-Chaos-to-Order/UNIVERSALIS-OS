## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. The selected subgroup examines biological research interfaces with Rehabilitation Science, including gut-microbiome research after spinal cord injury, molecular and multi-omics monitoring of rehabilitation interventions, and marine-derived biomaterials in regenerative medicine.

The assessment distinguishes three scopes:

- **Microbiology ↔ Rehabilitation Science:** gut microbial communities, microbial metabolites, microbiome changes after spinal cord injury, and their possible relationship with motor recovery and rehabilitation. The evidence supports an active research interface, but causal mechanisms and human clinical utility remain unestablished. It does not establish that microbiome modification improves rehabilitation outcomes or that rehabilitation reliably restores gut dysbiosis.
- **Molecular Biology ↔ Rehabilitation Science:** molecular profiling and multi-omics methods—including transcriptomics, epigenomics, proteomics, metabolomics, and metagenomics—used to investigate biological responses to rehabilitation interventions and monitor recovery after spinal cord injury. The relationship is research-oriented; it does not establish validated individual treatment-selection biomarkers or routine clinical decision rules.
- **Marine Biology ↔ Rehabilitation Science:** marine-derived decellularized extracellular matrices and other marine biomaterials used in tissue engineering, wound healing, and regenerative medicine. The reviewed literature establishes marine-source material and regenerative-medicine interfaces, but does not sufficiently establish a distinct contribution from Rehabilitation Science through rehabilitation intervention design, functional assessment, participation outcomes, or rehabilitation study frameworks. Marine-derived biomaterials are not by themselves evidence of a Marine Biology ↔ Rehabilitation Science relationship.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not establish that no narrower future relationship could ever be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.

- **Molecular Biology:** Molecular Biology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-018), Preventive Medicine (LSHS-023), Surgery (LSHS-031), Nursing (LSHS-097), Dentistry (LSHS-098; not promoted), Epidemiology (LSHS-093), Public Health (LSHS-094), Nutrition (LSHS-124), Physical Therapy (LSHS-133), and Emergency Medicine (LSHS-164) concern other exact pairs. Molecular Biology ↔ Rehabilitation Science is distinct from Molecular Biology ↔ Physical Therapy: this assessment addresses molecular/omics methods in rehabilitation research and functional outcome monitoring, not one profession's intervention practice.
- **Marine Biology:** Marine Biology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-060), Public Health (LSHS-061), Preventive Medicine (LSHS-062), Nutrition (LSHS-066), Epidemiology (LSHS-119), Pharmacology (LSHS-121), Emergency Medicine (LSHS-162), and Mental Health (LSHS-179; not promoted) address other exact pair scopes. None is Marine Biology ↔ Rehabilitation Science. The present non-promotion distinguishes marine-derived biomaterials and regenerative medicine from a direct rehabilitation-science interface.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-189 is reciprocal as a research interface: microbial ecology and host–microbe mechanisms inform hypotheses about recovery and rehabilitation response, while rehabilitation interventions and functional trajectories provide contexts and outcomes for microbiological investigation.
- LSHS-190 is reciprocal as a research interface: molecular profiles can characterize biological responses to interventions, while rehabilitation study designs and functional outcomes shape which molecular measurements are meaningful and clinically testable.
- LSHS-191 has no promoted direction or subtype. The available marine biomaterial sources do not establish a distinct Rehabilitation Science contribution.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim. This assessment does not claim microbiome-directed therapy improves human motor recovery, convert exploratory omics signatures into clinical decision rules, or equate tissue engineering with rehabilitation science.
