## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed microbial-discipline interfaces with Mental Health, selected from the reconciled inventory of 115 remaining pairs:

- Virology ↔ Mental Health: mental-health outcomes and clinical needs reported after SARS-CoV-2 infection, including depression, anxiety, trauma-related symptoms, and other psychiatric or neurocognitive outcomes.
- Microbiology ↔ Mental Health: the research interface between gut microbial ecology, microbiome–gut–brain communication, and psychiatric conditions, particularly major depressive disorder.
- Mycology ↔ Mental Health: examined as a candidate but not promoted; acute fungal central-nervous-system infection can produce altered mental status and other neuropsychiatric manifestations, but those manifestations alone do not establish a stable disciplinary interface with Mental Health.

The two candidate relationships concern different bodies of evidence. Virology ↔ Mental Health is supported by clinical and epidemiological literature on mental-health outcomes following COVID-19 and by clinical guidance addressing mental-health symptoms in post-COVID-19 condition. Microbiology ↔ Mental Health is supported by research on the gut microbiome and the microbiome–gut–brain axis. These do not establish a universal viral cause of psychiatric disorders or a clinically validated microbiome treatment for depression.

The Mycology ↔ Mental Health pair is explicitly not promoted. Fungal meningitis can cause confusion, altered mental status, hallucinations, and personality changes. These findings are important manifestations of an acute, potentially life-threatening central-nervous-system infection. They primarily support infectious-disease, neurological, and medical assessment and management. The presence of altered mental status during an acute infection is not, by itself, evidence of a general Mycology ↔ Mental Health disciplinary relationship. This assessment does not deny that people with serious fungal infections may also need psychological or psychiatric support; such supportive care is not specific enough to establish the proposed pair.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory contains 18 Life Sciences subjects and 15 Health Sciences subjects, producing 270 possible cross-domain pairs. The preceding assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.33.0 record 155 unique screened pairs, 141 candidate recommendations, and 14 screened but not promoted or deferred, leaving 115 individually unassessed pairs before this batch.

Each selected pair was checked as an unordered subject pair against all 33 prior assessment snapshots, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. The historical assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-155 were reconciled. None of the three selected pairs appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. A non-promotion is specific to the scope and evidence assessed in this pass; it does not establish that no future narrower relationship could ever be supported. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

The three selected pairs were checked as unordered subject pairs against all 33 prior assessment snapshots, v0.1.0 through v0.33.0. The reconciled prior ledger contains 155 unique screened pairs with assessment IDs LSHS-001 through LSHS-155. Exact normalized-pair checks found no occurrence of Virology ↔ Mental Health, Microbiology ↔ Mental Health, or Mycology ↔ Mental Health in the prior ledger. Canonical subject names and identifiers were checked against taxonomy v1.13.8.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- Neuroscience ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-005) concerns neural systems and neurobiological contributions to mental-health conditions. Virology ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-156) concerns mental-health outcomes and support after a defined viral infection history; it does not duplicate a neuroscience mechanism relationship.
- Immunology ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-141) concerns immune dysregulation, inflammation, and selected immune-associated psychiatric research. LSHS-156 concerns infection-associated outcomes and clinical context; it does not claim that the post-COVID mental-health relationship is necessarily immune-mediated.
- Genetics ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-126), Molecular Biology ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-127), and Cell Biology ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-128) concern genetic, molecular, and cellular research interfaces. Microbiology ↔ Mental Health (LSHS-157) is specifically limited to microbial ecology, gut microbial function, and microbiome–gut–brain research.
- Virology ↔ Medicine (LSHS-010), Virology ↔ Emergency Medicine (LSHS-044), Virology ↔ Public Health (LSHS-043), Virology ↔ Preventive Medicine (LSHS-015), Virology ↔ Nursing (LSHS-045), Virology ↔ Pharmacology (LSHS-109), Virology ↔ Dentistry (LSHS-150), Virology ↔ Surgery (LSHS-151), Virology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-152), Virology ↔ Physical Therapy (LSHS-153), Virology ↔ Occupational Therapy (LSHS-154), and Virology ↔ Rehabilitation Science (LSHS-155) concern other Health Sciences applications. None is the exact Mental Health interface assessed in LSHS-156.
- Fungal meningitis is a medical and infectious-disease condition that can involve neurological and mental-status changes. The current evidence does not establish that these acute manifestations are a distinct Mycology ↔ Mental Health relationship. The Mycology ↔ Mental Health non-promotion in LSHS-158 does not undo or contradict the previously assessed Mycology relationships with Medicine, Dentistry, Emergency Medicine, Nursing, Preventive Medicine, Pharmacology, Epidemiology, Public Health, or Surgery.
- Mental-health symptoms after viral infection and microbial research concerning psychiatric phenotypes do not mean that all psychiatric conditions have an infectious cause. Psychosocial effects of illness, pre-existing risk, medications, comorbidities, social determinants, and other non-virological factors remain relevant.

Direction and scope:

- LSHS-156 is reciprocal within the bounded post-COVID interface: Virology supplies infection and pathogen context; Mental Health evaluates and manages relevant psychological and psychiatric outcomes. Neither field contains or universally depends on the other.
- LSHS-157 is reciprocal within the bounded microbiome research interface: Microbiology characterizes microbial communities and function; Mental Health supplies clinical phenotypes and research outcomes. The proposed relation is research-oriented and does not imply clinical efficacy of microbiome-targeted therapy.
- LSHS-158 has no promoted direction or subtype. The available evidence does not justify registering the broad pair.

None of these decisions creates a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim.
