# Alchemy Taxonomy for the Universal Subjects of Knowledge

**Version:** 1.7.0  
**Status:** Sixteen supplied Universal Subject hierarchies recorded; 226 unique subject Alchemies mapped across 22 contexts; detailed contextual expressions pending  
**Coverage:** 22 C2O and UNIVERSALIS contexts

## 1. Purpose

The Alchemy Taxonomy for the Universal Subjects of Knowledge establishes a consistent classification system for the bounded processes, domains, responsibilities, and transformations represented by Alchemy across the C2O and UNIVERSALIS ecosystem.

This foundational release establishes the taxonomy's scope, terminology, context register, classification requirements, and validation rules. It does not presume that an unreviewed domain or specialized classification is already approved.

## 2. Foundational definition

**Alchemy** is the bounded, purposeful process through which a specified subject, domain, system, process, capability, relationship, resource, or state is identified and understood; its relevant constituents and conditions are classified and organized; its relationships and constraints are examined; and defined operations are applied to develop, integrate, maintain, protect, recover, renew, or transform it toward a coherent and usable condition. The resulting state is evaluated against its stated purpose, boundaries, and available evidence.

This is the working root definition for the taxonomy. Its wording and implications remain subject to review before being treated as permanently settled.

## 3. What the taxonomy classifies

The taxonomy distinguishes four foundational concepts:

1. **Universal Subject of Knowledge** — a canonical subject supplied within the Universal Subjects of Knowledge hierarchy. Its name, definition, and parent-child placement are preserved.
2. **Subject Alchemy** — the distinct Alchemical identity established for each Universal Subject of Knowledge. Every supplied subject, including parent subjects and subordinate subjects, becomes its own Alchemy.
3. **Context** — one of the 22 canonical C2O and UNIVERSALIS contexts established in Section 4. Contexts are the fixed matrix dimension through which every subject Alchemy is mapped.
4. **Contextual expression** — the specific definition, purpose, responsibilities, operations, relationships, dependencies, and boundaries of a subject Alchemy within one of the 22 contexts.

An operating system, application, intelligence, assistant, or guardian is not made into an additional subject Alchemy merely because it is a context. It remains a context dimension unless it is separately supplied and established as a Universal Subject of Knowledge.

## 5. Governing Universal Subject-to-Alchemy Rule

**Every Universal Subject of Knowledge becomes an Alchemy. Every such Alchemy is mapped and matrixed across the complete canonical 22-context register.**

This is the governing construction rule of this taxonomy. A Universal Subject of Knowledge is not merely a topic label, descriptive tag, example, or reference inside an Alchemy. Each subject is established as its own identifiable Alchemy while retaining its canonical subject name, meaning, position, and parent-child relationships in the Universal Subjects of Knowledge hierarchy.

The canonical naming form is **[Canonical Universal Subject] Alchemy**. The subject's identity is not replaced by the word *Alchemy*; the suffix establishes its Alchemical expression while preserving the source subject.

The rule applies recursively throughout the subject hierarchy:

1. Every top-level Universal Subject of Knowledge becomes its own Alchemy.
2. Every subordinate subject becomes its own Alchemy.
3. A parent subject and each of its subordinate subjects remain distinct Alchemies with explicit hierarchical relationships.
4. Every subject Alchemy is mapped across all 22 contexts in Section 4.
5. Each of the 22 context intersections must receive a context-specific Alchemical expression. The mapping must not be omitted merely because two contexts share an underlying architecture or have a close relationship.
6. Context-specific expressions preserve the subject's identity while specifying how it is understood, organized, related, and applied in that context.
7. Mapping a subject Alchemy to a context does not create another operating system, application, or independent intelligence entity.

The subject hierarchy supplied for this taxonomy is the authoritative source for subject names and relationships. Additional subjects, removals, renamings, or hierarchy changes must come from the supplied Universal Subjects of Knowledge or explicit owner direction; they must not be invented to fill apparent gaps.

## 10. Contextual expression requirements

Each mapped intersection must be expanded through the same controlled record:

1. **Subject-Alchemy identity:** the exact canonical Alchemy represented.
2. **Context identity:** the exact context identifier and name from Section 4.
3. **Contextual definition:** what this subject Alchemy means and encompasses in this context.
4. **Purpose and responsibilities:** what it establishes, organizes, examines, supports, maintains, or transforms within the context.
5. **Scope and exclusions:** what it includes and what remains outside its responsibility.
6. **Operations and processes:** how the subject Alchemy is expressed and applied in the context.
7. **Relationships and dependencies:** how it relates to other subject Alchemies and context structures.
8. **Authority, permissions, and constraints:** the applicable boundaries of the context.
9. **Inputs, outputs, and effects:** what the Alchemy receives, produces, changes, or supports.
10. **Evidence and validation:** what supports the definition and how its expression can be evaluated.

Context-specific expressions must not be generated by mechanically copying one generic paragraph into all 22 contexts. They must be individually reasoned from the subject's actual meaning and the identity and boundaries of the selected context.

## 11. Hierarchy, relationship, and matrix integrity

1. **Subject fidelity:** Preserve the exact supplied Universal Subject names and definitions.
2. **Alchemy correspondence:** Every Universal Subject becomes a distinct Alchemy; no subject is left as a mere label or descriptive note.
3. **Hierarchical fidelity:** Preserve every parent-child relationship. A parent Alchemy does not replace or absorb its child Alchemies.
4. **Complete context coverage:** Every subject Alchemy must appear across all 22 context columns.
5. **Contextual specificity:** Every mapped intersection must receive its own context-specific expression.
6. **No false equivalence:** Shared subject identity does not imply identical context responsibilities, authority, implementation, or operations.
7. **No entity proliferation:** The mapping must not create new OSs, Apps, or intelligence actors.
8. **Relationship traceability:** Parentage, dependencies, intersections, and distinctions must be explicit and supportable.
9. **Edition separation:** C2O private-source and UNIVERSALIS public/open-source boundaries must remain intact.
10. **Evidence discipline:** Mapping, definition, implementation, testing, and verification are different states and must not be conflated.
11. **No invented subjects:** No new Universal Subjects or subordinate branches may be added without source support or explicit owner direction.
12. **Controlled changes:** Any modification to a subject, hierarchy, matrix assignment, or contextual expression must be traceable.

## 12. C2O and UNIVERSALIS edition boundaries

The taxonomy must preserve the established distinction between C2O as the private-source edition and UNIVERSALIS as the public, open-source edition.

A subject Alchemy is mapped into both C2O and UNIVERSALIS contexts as identified in the 22-context register. Its shared subject identity is preserved, while each expression must respect the specific governance, authority, source visibility, permissions, and operational boundaries of its context.

Public-facing taxonomy material must not disclose private C2O implementation details, protected information, internal policies, credentials, or private operational evidence. A public subject Alchemy must remain understandable and usable without requiring access to private C2O internals.

## 13. Review and acceptance requirements

Each subject Alchemy and each of its 22 contextual expressions must be reviewed for:

- fidelity to the supplied Universal Subject of Knowledge;
- correct canonical Alchemy name and stable identifier;
- correct placement in the subject hierarchy;
- distinct identity, purpose, scope, and boundaries;
- completeness and accuracy of the context mapping;
- specificity and coherence of each contextual expression;
- accurate relationships and dependencies;
- preserved C2O and UNIVERSALIS edition boundaries;
- evidence quality, limitations, and traceability;
- consistency with the root Alchemy definition and the remainder of the taxonomy.

A subject's presence in the matrix establishes the required mapping. It does not by itself establish that its full contextual expression has passed review or that an implementation exists.
