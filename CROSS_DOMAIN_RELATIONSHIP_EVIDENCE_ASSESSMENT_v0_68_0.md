## 1. Scope and safeguards

This assessment continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences programme using the next three pairs in the v0.67.0 regenerated remaining inventory:

- Microbiology ↔ Health Sciences
- Botany ↔ Health Sciences
- Zoology ↔ Health Sciences

The assessment uses exact unordered subject-pair matching. A candidate RD-P requires more than general relevance: it must identify a contribution from each subject, a stable cross-domain research or practice boundary, and an interface that is not merely a restatement of an already-assessed specialist pair. A root-level candidate must also explain why its integrated scope adds value beyond the collection of specialist pairings and the broader Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences relationship, without claiming that every Health Sciences activity depends on the Life Sciences subject.

The three interfaces are supported by different evidence bases and have different limitations:

- **Microbiology ↔ Health Sciences:** microbiology supplies organism, community, resistance and microbial-function evidence used across infectious-disease diagnosis, antimicrobial stewardship, infection prevention, microbiome research and public-health response. CDC explicitly describes microbial ecology as relevant to protection against healthcare-associated and antimicrobial-resistant infections. Clinical guidance also shows that organism identification and susceptibility evidence inform selected antimicrobial decisions. The boundary is a cross-cutting microbial-science interface across Health Sciences, not a claim that every health-science decision is microbiological or that a microbiome intervention is clinically established for every condition.
- **Botany ↔ Health Sciences:** botanical knowledge contributes taxonomic identity, plant-material authentication, source variability and plant biological/chemical characterization to selected areas of health research and practice, including botanical drug development, plant-derived product evaluation, poisoning/toxicovigilance, nutritional resources and integrated plant-health approaches. FDA's current botanical-drug programme demonstrates an explicit regulatory and research interface, while WHO's research agenda emphasizes stronger evidence, safety and appropriate health-system integration for traditional, complementary and integrative medicine. Plant origin or traditional use alone does not establish safety, efficacy, clinical utility or a botanical-science contribution; the candidate is limited to settings where plant biology and botanical identification are substantive inputs.
- **Zoology ↔ Health Sciences:** zoological knowledge contributes species identity, host biology, comparative anatomy and physiology, animal behaviour and wildlife-population context to One Health, zoonotic surveillance and selected biomedical research. CDC and WHO describe integrated approaches linking people, animals, plants and shared environments. NIH recognizes that animal models have contributed to biomedical knowledge, while its current model-selection guidance requires suitability to the question, human relevance and translational context. The boundary is not a claim that animal models reproduce human disease, that animal use is always necessary, or that Zoology is equivalent to Veterinary Medicine.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. It does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, verification, or owner acceptance. No canonical change is made by this report.
