## 1. Scope and safeguards

This report continues the Life Sciences ↔ Health Sciences cross-domain screening programme. It assesses three previously unassessed pairs selected from the reconciled inventory of 148 remaining pairs.

The topic-focused group examines Nutrition interfaces at genetic, molecular, and cellular scales:

- Genetics ↔ Nutrition: inherited genetic variation and differences in nutrient handling, dietary exposures, and responses to nutritional interventions.
- Molecular Biology ↔ Nutrition: molecular responses to nutrients and food-derived bioactive compounds, including gene expression, transcriptional regulation, and epigenetic mechanisms.
- Cell Biology ↔ Nutrition: cellular nutrient and energy sensing, metabolic signaling, cellular growth, autophagy, and related processes.

The three interfaces are related but not interchangeable. Genetics addresses inherited variation; Molecular Biology examines molecular processes and regulation; Cell Biology addresses processes and functions at the cellular level. Nutritional exposure can engage all three scales, but evidence at one scale is not automatically evidence at another.

The canonical taxonomy remains the authority for subject names, identifiers, and hierarchy. The inventory was reconstructed from the v1.13.1 canonical subject lists (18 Life Sciences subjects × 15 Health Sciences subjects = 270 possible pairs) and assessment snapshots v0.1.0 through v0.22.0. The prior ledger contains 122 unique screened pairs. Each selected pair was checked as an unordered pair against the complete ledger, including early name-only rows and later stable-ID rows. None appears in the prior ledger.

A candidate RD-P recommendation is a proposal for owner review, not an accepted or registered canonical edge. A candidate does not establish hierarchy, dependency, enablement, implementation, testing, or verification. No canonical change is made by this report.

## 3. Duplicate, distinction, and scope cross-check

The three selected pairs were checked as unordered subject pairs against all assessment tables in v0.1.0 through v0.22.0. The prior ledger contains 122 unique screened pairs with sequential identifiers LSHS-001 through LSHS-122. Canonical names and stable identifiers were checked against the v1.13.1 taxonomy. None of the three selected pairs is an exact duplicate.

Previously assessed and related pairs remain distinct:

- Physiology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-006) concerns physiological systems and nutrient-related function or response. The present Cell Biology pairing addresses cellular nutrient sensors and intracellular processes; cellular mechanisms may contribute to physiology but do not substitute for organ-system and whole-body function.
- Immunology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-106) concerns immune function and nutritional exposures or status. It is not the same as the present Genetics, Molecular Biology, or Cell Biology interfaces with Nutrition.
- Botany ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-063), Marine Biology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-066), and Ecology ↔ Nutrition (LSHS-067) concern distinct source-organism, food-resource, ecological, or nutrition-related subject contexts. They do not replace gene–diet interaction, nutrigenomics, or cellular nutrient sensing.
- Genetics ↔ Medicine, Pharmacology, Preventive Medicine, Nursing, Dentistry, Epidemiology, and Public Health (LSHS-017, 020, 022, 029, 030, 091, and 092) involve other clinical, pharmacological, preventive, professional, or population-health interfaces. The current Genetics ↔ Nutrition assessment is specifically about inherited variation and dietary exposure or response.
- Molecular Biology ↔ Medicine, Surgery, Preventive Medicine, Pharmacology, Epidemiology, Public Health, Nursing, and Dentistry (LSHS-018, 023, 031, 093, 094, 097, and 098) concern other clinical, pharmacological, population, and professional interfaces. The current pair focuses on nutritional inputs and molecular regulation.
- Cell Biology ↔ Medicine, Surgery, Pharmacology, Nursing, and Dentistry (LSHS-016, 019, 021, 095, and 096) concern other clinical or applied interfaces. The current pair focuses on nutrient sensing and cellular metabolic regulation.

The boundaries between the three new pairs are explicit:

- LSHS-123 (Genetics ↔ Nutrition) asks how inherited variation modifies nutritional traits or response. It does not equate genetics with gene expression or cellular signal transduction.
- LSHS-124 (Molecular Biology ↔ Nutrition) asks how nutritional inputs influence molecular regulation and molecular phenotype. It does not treat every molecular response as inherited genetic variation or proof of clinical benefit.
- LSHS-125 (Cell Biology ↔ Nutrition) asks how nutrient and energy signals are detected and translated into cellular processes. It does not treat the cell as the only relevant level of nutritional science.

Direction is reciprocal within each bounded interface:

- LSHS-123: Genetics supplies inherited-variation hypotheses; Nutrition supplies characterized dietary exposures, nutritional measures, and interventions. Observed dietary responses can guide further genetic research.
- LSHS-124: Nutrition supplies dietary inputs and bioactive compounds; Molecular Biology characterizes gene-expression, signaling, and epigenetic responses. Molecular findings can inform further nutrition research.
- LSHS-125: Nutrition supplies nutrient and energy availability context; Cell Biology characterizes cellular sensing, signaling, and functional response. Cell-level findings can refine mechanistic nutrition questions.

None of these proposals is a hierarchy edge, universal dependency, or enablement claim.
